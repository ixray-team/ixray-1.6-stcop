# Rendering

This branch uses separate client renderer DLLs. Dedicated servers load `xrRender_DS0`; `on_idle` skips `seqRender` for them.

## Renderer selection

One client renderer, `xrRender_IXR` (`src/Layers/xrRenderIXR`), with one frame: the forward+ level frame over the baked light. `r_api d3d11|d3d12` picks the API (NRI on both). There is no renderer command: the deferred frame and the `renderer_*` commands are removed. `CApplication::ConfigureRenderer` reads the feature switches before the library loads; `CreateRendererList` offers the renderer when `xrRender_IXR` is beside the executable (`-perfhud_hack` bypasses the check).

## Video settings

Every player-facing video option is one command with a token value (`off` is the disabling token everywhere; on/off options also accept `1` / `0`). The value the player chose is the stored value and is what `user.ltx` keeps. One resolver derives the effective value from all stored values; the renderer reads only the effective ones. Lowering a tier and raising it back restores the choices that depended on it. `r_settings` logs every setting: stored value, effective value, the reason they differ, the apply class and whether it differs from the preset.

Apply classes: `live` is at once; `recompile` is one shader recompile on the next frame (all shader-affecting values share one key, so several changes compile once); `restart` needs `r_restart` (the console logs `- <cmd> is applied by r_restart`); `next start` needs a game restart. A setting marked inert is registered, stored and saved, and has no effect yet.

Notes on single rows:

- `r_upscaler nearest` exists in `DEBUG_DRAW` builds only. `dlss` and `xess` need D3D12 and device support; on D3D11 only `fsr` (FSR 3) is offered. `r_dlss_preset` is greyed unless `r_upscaler dlss`.
- `r_anti_aliasing msaa` turns MSAA on; `r_msaa` holds only the sample count and is greyed otherwise. An old `r_msaa 2x` is `r_anti_aliasing msaa` with `r_msaa 2x`; an old `r_msaa off` is `r_anti_aliasing off`. `fxaa`, `smaa` and `taa` run as `off` under `dlss`, `fsr` and `xess`.
- `r_shadow_quality`: the sun's shadow is filtered over 3x3 taps up to `high` and over 5x5 at `ultra`; `high` adds the sun's and lamps' detail shadows, `ultra` (ray traced) is not drawn and runs as `high`. Cloud shadows are drawn at `ultra` alone, as the presets of the deferred frame had them (they dim the sun by up to three quarters as the clouds drift, which moved sunlit ground by 127 to 139 of 255 between shots of one save); the HUD's shadows start at `medium`. PCSS is `r_pcss on`.
- `r_shading_quality ultra` is `high` plus order-independent transparency (without MSAA). The `r_wboit` command and the `ALLOW_WBOIT_TRANSPARENCY` addon option are removed.
- `r_volumetric_quality`: sun shafts from `low`, the alternative sun shafts from `medium`, volumetric lamps from `low`.
- `r_occlusion_quality high` (directional) is not drawn and runs as `medium`. `r_reflection_quality ultra` (ray traced) runs as `high`; `high` adds the reflection cube around the eye for the rays the screen misses (VSLR) and needs `r_restart`.
- `r_wet_surfaces` is one switch for wet shading, splashes, streaks, procedural puddles and authored puddles. `r4_rain_puddles` and `r4_puddles` are no longer commands.
- `r_post_processing_quality`: `off` adds no bloom (the frame draws only the first bloom downsample level, which the exposure is measured from), `medium` adds the bloom by `r_bloom_amount` (0.04), `high` is not drawn yet and runs as `medium`. Every value and every shading quality is exposed and tone-mapped the same way, on display values: the exposure is `r_exposure_bias - 0.15 x log2(average luminance)` stops, held between -0.5 and 2, and the measured brightness follows the scene with a time constant of `r_exposure_speed` seconds (1; at once on a camera cut); the curve is linear up to `r_tonemap_compression` (0.7) and has no toe; its shoulder reaches white at `r_tonemap_white` (4, in display units; 1 to 16, a lower value brightens and clips the highlights sooner) and is shared half and half between the whole colour and each channel, and the upper half of the range loses colour as it brightens, by `r_tonemap_desaturation` (0.2) at white. The response 0.15, the range and the channel share are constants of `combine_2.hlsl`. Removed: `r_exposure_key`, `r_exposure_response`, `r_exposure_min`, `r_exposure_max`, `r_tonemap_channel_mix`, `r2_autoexposure_*` (`r2_autoexposure_speed` is `r_exposure_speed`), `r2_tonemap`, `r2_tonemap_*`, `r2_bloom_*` (`r2_bloom_amount` is `r_bloom_amount`), `r2_ls_bloom_*`, `r2_crossfeed`, `r2_vibrance`, `r2_new_autoexposure`, `r2_new_bloom_tonemap`.
- `r_detail_bump` is forced off below high shading. `r_variable_shading` is greyed without hardware tier 2 and forced off under MSAA.
- `r_distance_quality` resets `r_lod_error` to its tier (4 / 2 / 1 / 0.5) on the next settings change. `r_lod_error` itself is a `DEBUG_DRAW`-only developer override.
- `r2_smap_size` stays a developer command and needs a game restart.
- `r_motion_vectors_quality` is forced `high` by `taa` and by every upscaler.
- Apply `level` (`r_shading_quality`, `r_streaming_quality`, `r_parallax`, `r_detail_bump`): they decide the maps and shader permutations of the level's material records, forward bins and texture buckets, which the scene builds once at load and cannot rebuild (the level geometry is freed after the load). `VideoSettings::SetLevelLoaded(true)` at the start of `CRender::level_Load` resolves the stored values, keeps their effective values as the built ones (`VideoState::Built`) and `SyncShadingQuality` compiles the shaders for them before the level's shaders are made; `level_Unload` releases them. In between, every derive runs these rows at the built values before the dependency rules, so the forced settings and the frame-wide shader options follow the built shading quality too; the stored value, the console and the menu show the new choice, one log line `- <names>: applied at the next level load` is printed per new pending set, and the hint says `st_apply_level`. With no level loaded they apply at once. `GpuTextures` also keeps the `texture_lod` its buckets were laid out with (`BuiltLod`) for every later head or tail read.

Dependency rules, later rules override earlier ones:

1. A stored value the renderer does not draw runs as the nearest drawn one below it, else above (reason: unavailable, or hardware for device and API support). A setting with one drawn value is greyed.
2. `r_dlss_preset` is greyed unless `r_upscaler dlss`; `r_msaa` is greyed unless `r_anti_aliasing msaa`.
3. `r_dof off` forces `r_dof_reload`, `r_dof_dialogue` and `r_dof_zoom` off.
4. `r_upscaler dlss`, `fsr` or `xess` runs `r_anti_aliasing fxaa`, `smaa` and `taa` as `off`.
5. `r_anti_aliasing msaa` forces `r_variable_shading off`.
6. `r_occlusion_quality off` forces `r_reflection_quality low` (screen-space reflections trace the depth the occlusion pass splits off).
7. `r_shadow_quality off` forces `r_pcss off`.
8. `r_shading_quality` below `high` forces `r_screen_space_quality low`, `r_reflection_quality low`, `r_gi_quality low` and `r_detail_bump off`.
9. `r_shading_quality low` forces `r_shadow_quality off` (lightmaps and baked lamps only: no cascades, no lamp shadows), `r_pcss off`, `r_volumetric_quality off`, `r_occlusion_quality off`, `r_culling_quality low`, `r_geometry_quality low`, `r_streaming_priority low`, `r_motion_vectors_quality low` and `r_post_processing_quality off`.
10. `r_anti_aliasing taa` or an upscaler forces `r_motion_vectors_quality high`.

Presets: `r_quality_preset minimal|low|medium|high|ultra|extreme` loads `gamedata*/configs/ixray_settings/rspec_<preset>.ltx` and sets every row marked Preset yes. It does not touch anti-aliasing, motion blur, depth of field, the display rows (resolution, fullscreen, vsync, limit, latency, API, gamma), the stylistic extras (sharpening, vignette, aberration, saturation, gas mask, raindrops) or variable shading. Once a covered setting differs from the preset, `r_quality_preset` reads `custom`; the saved config writes `r_quality_preset custom <preset>`, which restores the base preset and the changes on top of it.

Options menu (`gamedata*/scripts/ui_mm_opt_main.script`, `ui_mm_opt_video.script`, `ui_mm_opt_video_adv.script`, `ui_mm_opt_video_extras.script`, `ui_mm_dof_options.script`, identical in the four packs; strings in `configs/text/{eng,rus}/ui_st_video_options.xml`): the Video page holds the video memory bar, the display rows, `r_quality_preset`, the upscaler, scale and DLSS preset, anti-aliasing and MSAA samples, motion blur and the depth of field section; Advanced holds every preset tier in table order with `r_grass_distance` and `r_motion_vectors_quality`; Extras holds the on/off extras, the sharpening and saturation sliders and the leftovers (background render, screenshot format, actor shadow, NPC torches). A row whose `get_setting_reason` is not empty is greyed, a greyed list shows the effective value, and its hint leads with the reason and what runs now; a preset row for which `is_setting_changed` is true gets a `*` marker, and the preset box reads `Custom (<base>)`. Rows follow the pending values live: every list choice and check click on the three video pages pushes each table row's shown value with `set_setting_preview` and re-reads the rows, so the greying, the effective values, the `*` markers and the preset label answer for what the pages show, resolved by the same rules (hardware and availability included); nothing is stored, applied or marked for `r_restart` until Accept. Sliders and commands outside the table are not previewed. `clear_setting_preview` drops the preview after a preset choice and when the menu closes. Choosing a preset executes `r_quality_preset` at once and re-reads Advanced and Extras; every other change is applied on Accept, after which `r_restart` runs once if `is_restart_pending()`. Cancel and Escape undo the pages and put back the preset and the rows as they were when the menu opened.

## Device and resources

`Device.InitRenderDevice(Engine.External.GetAPI())` creates `GRHI` and its device during stage 4. `src/xrRHI` contains D3D9 and D3D11 backends; Linux builds use DXVK Native. `message_loop` calls `GRHI->BeginFrame()` and then `Device.PaceFrame` (the renderer's frame pacer, see the swap chain below) before SDL polling. `Device.ConnectToRender` binds the renderer device interface (`dxRenderDeviceRender` for clients).

Shared code is in `src/Layers/xrRender`, `xrRenderDX9`, and `xrRenderDX10`; renderer CMake files select the sources compiled into each DLL. Resources use `CResourceManager`; materials use `IBlender` and shader permutations. R4 compute shaders use `CSCompiler`. Game code requests visuals through `Render` (`ModelPool`, `SkeletonAnimated`, `WallmarksEngine`, `PSLibrary`).

## Texture loading

With `r__mt_texture_load on` (D3D11; D3D12 and the editors load synchronously), creating a texture returns its logical `CTexture` at once and starts its DDS load as soon as the render device is ready, including during deferred resource creation. Each `CTexture` owns a task group allocated only while a load is pending. Repeated requests find the same `CTexture` in the manager and reuse that task. The task reads and decodes the DDS and makes the texture's NRI texture and view and the old backend's adopting surface and view (`GpuResource::Load`; the device is free-threaded). The copy of the levels runs on the D3D11 context, which only the primary thread may use: a texture made on a worker keeps its levels until the primary thread next looks at it (`GpuResource::Ready`: a bind, its view, `surface_get`, `GetView`, the end of its load). The task writes only that texture and then sets a release flag. Worker tasks never change the bind delegate or the views a draw reads. Video textures and user surfaces use the synchronous path.

Textures owned by NRI (A5, 2026-11-23, `GpuResource.{h,cpp}`): every texture `CTexture` loads from a file, the frames of a sequence, and the renderer's own small textures (font atlases, the noise, the material table, the puddles' water height; `CTexture::Make`) are made, filled, viewed and destroyed through NRI: one NRI texture, one shader view (2D, array, cube, cube array or volume, as the file says), and its state: once filled it is read by shaders for the rest of its life. It is destroyed through `NriDevice::Retire` once the GPU has run the frame that let it go (`Unload`, a texture quality change, the texture's destruction). DDS files are read with DirectXTex in the renderer: the mips the texture quality leaves out are skipped (2D only, as before), a 16-bit format the device cannot sample is widened, and what NRI has no format for (`B8G8R8X8`, `A8`) is read as `R8G8B8A8_UNORM`. NRI makes every texture typeless; its views and the old backend's take the file's format, UNORM or sRGB as the file says. The fill: on D3D12, on the primary thread in a level's frame, the copy is recorded into the frame's commands (`BeginNriCommands`, a staging buffer retired with the frame); elsewhere `NriDevice::UploadData` copies and waits (D3D12 from any thread, D3D11 on the primary thread only, as it copies on the context the primary thread draws with); a D3D11 texture made off the primary thread waits for `Ready`. The whole copies the frame streams (`TextureDecode`) end in the same read state and are held the same way. The old backend draws such a texture through a surface that adopts the native texture (`RHITextureDesc::Adopt` through `CreateTextureFromMemory`: D3D12 takes it in the shader-resource state for good, D3D11's surface counts its own references); `pSurface`, `get_SRView`, `surface_get` and the ImGui image handles keep working, the editors' included. NRI passes and the scene read the texture's own view: `NriPass` (`Collect`, `Dispatch`) and the forward scene's reads take `CTexture::BoundView` (the placeholder's while it loads), and `GfxCmdList::SetTexture` binds a texture by hand with it (rain, snow, puddles, lamp projectors). Nothing wraps these textures per frame, and the old backend's state tracker never moves them. `NriPass::Wrap` still wraps, every frame: render targets and the `CTexture`s of targets, the aliases that `surface_set` another texture's surface (the skies and ambient cubes of the weather blend, the colour maps), video textures (theora, AVI: written through the old surface every frame), `CreateEmpty` textures (the texture atlas writes them through the old surface), the decal array, views bound by hand by surface, and ImGui images. The staging copies of `r__no_ram_textures off` are gone: a texture is made in video memory and its levels are freed after the fill. NOT VERIFIED: written 2026-11-23, not built or run.

`CResourceManager::OnDeviceCreate` loads `ed\ed_not_existing_texture.dds` once, synchronously, as the shared placeholder; it is never unloaded. A texture that starts a background load keeps a reference to it until destruction, so the placeholder lives while anything can show it. A draw of a texture whose load is still running binds the placeholder view instead of waiting; once the task has finished, the next bind adopts the result on the rendering thread and all shaders, passes and visuals that already hold the texture use the loaded content. `GfxCmdList::SetTextures` keeps such slots marked and binds them again until the texture is no longer pending. The placeholder is a 2D texture, so it stands in only for textures a pixel shader declares as `Texture2D`: pixel shader reflection records those registers, and creating a pass enables the placeholder for the textures in them. Textures bound only to cube, volume, array, compute or vertex registers, render targets and `$user$` surfaces never show it; their first draw waits for the load as before. If the file fails to load, `texture_load` reports it as before (missing files already fall back to `ed_not_existing_texture`), and a 2D texture left without a view binds the placeholder.

Accessors that need real data (`get_Width`, `get_Height`, `get_Format`, `isUser`, `surface_get`, `GetView`) still wait for the load. `Unload` and destruction cancel a load that has not started and wait for a running one, so a late result never touches a destroyed texture; level unload therefore discards queued loads of released textures. `CResourceManager::DeferredUpload` only starts loads and returns; level start no longer waits for all textures. Memory statistics skip textures whose load is pending. Device reset/destruction and texture-description reload still wait before changing the resources used by the loader. Compiling a shader never opens a texture file: alpha-tested `deffer_base`, `forward_base` and `ui_base` undo the black fringe of BC1 (1-bit alpha) textures through `CBMaterial::BaseIsBC1`, which `GfxCmdList::SetTextures` sets from the format of the view bound to `s_base` (the placeholder's while the load is pending), instead of a `USE_DXT1_HACK` permutation chosen from the DDS header. Turning multithreaded loading off retains synchronous or lazy loading according to `bDeferredLoad`; `r__deferred_texture_load` (default on) controls bulk upload/unload on R4; the bulk upload leaves out the level textures the scene reads from its buckets, which get a whole copy only when a draw binds them. R1 and R2 ignore it and always unload/upload: Direct3D 9 textures live in `D3DPOOL_DEFAULT`, and `IDirect3DDevice9::Reset` fails while any of them exists. `InternalDevice9::ResizeBuffers` retries a reset only while the device is lost and exits with an error on any other result.

The R4 `render.experemental.target_res` command is registered during renderer console initialization, before any background texture load. Texture loading never registers console commands from a worker.

`r_texture_budget` (megabytes, 256 to 16536, default 512) is how much video memory the R4 scene's own textures may take for their large mips. Every texture of the level keeps its mips up to 512 pixels loaded; the larger ones are loaded on demand into pools that share this budget, and a texture that finds no free place is drawn from its 512 pixel version until one frees up. The budget is read when a level loads and is cut to the video memory the system still grants the process at that moment, never below 256. The log line `[scene] texture heads` shows what the level asks for, what the pools got, and that limit.

## The frame

`CRender::Render` in `src/Layers/xrRenderIXR/ixr_R_render.cpp` is reached from `seqRender`.

| Early out | Result |
| --- | --- |
| `OnRenderPPUI_query` | `render_menu` |
| menu skips scene, or no level and no HUD | select LUT render target and return |
| `m_bFirstFrameAfterReset` | `xrRender_apply_tf`, clear the flag, return |

Its start: the editors' shading settings and Hi-Z fetch (and their unlit view, `EditorRenderUnlit`), the sun cascades' matrices (`prepare_sun_collect`, under `SunShadows`), the sun's shadow constants, the TAA / upscaler jitter (`ps_r_scale_mode > 1` or `ps_r2_aa_type == 3`; the upscale step is `PhaseUpscaleNri` on D3D12 and `phase_fsr` on D3D11, see "Upscalers" below), the item UI into `rt_ui_pda`. Then `RenderForwardLevel`: occlusion map, the sun cascades' static collection on the task pool (`BeginFarSunCascades`), the rain map, `render_main`, lamp shadows, `SurfaceLights`, decals, the sun cascades (`RenderFarSunCascade`), the cube around the eye, the scene's cull, then (with the occlusion on) `ScenePrepass`, `RenderForwardOcclusion`, `ForwardHud`, `ForwardOpaque`, `ForwardGrass`, or the plain opaque pass without it, then `ForwardLods`, `Sky`, `Wallmarks`, `ForwardOpaqueLate` (with `RenderForwardPuddles`), portal fades, `RenderForwardOit`, `ForwardSorted`, `EnvironmentLast`, `CopyForwardLensScene`, `HudSorted`, sprite glows, the UI's post-process, distortion, `EndForwardMsaa`, `ForwardVelocity`, `ForwardVolumetrics`, the scope's depth-of-field mask, `DistortCombine` (`r1_distort`) and `phase_post`.

The thin target `rt_Thin` (R32_UINT, `common\thin_surface.hlsli`) is one word a pixel the depth pass writes: octahedral view normal 8 + 8 bits, hemi 4, roughness 8, material id 2, a bit for surfaces that reflect at all and a bit for grass. GTAO at high shading quality, the HUD's contact shadows and both SSLR passes (`sslr_render`, `sslr_filter`) read the depth and this target and nothing else of the surface. The frame traces reflections at high shading quality with GTAO and `r_reflection_quality` medium or higher (`ForwardReflections()`, `USE_SURFACE_REFLECTIONS`): after the occlusion pass a velocity target is built from the depth and the camera's motion, `phase_sslr` runs, and the forward surface shader takes the result in place of the mirrored sky, weighed by a GGX fit. The word's hemi tells the trace how much sky a missed ray may see. The reflection target is gamma space (the frame of medium shading quality and up converts a hit, `SceneToGamma`).

`PreRenderThread` runs `seqParallelRender`, the `CollectGraphs` delegate, rain item updates, and particle updates. GPU submission and swapchain operations stay on the primary thread.

### Render graphs

A graph (`R_dsgraph_structure`) belongs to one view. `GraphView` describes it: phase, sector, frustum, transform, origin, pass mask, and a copy of what the collection reads (camera, FOV, fog, SSA thresholds). A new pass needs a description, not a new collection path:

| Graph | Phase | Collected | Thread |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `GraphMain` | normal | `render_main` (its portals; with `GpuScene::IsStaticBinned` also the few statics the scene leaves, `GetMainStatics`), particles before forward | primary |
| `GraphReflection[6]` | reflect | static, `Build`; the portals alone (`Begin`, `Traverse`) when the scene draws every static of the level (`GpuScene::IsStaticBinned`) | `PreRenderThread` |
| `GraphRain` | shadow map | `Begin`, `TraverseShadowView` in `RenderRainMap` | primary |
| `GraphSun[3]` | shadow map | `Begin`, `TraverseShadowView` (`BeginFarSunCascades`); of the moving objects only what the frame's table cannot draw (`GpuScene::CollectCasters` in `RenderFarSunCascade`) | task pool, then primary |
| `GraphPass` | any | lamp parts: `Begin`, `Traverse` once a part a frame (`LampCollect`: its hits and the sectors its level is culled by, `LampTiles`), `TraverseShadowView` again only for a part drawn whole on a level whose batch leaves the lamps statics, the spot shadow cache's classification and draws; the editors' passes (`BuildPassGraph`) | primary, one pass at a time |

`Build` is `Begin`, `Traverse`, `CaptureStatic`. A shadow view (sun cascades, rain map, a lamp part drawn whole) calls `TraverseShadowView` after `Begin`: its portals, which give the sectors of the scene's cull (`GpuScene::MakeCullBlock`) and, for the cascades and lamp parts, the sectors and frustums the moving objects are tested against (`IsInPortals`); `CaptureStatic` of the level visuals the depth batch leaves runs only when the batch leaves the view's passes any (`GpuScene::IsShadowBinned`, counted by `SplitLeftovers` at load and after a shader recompile; a level without clusters walks every sector). A lamp part's portals are traversed once a frame, in `LampCollect`, for every part that casts what moves or draws the level's batch (drawn whole, or cached and waiting for its depth); the reached sectors go to `LampTiles[part].Sectors`, which the part's depth batch culls by (`GpuDepthDraw::Sectors` in `MakeCullBlock`) instead of a traversal of its own. `RenderLampShadow` traverses again only for `CaptureStatic` on a level that leaves the lamps statics; a cache entry's build (`BuildSpotShadowCache`, before `LampCollect`) traverses for its depth. The spot shadow cache classifies an entry the same way: with nothing left it walks no portals and keeps no statics. In the game no shadow view calls `renderable_Render` into its own graph (the editors submit their scene into it): the moving objects come from the frame's table (`GpuScene::CollectCasters`). Traversal results stay in the graph's `CPortalTraverser`; `CSector` and `CPortal` hold no per-frame state.

`on_idle` calls `BeginGraphCollect` before starting `PreRenderThread`. It fills `GraphJobs` with the reflection cube's faces, so these views use the camera of the previous frame; `render_reflections` waits for them (`WaitGraphJobs`). `level_Unload` and `reset_begin` wait for `PreRenderThread`: both can run inside `FrameMove` and delete what the collection reads. The main view walks no sector when the scene draws every static of the level (`GpuScene::IsStaticBinned`: clusters and no level visual left to the graph, counted at load). Its portals are traversed on the primary thread in `render_main` (the visited sectors of the scene's cull, the sector frustums `CollectObjects` tests moving objects against, the portal fade, the details' outdoor test); then, inline before the moving objects (zone `add_static` inside `CollectMain`), each visited sector's frustums test only `GpuScene::GetMainStatics` of the sector: the sorted surfaces the scene holds as clusters, whose visibility and distance key go to `mapSorted` / `mapDistort` as before (`DrawSorted` / `DrawDistortion` draw them from the clusters in that order), and the LOD groups holding such a surface or a card the scene cannot draw (`mapLOD`). The list is built at load in the order a walk meets it; its count is logged ("main view statics"). The cull steps of the scene's cull are the `o_optimize_static` tables (`GetStaticCullSteps`), not the walk. On a level the scene does not draw whole the sectors are walked on the task pool (`BeginMainStatic`, zone `add_static`) into `GraphMainStatic` while the primary thread collects its dynamic objects and the HUD, and joined at the end of `render_main` (`join add_static`); the task writes only its own graph and the occlusion test frames of the level's visuals, which no other view tests, and the occlusion map is final before it starts. The occlusion map is fed only by `level.hom` (`CHOM::Render` at the start of the frame); the walk only read it.

The main scene is collected once per frame: priority 0, priority 1, wallmarks and particles together. The editors have no sectors: each view submits the editor scene into its own graph on the primary thread (`EditorRenderSubspace` sets `EditorGraph`, which `GetMainGraph` returns), the main view into `GraphMain`, every sun cascade every frame into its `GraphSun`, the lamps and the rain into `GraphPass`, and the shadow views draw it per draw, as no clusters hold it. `BeginMainCollect` gives `GraphMain` a `GraphView` copy for each collection. All graphs deduplicate through `private_visuals`. The 3D icons have no graph (see below).

The game sees a collection through `IDSGraphManager` (`src/xrEngine/Render.h`). The renderer passes it to `renderable_Render(DM)`, `CCustomHUD::Render_First(DM)`, `Render_Last(DM)` and `OnHUDDraw(hud, DM)`, and objects add their visuals with `DM->add_Dynamic(visual, &xform)`. The parameter is required, so an object always adds to the collection that is calling it. In the game frame that is the scene's collector (`SceneCollector`, `GpuScene::Objects`), not a graph: see the frame's table of moving objects below. `::Render->GetMainGraph()` returns the graph for code outside these calls: the editors, whose views still collect into graphs (`add_Dynamic` → `add_leafs_Dynamic` → `r_dsgraph_insert_dynamic`). `Render_First` (`set_Invisible`) is not called by the renderer.

**3D icons.** `UI3dStatic` sets the device's camera for its icon and calls `::Render->RenderUI(visuals, color)` once per icon while the interface draws: `visuals` is a list of `UIIconVisual` {visual, transform}, the icon's model first, then what the widget's `OnDrawVisual` appends (the game adds the item's attached items, scopes and silencers on their bones, recursively); the weapon customise window passes the weapon and its attachments the same way. `RenderUI` first asks whether the icon's picture is still kept (below); when it is not, it clears `rt_ui_depth`, clears the icon's place in `rt_ui_color` to transparent (`phase_ui_postprocess_clear`, a fullscreen pass under the doubled scissor) and calls `GpuScene::DrawIcon`: every mesh part of every visual in the list (a skeleton's or a hierarchy's children), with its visual's transform, becomes a model record at the top of the scene's table (`IconModels` = 512 a frame, from `FrameModels` = `SCENE_MODELS` - 512; the frame's own records stop below it), skinned parts through one skinning flush per icon, all visuals of the icon in one draw sequence under one scissor and camera, written into the frame slot's model upload buffer and copied into the table by `UploadSorted`. The parts are drawn as the sorted list's segments (`DrawForward` Parts 32 | 128): neighbours of one pass in one instanced draw, the opaque ones in their order, then the blended (strict or second priority) ones back to front, with the view and projection the command list holds, into `rt_ui_color` / `rt_ui_depth`, clipped by the interface's scissor (bit 7); bit 7 also streams `CBUIIcon` at `b10`, where the other forward draws have the grass block. The pass is the material's `UI` element compiled as a cluster pass (`SURFACE_CLUSTER`, its state as written): `B_MODEL` and `B_MODEL_EbB` always in the game, an XML blend's `normal_ui` with `cluster="1"` (the five shipped ones). A part is not drawn when its element has no such pass, when the pass reads the material table (the Surface shaders of low shading quality) and its material has not joined the scene (it joins with the next frame's `JoinModels`, as the world would join it), when its mesh is not in a page of the heap, or past the caps; `* [scene] 3D icons in the frame before` counts them. Each icon is its own draw (more where two passes bind different textures at one register); the interface composites it at its place in the interface's order (`phase_ui_postprocess`): `rt_ui_color` holds premultiplied linear light and coverage over transparent black, tone mapped and blended over whatever the interface's target holds (one, one minus source alpha), the widget's colour multiplied in. The scene behind the icon is no longer copied into `rt_ui_color` first, so the blended parts of an icon and its edges blend with the interface's picture after tone mapping. Without a level whose scene is binned (the main menu) no icon is drawn.

**Kept icon pictures.** Every icon's picture stays in `rt_ui_color` at its place, the interface's scissor doubled, which is where the composite reads it; `CRender::RenderUI` reads the frame's size before the icon's target, twice that size, becomes the current one, and restores the targets with it (restored with the icon target's size, every composite after the first icon drawn in a frame covered twice the screen and showed a magnified piece of another icon); `IconPictures` (`IconPictures.h`, 256 places, no memory beyond the target that already exists) remembers per place the key of the icon drawn there. `GpuScene::IconKey` hashes (crc32) everything the picture is made of: per mesh part its material (`Shader*`), type, visibility and heap place (vertex and index base, primitives, page), per visual of the list its transform, its skeleton's bone visibility and every bone's `mRenderTransform`, the device's view and projection the widget set, the icon light (`UIIconLighting`), `r2_aref_quality` and, once a frame, the table of streamed texture heads. `IsIconKept` compares the key at the place; a match costs no skinning, no scene draw and no clear, only the composite. Anything else draws the icon again: the widget moved or resized (UI scale, resolution), an attachment was added, removed or moved, its model, rotation, scale, offset or bone visibility changed, the model animates (the idle cycle advances while its pose changes), the icon light was tuned, a texture head arrived or left anywhere in the frame. Drawing at a place loses every picture whose place overlaps it (half-open rectangles: neighbours that touch keep theirs), so two overlapping icons draw each other every frame as before. A picture is not kept when a part was left for a reason a later frame may lift: its material joining (`IconJoins`), its mesh not in a page of the heap, the caps; a part without an interface cluster pass or with a material that cannot join does not hold it back. The widget's colour (tint, fade) is not part of the key: the composite applies it. `GpuScene::ForgetIcons` drops every picture when `rt_ui_color` is made (`CRenderTarget`: start, `vid_restart`, device reset), when the scene loads or unloads a level and when the materials are compiled again (`RecompileShaders`: shading quality, MSAA and the other compile options). When all 256 places are taken the least lately used picture gives way. The stats line `* [scene] 3D icons in the frame before` counts icons drawn, kept, pictures lost and the places held of 256; `SceneIconKey` and `SceneDrawIcon` are the profiler zones.

## 3D scope target detector

With `g_3d_scopes on` on R4, a lensed scope enables target frames through `scope_alive_detector = scope_detector` in its scope section (or a weapon upgrade). The detector also works when there is no 2D `scope_texture`. The section `scope_detector` supplies `vis_frame_speed`, `vis_frame_color`, `found_snd`, and `catch_snd`, as in the 2D detector.

`CActor::RenderScopeUI` routes the active first-person weapon to `TBinocularsVision::DrawScopeUI`. Visual-memory selection stays in the main frame update; rendering updates only target projection with the current world camera once per frame. The regular 2D UI query stays separate; switching to alternate aim, grenade mode, zooming out, or removing the scope leaves the shared texture clear of detector frames.

Detector upgrades are applied after HUD/addon initialization. `LoadScopeEffects` resolves weapon and scope defaults, then installed upgrades, so `up_sect_wpn_gauss_detector` remains active after addon reloads and save loading. Upgrade previews do not apply the detector.

`rt_ui_pda` (`$user$ui_pda`) uses RGBA16F to retain coverage alpha. `hud_scope_detector` writes linear premultiplied color with `ONE / INVSRCALPHA` blending, preserving overlapping frames. The scope shaders sample it in normalized screen coordinates, recover straight color and apply `lerp(World, UI.xyz, UI.w)` before scope fade and the reticle. Normalized coordinates keep the overlay aligned when render resolution differs from display resolution. Ordinary, variable-reticle, night-vision and Gauss scope materials bind `s_scope_ui` to the shared texture.

## R4 rain streaks

`CEnvironment::RenderLast` draws `CEffect_Rain` after sorted geometry (`EnvironmentLast` of `RenderForwardLevel`). `dxRainRender` uses the `effects\rain_streaks` shader (`effects_rain_streaks.*`) with the `fx\rain_streak_drop` texture and its own vertex layout: a point on the streak axis, the offset to the sprite edge and `CEffect_Rain::SpriteMotion`, the drop's world-space displacement over the last frame. One drop fills a sprite, so the sprites are narrowed to `rain_width` times `r4_rain_width_scale` (0.1 by default); the vertex shader widens streaks thinner than about a pixel and fades them. The splash model `dm\rain.dm` keeps its own shader.

`fx\rain_streak_drop` is `rain_drop` from Screen Space Shaders (used with the author's permission). It is an atlas; its top-left 7x7 texels hold one lens-shaped drop (r - normal across the streak, g - along it, a - thickness), stretched over the whole sprite and sampled at mip 0 because the streaks are a few pixels wide.

`CRenderTarget::PhaseRainStreaksBegin` blurs `rt_Generic_0` into `RainRefraction` (`$user$rain_refraction`, 1/4 resolution, `rain_refraction_blur`; `rmNormal` sets the viewport for the smaller target) and binds `rt_Generic_0`, `rt_Velocity` (omitted with `DISABLE_MOTION_VECTORS`) and `ReactiveMask` (`$user$reactive`, R8); the static frame binds only `rt_Generic_0`. It first copies the depth buffer into `RainDepth` (`$user$rain_depth`): forward geometry is included, and the static frame has no `rt_Position`. `CreateRainStreakTargets` creates these targets for both frames. The streak shows the blurred scene offset by the lens normal (up to 1.25% of the screen height times `r4_rain_refraction`, 0 disables it; `RainDepth` rejects geometry in front of the drop), 10% of a fake reflection taken far away in the same buffer and sky-tinted scattered light of 15% of the background luminance, so it reads as a transparent distortion over both sky and shaded ground and dense rain does not add up to a white sheet. PBR and legacy lighting share the shader. All targets blend `SRCALPHA / INVSRCALPHA` with the alpha of their own output: the velocity alpha is the share of the drop's own light, so the refracted background keeps the motion already in `rt_Velocity`, and the reactive alpha is the streak coverage. `PhaseRainStreaksEnd` restores the frame targets.

Streak details: the vertex shader varies width and opacity per drop (hashed from its fall direction, stable for the drop's life); the trail moves by the camera velocity over a 1/30 s exposure (at most half the streak length), so streaks smear when the camera moves. The pixel shader fades streaks near geometry (`RainDepth`) and closer than 0.3 m to the camera, and lights them with up to 8 nearest lamps from `Lights.package` (`rain_lights_*`, forward scattering when the lamp is behind the drop; none in the static frame) and with forward-scattered sunlight, which also picks up lightning flashes (`CEffect_Thunderbolt` raises `sun_color`).

`CRenderTarget::PhaseRainLayers` draws distant rain into the same targets: `rain_layers` scrolls streak columns on four cylinders around the camera (9, 18, 36 and 72 m), tilted by the fall direction, shifted by the camera position across the view so they stay in the world, and filtered to their average where a column is thinner than a pixel. A layer hides behind `RainDepth` and in fog; layers closer than about 40 m fade with `CEffect_Rain::SkyVisibility` (the camera's sky hemisphere factor), so they vanish under a roof. Layers leave `rt_Velocity` unchanged and write half their coverage into `ReactiveMask`. `rain_common.hlsli` holds the shared sky tint and sun scattering.

## R4 reactive mask

`ReactiveMask` (`$user$reactive`, R8, render resolution) marks translucent pixels whose content changes every frame and must not be taken from history. `ForwardVelocity` clears it every frame; translucent forward effects blend their coverage into it (rain streaks and distant rain layers). TAA (`taa_render`) lowers the history weight and narrows K-DOP clipping by it, FSR receives it as the reactive mask and DLSS as `pInBiasCurrentColorMask`.


Static lighting uses baked light, the light buffer of the surface shaders for local lights, `L_Glows`, and optionally one sun cascade for dynamic objects (see Render features). A local light never redraws geometry, and no light of the surroundings is rendered per object; XML blends no longer have `l_point` / `l_spot` elements.

## Console and font submission

The regular console uses `CConsole::OnRender` in [XR_IOConsole.cpp](../../src/xrEngine/XR_IOConsole.cpp): draw backgrounds, queue prompt/tips/history through `CGameFont::OutI`, then flush the two console fonts. The ImGui debug console is a separate path in `XR_IOConsole_UI.cpp`.

[dxFontRender.cpp](../../src/Layers/xrRenderIXR/dxFontRender.cpp) is shared by both RHI backends. Previously, `RenderBase` mapped the vertex stream and issued an indexed draw for every queued string. It now batches consecutive strings from one font, with one map/unmap and draw per nonempty batch. Colors, gradients and positions remain per vertex; string order is preserved. Gamepad icons still render after the base text through separate draws.

Batch capacity is `RImplementation.Dynamic.GetCapacity() / 4` (one chunk of the dynamic geometry stream, below). Each glyph uses four records drawn by `DrawQuads` (non-indexed, `SV_VertexID`), so there is no 16-bit index limit. Reserved glyph counts use string byte lengths, which conservatively bound emitted glyphs.

`CGameFont::MasterOut` adds eight extra strings when outlining is enabled. The supplied console font configuration does not enable outlining by default. `CConsole::OutFont` also recursively wraps long log entries and measures growing prefixes; that CPU work remains separate from draw batching. Neither was measured as the cause of the reported FPS drop.

## Dynamic geometry stream

[DynamicGeometry](../../src/Layers/xrRenderIXR/DynamicGeometry.cpp) (`RImplementation.Dynamic`) holds the frame's dynamic geometry as the 32 B records the vertex shaders pull at t57 (`g_RingVertices`, `PullRing` / `PullVertex` in `common/vertex.hlsli`; `draw_vertex_base` and `DRAW_QUADS` in `CBDraw`). `Lock` returns CPU memory (legacy formats are packed on `Unlock`); a draw puts what was written into the frame slot's chunks of upload memory (4 MB, 131072 records each, four frame slots, a chunk added when a frame needs more). An offset is the record's place in the frame; a lock never straddles two chunks unless it is larger than one, and then its draws are not drawn (every game producer batches to `GetCapacity`). The stream has no index buffer: the sky, the clouds and the thunderbolt model write their triangles' vertices one after another, a fan (`DrawFan`) is written out as a list.

Producers on the stream: `dxFontRender` (fonts, console, gamepad icons), ImGui's main viewport (`ImGuiNri`, below), `dxUIRender` (every UI primitive: windows, lists, strips, lines), `dxDebugRender`, `dxStatGraphRender`, the menu quad (`CRender::render_menu`), glows, lens flares, the sky, the clouds, particles (`CParticleEffect::Render`, in the graph's sorted order), the thunderbolt model and gradients, portal fades, rain (instanced: `DrawInstanced`, the drops and splashes from an NRI copy of the instance records), puddles (`ForwardPuddles`), and in the editors the draw utilities (`CDrawUtilities`, `CEditorDrawUtilities`: fans and the indexed helpers are written out as lists), the detail paint brush and trail, skinned edit meshes, wallmarks, the AI map and the 2D projector, the editor's particle path and the object editor's LOD preview (its card record names its facets by their place in the chunk). The LOD cards the scene does not batch (`r_dsgraph_render_lods`; every card in the editors) go through it too.

A draw is recorded by `NriPass::DrawDynamic` from what is set on the command list, as a fullscreen pass is: the pass's shaders and state, its pixel-stage textures (a loading texture reads its placeholder), the targets, viewport, scissor and stencil reference. A view bound by hand (`GfxCmdList::SetResource`) is read only where its caller gave NRI the same resource: the surface of a texture, or a view NRI made of the same records (`NriPass::StreamRecords`; the forward decals, the surface lights, the rain instances, the editor's identity detail instance). The frame's hand-bound views (light tiles, decals, surface lights and projectors, the sky behind, reflections, the rain map and its ripples, snow and puddle textures) are given so. A vertex shader's reads come from its reflection (`SVS::ReadSlots`): beside the ring it reads only views given so. A register a shader reads with nothing bound reads a zero view of the kind its reflection names (`ESlotKind`: flat, array, cube, cube array, volume, structured buffer), as it read zero on the old backend; a multisampled, one-dimensional or typed-buffer read has none. The pipeline is found by a hash of that state plus topology and sample count, so multisampled targets are drawn too. The layout adds the vertex stage's buffers t0..t63 (t57 is the chunk; the skinned vertices and the pages after it are not read for ring records) and a per-instance `DRAWID` stream (an NRI buffer of 0..65535). Descriptor sets are kept for the frame by what they hold (chunk and views), from pools of 512 sets added as needed. Constant blocks are streamed again only where they were written and changed since the last dynamic draw (the NRI constant ring is 16 MB and not fenced). There is no fallback: a dynamic draw or fullscreen pass NRI cannot take is an error of its caller and is not drawn (geometry or tessellation shaders, which no engine shader has; a view bound by hand that NRI has no view of; a blend factor, which no blender names; a typeless or unknown texture or target; mixed sample counts; an empty read of a kind with no zero view; more than 32 reads; a pipeline NRI refuses; a vertex shader of input-assembler vertices or a triangle fan, which the old backend skipped too). The log says `! NRI pass: a draw with <reason>[, <stage> register t<n>] (vertex shader <name>, pixel shader <name>) is not drawn` once a reason. Clears, copies, mips and dispatches NRI cannot do (a typeless colour, a multisampled depth copied into a plain surface, a slice view of an array the renderer does not know, a dispatch that writes a buffer it was given no storage view of) are skipped with `! NRI pass: <what> (<name>), skipped`. Slice views the renderer knows: spot shadow pages, sun cascades, decal layers, the faces of the two reflection cubes.

D3D12 records each dynamic draw in place, in the list the old backend records into, and binds all it uses each time. D3D11 records consecutive dynamic draws into one NRI command buffer (a run) and binds only what changed since the run's last draw; the run is replayed (`FlushNriRun`) before anything else reaches the context: the old backend's draws, clears, copies, mip generation and queries (`GfxCmdList`), every other NRI pass (`BeginNriCommands`), the end of the frame, a target's release and a reset. After a replay under an old draw already set up, the command list binds its shaders, viewport and scissor again. Code that reaches the D3D11 context outside `GfxCmdList` while dynamic draws may be pending has to call `FlushNriRun` first. GPU timestamps taken between dynamic draws on D3D11 measure the run where it is replayed, not where it was recorded.

D3D11 requires `MapNoOverwriteOnDynamicBufferSRV`: the renderer exits at start with a message on drivers without it (the chunks are written while the GPU still reads the frames before).

`rs_stats` shows the frame before: `DYN` dynamic draws through NRI and draws dropped as errors (dynamic and fullscreen); `NRI D3D11` runs replayed and all replays of NRI commands (runs and passes; 0 on D3D12).

## ImGui

In tool builds the gamma pass leaves the frame in the debug scene target and `dxRenderDeviceRender::End` binds the swap chain; `CImGuiManager::Render` builds the ImGui frame and calls `IRenderDeviceRender::DrawImGui`, which draws the main viewport through NRI on the dynamic geometry stream ([ImGuiNri.cpp](../../src/Layers/xrRenderIXR/ImGuiNri.cpp)), on the primary thread inside the `ImGui EndRender` zone (`ImGui: NRI`). Each `ImDrawCmd` is one stream draw: its indexed triangles are written out as records (pixels of `screen_res` with the `-0.5` the `stub_notransform_t` vertex shader adds back, colour swizzled from ImGui's `0xAABBGGRR`, `w` 1), its clip rectangle scaled by `FramebufferScale` is the scissor, its texture is bound by hand at `s_base` of the `hud\default` pass (`dxRenderDeviceRender::m_ImGuiShader`, created on `$null`). Blending is ImGui's (straight alpha into colour, `ONE / INV_SRC_ALPHA` into alpha) into the `R8G8B8A8_UNORM` swap chain, with no sRGB conversion, as the ImGui backends draw it; `ImGuiNri::BlendCallback` added to a draw list switches it off for the images after it (the render debug window's target previews) and on again. Other draw callbacks are run as they are. A draw command whose texture has no flat 2D view is not drawn.

`ImTextureID` is an engine image: the address of an `IRHISurface` with its low bit set (`RHIUtils::ImGui::Image` / `ImageSurface`), which tells it from the native views the editors still pass. Producers hand out the surface of what they show: `ImGuiNri::Image` for a texture (it waits for the load, as `get_SRView` did), the target's surface for render targets and `GRHI->DevicePtr->RenderRTV->GetSurface()` for the game's picture. ImGui's own textures (the font atlas, created and grown by ImGui; `ImGuiBackendFlags_RendererHasTextures`) are engine images too: `RHIUtils::ImGui::UpdateTextures` makes, updates and destroys them as RGBA8 surfaces before the main viewport is drawn (after a `FlushNriRun`, as D3D11 uploads on its context), and destroys them before the backends shut down.

The platform windows (panels taken out of the main window, the Game window taken out) are still drawn by `imgui_impl_dx11` / `imgui_impl_dx12` through [RHIImGuiLayout.cpp](../../src/xrRHI/Layout/ImGui/RHIImGuiLayout.cpp), as the editors are. They resolve an engine image to a view of the old backend kept by `RHIUtils::ImGui::ImageView` (one per surface, holding a reference to it, released four frames after its last use; depth formats read through their typed format; none for arrays, cubes and multisampled surfaces, whose commands are not drawn), and do not touch ImGui's textures in the game: the main viewport has made them. The main viewport binds the same view with the surface by hand (`SetResource`), and unbinds it after its last draw.

## D3D12 dynamic streams

In [D3D12/Resources.cpp](../../src/xrRHI/D3D12/Resources.cpp), dynamic vertex/index buffers map their upload resource directly. `WRITE_NO_OVERWRITE` reuses owned upload storage; discard acquires another stream through the fence-aware pool in `D3D12/Device.cpp`. Their write unmap returns without uploading the whole shadow buffer. Do not infer a full-buffer copy or GPU flush for every console line from the generic map API.

`InternalDevice12::PrepareDraw` in `D3D12/Binding.cpp` still prepares descriptor/root bindings, pipeline state and vertex/index views for each draw. Batching reduces how often this path is called; it does not change its synchronization or resource lifetime.

## D3D12 per-draw path

`PrepareDraw` drains queued uploads once, then resolves bindings with `_preparing` set so no upload copy lands between binding validation and the draw. Draw calls record on `_commands` directly after it.

The graphics PSO key is rebuilt only when `_pipelineDirty` is set (shaders, layout, topology, attachments, depth-bounds enable, or `DX12StateManager` state). A barrier elsewhere only reruns `TransitionAttachments`; a compute dispatch or command-list reset rebinds the cached `_graphicsPipeline`. Pipeline buckets store the key hash. `DX12StateManager::Apply` converts RHI descriptors only after a setter ran and compares with the device only when `_stateSerial` moved; `SetRawBlendState` bumps that serial.

UAV writes are hazard-tracked per resource with `UAVPendingEpoch` (command-list epoch). `Dispatch` and `FinishDraw` mark written UAVs instead of issuing a UAV barrier each time. `UAVTable` emits one batched UAV barrier when a pending resource is bound as a UAV again in the same list; a full transition out of `UNORDERED_ACCESS` clears the mark because the transition already orders the writes. `PrepareUpscale` emits the barrier before external upscalers write a pending output. Command-list boundaries are fully synchronized, so marks from older epochs are ignored.

Default-heap buffer addresses are cached for the vertex/index path, and depth bounds are reapplied only when they change.

Validation: compare D3D12 frame time and the `D3D12 ... barrier ... pso` counter line before and after in the same loaded scene; check SSR/GTAO/compute chains, upscalers, occlusion-culled lights and depth-bounds lights in RenderDoc or with `-dxdebug`. Not compiled or measured yet.

The 2026-10-03 font batching change passed a Debug translation-unit compile with no warnings or errors. Runtime FPS and rendered output remain unverified. Check console-open versus console-closed frame times in the same loaded scene, and inspect text order, colors, selection, scrolling and long lines. A RenderDoc capture should show font draws split at batch capacity rather than at every string. Compilation alone does not establish the performance fix.

## Constant buffers

Engine-owned `b0`–`b5` layouts and the matrix encode/decode live in `shaders/common/fixed_cb.hlsli`. C++ includes that file; dynamic, static and editor declarations expand the same field lists. The R1-on-D3D11 declarations (`r1_decl.hlsli`) keep the shorter `cb_frame` prefix without `hud_rain` and `m_digiclock`. CPU `static_assert`s lock sizes and offsets. `encode_float4x4` / `encode_float3x4` are the shared transpose; a 3x4 decode restores an affine matrix (`0,0,0,1` on the dropped row).

The HUD is drawn with a view whose eye sits at the origin, so `eye_position` is zero for it. `eye_world` of the view block is the camera in the world for every view: anything that takes a HUD point into the world (the sun shadow lookup of `sun_shadow.hlsli`) adds `eye_world - eye_position`. Without it the HUD took the sun shadow of the world origin and was almost never sun-lit in the advanced frame.

`FixedConstants` owns the CPU shadow and the six dynamic buffers. Writes compare the shadow and skip the upload when the bytes match. A failed map leaves the buffer dirty. D3D12 upload alignment stays in the RHI and does not change these offsets.

Texture and sampler slots come from the pass. `dx10texture` / `r_dx10Texture` assign `t0` upward in call order, or the slot passed as the third Lua argument. `dx10sampler` / `r_dx10Sampler` assign `s0` upward. The shader compiler rewrites those declarations to `register(tN)` / `register(sN)` before compilation. Only pixel and compute passes bind textures, at most 16 per pass. A pass that requests more must drop unused ones (`r_dx10Unbind`), as the terrain blender does. Shader load does not call D3D shader reflection. `m_affects` is written once per frame. Bones, bloom, tonemap, wind and trample constants are written by name into their explicit buffers. Other loose `$Globals` have no reflected offset anymore.

D3D12 reads resource binding metadata from DXBC/DXIL when creating or replacing an RHI shader. This metadata supplies CBV/sampler/UAV masks and SRV/UAV dimensions, return types and buffer kinds; it does not restore renderer constant-offset reflection. Missing or feedback-bound SRVs use null descriptors matching the shader declaration, including `TextureCube`. Replacement refreshes this metadata and its null-descriptor cache together, and preserves the old shader if reflection fails. The 2026-10-03 change addresses GPU validation error 940 at level startup and awaits Debug/RelWithDebInfo runtime verification.

Slots `b6`–`b10` are explicit pass buffers, reused only by shaders that do not declare the same slot together:

| Slot | Buffers |
| --- | --- |
| b6 | trample, volumetric lights, fluid sim, fluid render, bloom, tonemap (CBSkin is an empty placeholder) |
| b7 | detail wind, fluid AABB |
| b8 | fluid emitter |
| b9 | fluid OOBB clip planes |
| b10 | dynamic OOBB, static box bounds |

Grass values are written by name into their explicit buffers. `SkinningPass::Flush` poses the skeletons of what it skins on the task pool's workers before it reads their bones. A skeleton with an update callback (`GetUpdateCallback`: the game's foot IK, attachments and scripted actions, all run by `VisualCallback` after every bone is calculated) is posed in three steps (`PoseSkeletons`): its tracks advance on the calling thread (`PrepareBones`: the animation callbacks are the game's), its bones are calculated on a worker with the rest without the callback (`PoseBones`), and the callbacks run last on the calling thread, one skeleton after another in the order of the list (`FinishBones`, profiler zone `CKinematics::UpdateCallback`). The callback runs inside the skeleton's lock and ray-queries the characters around it, which takes their locks: two workers running two of them for characters that stand together waited for each other for good, so no callback runs on a worker; one that asks a neighbour for its bones gets its calculated pose, without the neighbour's callback when that comes later in the list. Between the two steps another thread that reads the bones sees the pose without the callback's changes (the foot IK). The same function poses the skeletons the main view's collection asks for (`GpuScene::CollectObjects`, once its loop is done) and those only lamp parts cast, within 100 m (`GpuScene::CollectCasters`, before it calls them); the objects only the sun cascades cast, and the rest, are posed by the skinning pass after they were called. A skeleton only the shadow views cast keeps its last pose when it is far (`CKinematics::HoldPose`, see the frame's table below): the skinning pass skins that pose again (`SkinningPass::Snapshot` stores it without calculating it). A skeleton already posed or held this frame (its bones' time stamp is this frame's) is dropped from the list first, so a list of posed skeletons dispatches nothing; each skeleton's callback still runs once a frame, at its first pose. The zones `CKinematics::CalculateBones`, `CKinematics::PoseBones` and `StoreVisualMatrix` count only calls that do the work, not those that find it done this frame. On `perf_test` the pass's scope went from 0.33 to 0.27 ms on the primary thread; about half of the bone time there is characters with IK. Skinned meshes no longer upload bones per draw: `SkinningPass` skins every requested skeleton child once per frame in `skin.cs.hlsl` into `g_SkinnedVertices` (VS t58), with the previous position as a half delta for motion vectors. The dispatch goes through `NriPass::Dispatch`, which writes structured buffers as well as flat textures: the bones and jobs are streamed records (`StreamRecords`), the geometry pages are read through views of the old backend's buffers made each frame (`NriPass::WrapRead`, a `ReadExternalBuffer` per page and flush), and the output is an NRI buffer the pass owns (made, grown and retired through `NriDevice::Retire`), handed over read by shaders and taken back so. The scene's sets hold its view directly (`WrapSkin` writes it again when the output grows; the draw layout's buffer range allows that while bound). The old backend's draws (graph leftovers through `CSkeletonX`, the editors) read the same buffer through a wrapper of its native object (`RHIBufferDesc::Adopt`). A dispatch lets go the compute textures of its pass and leaves the graphics shaders set on the command list bound, on D3D11 too, so an old draw that skins its mesh goes on; the old command list has no dispatch or compute binding of its own. Fluid and editor-brush globals have no upload path yet. UI icon lighting, visor/gas-mask, `m_tc_xform` and editor constants live in `shared/extra_cb.hlsli` (`b11`-`b13`); those buffers stay bound, so a shader reading one that was never written sees zeros. No frame-time measurement is recorded here.

## Scope lens chromatic aberration

R4 lens materials share `scope_lens.hlsli`. `SampleScopeScene` reads the scene snapshot and separates its red and blue samples in opposite diagonal directions using the Gunslinger edge-distance formula. The existing `m_hud_params.z` channel carries `scope_abberation`; both legacy constant binding and fixed constant buffers export the same value. No new GPU constant is introduced. Permanent scopes read the weapon's setting; detachable scopes use their own setting with the weapon as fallback. Installed upgrades are reapplied on scope reload.

Sampling uses the source texture dimensions and clamps coordinates to its texel centers. At zero strength, the helper retains the original scene load and skips the extra texture samples. Scene sampling precedes material night vision, the reticle and detector UI. The helper is included by all four lens shaders in every game package.

## Scope postprocess effects

`scope_nightvision = scope_contrast` and `scope_nightvision = scope_nightvision_gauss` load the configured `.ppe` animation for the weapon. Installed scope effects are reapplied after addon initialization. For R4 3D scopes, the animator supplies an independent postprocess state; the lens material chooses whether R4 processes the world snapshot in `$user$accum` before rendering the lens, detector frames and reticle. Separate color-map aliases and noise timing leave the actor camera postprocess independent. The lens PPE stays active throughout normal zoom-in and zoom-out animations, until the zoom rotation factor reaches zero; switching implementations only after zoom completion would produce a bright material-NVG flash during the transition. Detector UI still requires fully completed zoom. Alternate aim and grenade mode disable the lens effect. `SPPInfo::lerp` adds to color-map influence and interpolation, gray, blur and duality, so `Device.ScopePostprocess` is reset to `pp_zero` before every `Process`, as `CCameraManager::UpdatePPEffectors` does for camera effectors; otherwise the color-map influence grows every frame and the lens turns white.

The `models_zoomnight` lens receives the same configured PPE profile through the scope snapshot. Its `ScopePostprocessActive` constant is bound from the current renderer state when the lens pass is selected. When PPE is active, `model_scope_lense_night` skips its additional material night-vision amplification; applying both effects compounds the configured profile with the material's brightness and green gain before scene exposure. The material effect remains available when no PPE pass is active, and zero material brightness leaves the snapshot unchanged.

The Lua material option `:scope_postprocess(false)` requests a raw world snapshot for the HUD scope; the default is `true`. R4 checks this flag on the queued HUD materials before preparing the snapshot. No weapon or profile names are checked in the game component or renderer. For the Gauss material, use `false` to let `model_scope_gauss` provide its own grayscale gain, blue tint, brightness jitter and electronics-interference noise. It uses the existing `s_tonemap` texture and tonemap functions to restore HDR color before composing the lens; no additional shader constant or binding is needed. The existing brightness steps remain active, and the 2D scope continues to use `nightvision_gauss.ppe`. Setting `true` selects the snapshot PPE when available; the Gauss shader uses the existing `ScopePostprocessActive` binding to skip its own amplification and tint, while retaining electronics-interference noise.

D3D11 postprocess shaders sample color lookup tables and noise at mip level zero. Lookup-table sampling must not depend on screen-space brightness derivatives: selecting different mip levels across image edges produces bands. Noise coordinates use floating-point image-to-texture ratios to preserve grain size across resolutions.

At zero brightness, the 2D weapon postprocess stays disabled. Recreating an expired zero-factor animator at full strength causes alternating bright/dark frames; enabled animators receive their factor immediately on creation. Brightness upgrades initialize their new control range and retain installed parameters across addon reloads. Saved brightness is consumed only after a nonzero control range is available.

The 3D postprocess shaders convert the HDR world snapshot to display colors using the final pass's exposure before evaluating the `.ppe` color table. They then invert tone mapping and exposure before returning the snapshot to the lens shader. This prevents the scene combine from applying exposure twice to display colors and washing out the scope. `$user$tonemap` holds the scene's adapted average log2 luminance, not a scale: `DisplayExposure` in `common_functions.hlsli` turns it and `r_exposure_bias` into `combine_2`'s multiplier for display values, and `SceneExposure` adds the exposure grade and raises it to 2.2 where the scene holds linear light (above low shading quality). `model_scope_gauss` uses the same for its own night vision and noise, and the reflex lens fades by the linear-light exposure.

## Rain wetness

Rain is a part of a surface's material, applied in the surface shader before anything lights it. `common/wetness.hlsli` holds it: `rain_exposure` (how much of the rain reaches a point), `rain_ripples` (splash rings on what faces up, streaks down what stands) and `wet_surface` (darker unless a metal, roughness down to 0.2, the sheen of water).

The rain's map, what the rain reaches seen from above, is the fourth slice of the sun cascade array `s_smap_sun`. `CRender::RenderRainMap` draws it at the start of the frame, into a corner of the slice `r3_dynamic_wet_surfaces_sm_res` wide (512 by default), and publishes the lookup as frame constants: `rain_map` (world to map), `rain_setup` (fade distances, the corner's share, on/off), `rain_level` (the level's rectangle, for puddles), and the streak scroll in `rain_params.z`. It leaves the target size and view as it found them: the main passes take their viewport from the size of the target last set. `BindRainTextures` binds the slice and the two ripple textures for the frame's surface shaders.

| Frame | Where wetness is applied | What gets wet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Forward | `common/surface_forward.hlsli`, before the sky is mirrored; the depth pre-pass runs the same code, so reflections see the wet roughness | Bump-mapped level geometry and models at high shading quality, with 8 lookup taps instead of 16 |

A surface with the old gloss maps gets wet as the deferred frame of the old light model wet it (`rain_render` of that frame, `USE_LEGACY_LIGHT`): its colour is multiplied by `max(1 - sqrt(wet * brightness), 0.5)`, where the brightness is the mean of its albedo, and its gloss rises by 0.8 of `wet * brightness`, as that frame raised it (the gloss is kept squared: the rise is made on its root); a PBR surface keeps the 0.66 darkening and the roughness of 0.2. The scene resolve wets every surface the rain reaches, with a normal map or not; the forward shaders still wet only bump-mapped ones, and the grass: at high shading quality a blade darkens by the same formula, by how much of the sky it has and the distance fade alone, without the rain map's taps (a pixel of grass is written many times over). Measured against that frame on `test_scene` at half rain density: the road's albedo 0.239 there, 0.376 before this and 0.24 by the formula.

The exposure is a 16-tap disc of two metres on the map, turned per pixel and per frame, times the sky a point has; outside the map the sky alone decides. It fades between `r3_dynamic_wet_surfaces_near` and `_far` (the wet shading is on with `r_wet_surfaces`), on faces that look down, and with the rain's density.

## Snow

With `UseDynamicSnowMask` on in `engine_external.ltx` (off by default) and a level that allows it (`[weather] snowmask`, on unless set), level geometry that faces up under the open sky takes the material of snow: `shaders\snowmask\snow` and its bump maps, or `[custom_textures] snow_texture`, repeated every five metres. Models and foliage keep their own material.

The surfaces that are surely level geometry to a pixel shader, lightmapped ones and terrain, take the snow's colour before lighting and, with PBR maps, its roughness; they keep their own normal map, and the ground's detail texture is hidden under the snow (`SURFACE_SNOW` in `common/surface_forward.hlsli`, textures at t4 and t5 in the scene's batched draws, at t21 and t22 in a draw of its own, whose t4 and t5 hold the detail texture's bump maps; bound by `BindRainTextures`, `CRenderTarget::SnowInMaterial` says the shaders do it). Vertex-lit level geometry gets none: a forward pixel shader cannot tell it from a model. The deferred snow pass (`snowing_main`) is removed with the deferred frame.

## Rain puddles

`r_wet_surfaces` is one switch for rain puddles, authored puddles and ordinary wet-surface shading, splashes and streaks. Procedural resources are created when the renderer starts with it on, so changing it requires `r_restart`.

Procedural puddles are a part of the forward shader of a PBR terrain (`SURFACE_PUDDLES` in `common/surface_forward.hlsli`, the layers' weighed heights from their bump maps), at high shading quality, with the puddle textures in slots t31-t33 (`RAIN_PUDDLE_FORWARD`): water fills the terrain from its low places up to 0.6 of its height range at full wetness, as far as the rain reaches. A puddle mirrors as water does: 2% head-on, all of it at grazing angles. `o.RainPuddles` is on without the rest of the wetness feature.

The forward frame lays its puddles procedurally on the terrain when `r_wet_surfaces` is on (`CRender::UpdateRainPuddleMode`; a level's `rain_mask.dds` is not read; the authored-mask compute pass of the deferred frame, `CBlender_rain`, `rain_render`, and the legacy overlay `PhaseRainPuddles` / `rain_puddles` are removed).

The PBR procedural slope uses `1 - smoothstep(0.05, 0.2, max(abs(WorldNormal.x), abs(WorldNormal.z)))` on the terrain mesh's own normal. The supplied-mask PBR path retains its one-texel geometric normal reconstruction, and legacy procedural shading retains its original slope calculation.

Procedural PBR water normals follow that geometric normal, with wave/ripple perturbations applied in its tangent frame. A fixed world-up reflection normal on sloped receivers can direct grazing SSR rays back into the ground, producing a second contour inside the puddle. This adjustment changes only the procedural material normal and adds no texture samples or reflection passes; the native SSLR/VSLR code, supplied-mask shading and legacy water normal remain unchanged.

`RainPuddleWaterMap` builds a 2048-square R16G16_UNORM height/coverage map from passable liquid collision triangles for procedural modes. Hardware filtering samples both channels together, and dividing out coverage keeps empty cells from corrupting height. Puddle visibility fades between 2 and 25 cm above the water surface and across footprint edges after contour smoothing. The GPU map occupies 16 MiB, is rebuilt on level load/reset without per-frame collision queries, and is released when a supplied PBR mask is selected. Levels without liquid collision geometry use a neutral one-pixel map.

### Authored puddles

`r_wet_surfaces on` draws the boxes of `$level$\level.puddles`, an ini file with one section a puddle, as the Level Editor's build writes it from its puddle objects: `position = x, y, z` (the box's centre, metres), `size_xz = x, z` (half its extent along its own X and Z), `max_height = h` (half its height: how far above `position` the water stands at full wetness) and `rotation` (radians, optional: one value turns the box about Y, three are the editor object's rotation, `setXYZi`; earlier builds read the value with a broken format and left the angle undefined). Each one is a horizontal quad at `wetness_factor * max_height` inside its box: the water rises as rain keeps the level wet (`wetness_factor` climbs with `rain_density`, about 40 s of full rain to the top; nothing sets it from the console), and the ground above that height hides it. The forward level frame draws every box in view, up to 64, as one draw of the vertex ring at the end of `ForwardOpaqueLate` (`CRender::RenderForwardPuddles`, `ForwardPuddles.cpp`, marker `ForwardPuddles`): the blend's simplified option, `r1_water_puddles` of `surface/water_baked.hlsl` (`WATER_PUDDLE`), lit as open-sky vertex-lit water (hemi and baked sun one, the cascades decide the sun), with its ripples over the world, the TAA jitter, the scene's linear light, a shore that fades in over the first two centimetres of water (from `$user$position`), the sun's glitter, the trace of the baked water against the frame before (`water_mirror`, whatever `r_water_reflections` says) and, where the trace misses, the cube around the eye marched as the baked water marches it (`s_env`, `s_env_dist`, when the frame keeps it: `HasReflectionCube`), fading to the sky cubes where it meets nothing. Puddles take no foam, caustics or order-independent weighing. A level without the file loads nothing and draws nothing. The planar-reflection code of 2026-10-06 (`phase_planar`, `LoadPlanars`, `level.planars`, `$user$planar_color` / `$user$planar_depth`) never ran and was deleted.

## Visor glass and rain drops

Full equipment configuration, condition mapping, accumulation/reset rules, wiping animation setup, assets and troubleshooting: [English guide](../docs/main/en/gameplay/equipment/visor-glass.md) / [Russian guide](../docs/main/ru/gameplay/equipment/visor-glass.md).

`r_raindrops` enables visor droplets. `r2_rain_drops_mode texture` selects the existing normal-map effect; `procedural` selects animated procedural drop layers. `r_raindrops` is on the Extras page of the video options; the mode is a console command. An equipped helmet or outfit must have `glass_present = true`. A helmet with glass takes precedence over an outfit with glass. Glass is active only for the living actor in first-person view. `hud_gas_mask_avaliable` and `hud_rain_drops_avaliable` retain their existing spelling and independently control the glass and droplets. The base scientific outfit and protective helmet declare glass in the corresponding game packages.

`hud_gas_mask_type = flat` is the default and selects ordinary flat glass. Flat glass draws the selected droplet implementation in its own pass; curved glass and disabled glass postprocess use a separate droplet pass. `VisorGlassType` carries the selected `Flat` or `Curved` type from equipment to the renderer.

`IGame_Persistent::VisorRain` owns the accumulation state for both drop modes. The primary render thread checks for overhead cover once a second with a 50-metre upward ray, then changes coverage by 0.05 per second, capped by rain density. `CActor::ClearMaskCB`, called by the `Left` motion mark of `anm_gasmask` or the configured clear-mask animator, clears coverage. The dedicated item animator also invokes an unconsumed callback at natural completion; the HUD-item animation requires its motion mark. Drops accumulate again in rain and dry gradually under cover while rain continues. Rain stopping clears them immediately. Disabling droplets, removing or replacing the glass item, changing the view entity and disconnecting reset the state. This state is independent of weapon droplets and world wetness.

The D3D11 shaders calculate procedural normals with finite differences. R2 computes height once and uses screen derivatives, scaled to the same normal strength, to fit within the minimum Shader Model 3 instruction limit. The shared algorithm is in `shaders/{r2,d3d11}/shared/VisorDrops.hlsli`; flat-glass damage textures are in `textures/shaders/gasmasks/`. The curved-glass shader and textures remain available.

## GPU crash dump

When the graphics device is lost (driver reset, hang, GPU page fault), the engine writes `gpu_crash_<unix time>.txt` to the logs folder and names it in the log before it stops. The file starts with the removal reason and the adapter.

Start with `-gpu_dump` to make the file useful on D3D12: the device then records the last operations of every command list, the named render passes they belong to, and recently freed resources. The dump lists which operation each unfinished command list stopped at and, for a page fault, the address and the resources around it. `-dxdebug` records the same and adds the debug layer, which is much slower.

D3D11 keeps no record of what the GPU was executing, so the engine tracks the named render passes itself. With `-gpu_dump` it waits for the GPU at the end of every pass; when the device is lost the file names the pass the GPU was running (`Lost while the GPU ran: ...`). This makes the frame much slower. Without the flag the file lists only the passes submitted last, newest first: the GPU runs behind the engine, so the faulty pass is somewhere among them, not necessarily the first.

Without the flag on D3D12 the file holds the reason only.

`-gpu_dump` costs GPU and CPU time, a lot of it on D3D11; use it for reproducing a crash, not for measuring.

## Decals

Bullet holes, blood, scorches and other runtime marks, on level geometry and on models, are decals: a box the mark's texture is projected through, placed at the hit point along the wall's normal. A frame takes up to 256 marks in view, nearest first, and writes which of them reach each 16 px screen tile into `s_light_tiles` beside the lights (`LightLists.cpp`, `Decals.cpp`, `lighting/light_tiles.hlsl`). `common/decals.hlsli` loops a pixel's decals and multiplies the base colour before lighting: 0.5 in the texture leaves it alone, the mark fades to that as it ages. The surface shaders of level geometry apply it.

The textures live in one array (256 x 256, 64 layers); a wallmark shader gets a layer the first time one of its marks is in view, a 65th shader draws nothing. A box is a third of its size deep, so a mark wraps a corner a little and can land on an object standing inside it; faces steeper than about 70 degrees to the wall take none. Every mark is blended as 2x multiply, whatever its shader says. A mark on a skinned model is the same kind of box fixed to the bone that was hit: it follows that bone rigidly, so near a joint it can slide a little over skin that bends. A box does not know what it was made on: a mark on the ground also lies on a model standing in it, and a mark on a body on a wall the body leans against. The HUD takes none. The level's baked decals are still drawn as geometry (`WallmarksEngine.cpp`).

## Tone mapping on the simplified frame

From medium shading quality up the simplified frame writes linear light to the scene target, as the advanced frame does (`USE_LINEAR_SCENE`): what blends over the scene (particles, water, glass, marks, the sun) then adds and covers as light does. Low shading quality (`USE_SURFACE_CLASSIC`) keeps the classic gamma target. A surface shader works its colour out in linear light and writes it as it is (`default_shade`, `surface_plain`); one that passes a texture through decodes it with `surface_scene` (glows, light planes, the multiplying wallmark, the water's fog). Shaders shared with the advanced frame decode with `SceneFromGamma` / `SceneToGamma` (`common/common_functions.hlsli`): `GammaToLinear` does nothing under the baked light model, so it cannot be used for what goes to the scene. The sky, the clouds, the sun disc, lightning and tracers decode the same way; the blends of the last three pick `stub_default` only for low shading quality (blend option `USE_SURFACE_CLASSIC`). Lens flares are drawn once, after the tone curve, as in the advanced frame. What is far fades into the sky behind it, clouds included, by the fog on it squared, as the advanced frame blends its lit picture over the sky (`ApplyFog` of `common/ambient.hlsli`): the occlusion step draws the sky and the clouds with nothing in front of them into `$user$sky_behind` (marker `SkyBehind`, 0.03 ms), and `default_shade` reads the pixel's own place in it (`s_sky_behind`, pixel slot 58, bound for the whole frame and listed in the batched draws' ranges). Fogged to the fog's colour alone, a far hill stood as a flat shape against the clouds. What is blended over the scene leaves the picture by the same amount instead of reading the sky: a particle that covers loses its share, one that is added loses its colour (`surface/billboard.hlsl`), the baked water thins by its own fog squared, and blended level surfaces go through `default_shade`. Light planes and glows are not fogged at all. A bumped surface with old (gloss) maps mirrors its surroundings as the advanced frame makes it of those maps: its roughness is 0.15 to 0.4 by the base texture's material value (`surface_kind`, what `L_material.w` is in that frame), widened where the normal map is busy, and its reflectance is 0.16 of the gloss channel to the fourth power. At high shading quality with `r_parallax on` the terrain's four detail layers are each read where the eye's ray meets the layer's own height map and weighed by the mask (`SURFACE_TERRAIN_PARALLAX` in `common/surface_forward.hlsli`, under the `USE_TERRAIN_PARALLAX` shader option, as `surface/surface_level.cs.hlsl` does in the advanced frame); the mask, the base colour and the lightmap stay where the surface is. Measured on a view filled with terrain: the terrain colour draw went from 0.760 to 0.796 ms. The distortion mask is applied by `postfx/r1_distort.hlsl`, which reads the mask unfiltered and leaves a pixel where it is when the push would pull the HUD into the scene behind it, as `lighting/combine_distort.hlsl` does. It runs the same bloom chain as the advanced frame; the bloom is added to the colour as it is, with nothing taken away for it, because this frame's light scales already put its colours where the advanced frame's stand after it makes room for its bloom. Its final pass (`lighting/combine_2.hlsl`) exposes the colour by the scene's measured brightness and applies the same tone curve as the advanced frame, with the same console settings (`r_exposure_*`, `r_tonemap_*`). A particle that covers or is set takes the light of where it is (the sky's light and half of the sun's), which the advanced frame does not give it: unlit, smoke glows grey at night. One that is added keeps its own colour. Otherwise a particle is drawn as the advanced frame draws it (`surface/billboard.hlsl`): fogged by its distance from the eye (an added one thinned in colour and in share, a covering one taken to the fog's colour), its share decoded like its colour (that frame's particle shaders are compiled without `USE_PBR`; measured in a capture, a faint gas card had a share of 0.01 there against 0.04-0.1 before this), and with `r_soft_particles` faded where it nears what stands behind it, by the depth the occlusion step copies (`USE_SURFACE_SOFT_PARTICLES`, `s_position`). Without bloom (`r_post_processing_quality off`, always at low shading quality) the frame runs the first level of the bloom downsample alone, for the luminance adaptation passes that measure the brightness (`phase_post`). The eye's ray is marched through a height map only within 12 m (fading from 8 m); past that the plain offset fades out by 16 m and beyond it the height is not read (`stop_offset` of `surface_shift_uv`): the tap was 13-15 % of what a far terrain pixel cost (`keep_terrain_far` capture, 2026-11-19), for a shift nobody sees. A terrain pixel takes one march, on the layer its mask gives it the most of, and every layer is read at that shift; where the next layer comes within an eighth of it the two are marched and mixed, half and half where they are even, so the shift never jumps. A march a layer gave more relief where the mask mixes layers, which on `garbage` is most of the field (weights of 0.2 to 0.5 a layer), and cost a fifth more: the user took the flatter ground (2026-11-19; `grass_overdraw` with the grass thinned, D3D11, colour pass 0.79 to 0.65 ms). The advanced preset's `surface_level.cs.hlsl` still marches every layer. Texture anisotropy and the march's step count cost nothing measurable.

From medium shading quality up the simplified frame sums its light in linear, as the advanced frame does. The vertex shaders still hand over gamma values in the unit of the classic frame (a light of one half leaves the texture as it is); the pixel shader doubles and decodes each of them: what comes from all around (sky, ambient, lightmap, vertex light) as one term, the sun as another, with the plain cosine of its angle and its shadow as linear shares, the local lights, and the GGX highlights of the sun and the lamps as light. The result is encoded once at the end (`default_shade`, `surface_plain`). The light colours are therefore half of what the advanced frame decodes (`ShaderConstants.cpp`): sun `r2_sun_lumscale / 2`, ambient `r2_sun_lumscale_amb`, hemi `r2_sun_lumscale_hemi * 1.08` (its hemi colour is four times the weather's, times its ambient cube; the forward shaders have no cube, so how bright the two weathers' cubes are is measured when they load, the mean of a cube's DXT1 texels without the side that faces the ground (`AmbientCubeLevel`), and the hemi colour takes `2 * level`, blended as the weather blends: about 0.54 by day and 0.25 at night, where a fixed 0.54 left ground lit by the sky alone three times too bright in linear light). Trees and multi-use models (a parked truck is one: `SurfaceLight_Tree`) follow the same rule as the level at high lighting quality: the lamp light baked into them is left out (`USE_SURFACE_DYNAMIC`), and their sky light is half of their baked hemi, as the deferred tree shader takes it; before, a truck beside a baked fire stayed warm-lit at night while the ground around it was not; the sky dome keeps a tuned 0.78. Low shading quality keeps the gamma sum and the raw weather colours.

With `USE_LEGACY_LIGHT` (set in `engine_external.ltx` of every pack) the pass that lights the frame's surfaces (`common/scene_light.cs.hlsl`) sums as the deferred frame of the old light model did: the sky, the sun and the local lights are added as gamma values, a lamp's falloff and the cosine of its angle with them, the occlusion multiplies the sky's light as it is, and the sum is decoded once. Summed in linear light a lamp faded with distance by the 2.2 root of its falloff and lit a wall 1.3 to 2.9 times as bright as in that frame (gamma values, `light_flicker`, captures of both frames with the same albedo, sky share and gloss at the pixel). The forward shaders (the HUD, blended surfaces, MSAA, medium shading quality) sum the same way: `surface_lit` of `common/surface_forward.hlsli` decodes the sum, `surface_data::sun` and `local` are gamma values then, and the baked water takes the local lights as they are (`water_local`). Highlights and what a surface mirrors are still added in linear light.

With `USE_LEGACY_LIGHT` the final pass exposes and curves the picture as that frame did, and `r_tonemap_compression`, `r_tonemap_white`, `r_tonemap_desaturation` and the exposure's response and range do nothing: `bloom_lum_copy` keeps the display luminance in place of its logarithm, the exposure is the classic `0.86 / (0.7 x average + 0.307)` held between 1/128 and 20, times two to the power of `r_exposure_bias` (`DisplayExposure` of `common/common_functions.hlsli`: a middle gray of 0.8, a low luminance of 0.01 and an amount of 0.7, the defaults of the removed `r2_tonemap_*`), and the curve is `x (1 + 0.346 x) / (1 + x)` over the exposed display value (`LegacyToneMapping`). It reproduces the old frame's capture: 0.1337 at a scale of 2.005 gives 0.231, 0.2314 there. The sky's colour is scaled by 1.2, that frame's `r2_sun_lumscale_sky`, in place of the 0.78 tuned for the other curve. A self-lit material (`r1_selflight`, `SURFACE_SELFLIGHT`) gives off four times its texture, as `accum_emissive` of that frame added it; a self-lit material of the level is kept out of the pass that lights the frame's surfaces (`MaterialFeature::Unlit`) and drawn forward. The engine always defines `USE_LEGACY_LIGHT` (`ShaderDefines.cpp`), whatever `engine_external.ltx` says.

A local light lights the grass by the angle it has to the ground the grass stands on, not by a blade's normal (`SceneClass_Grass` in `common/scene_light.cs.hlsl`, `default_light` of `common/surface_forward.hlsli`): a blade's normal faces the eye, and by it a torch at the eye lit a tuft 2.2 times as bright as the ground around it, where the deferred frame of the old light model had it at 0.6 times (medians of the light over the albedo, `test_scene_2`, captures of both frames).

With `USE_LEGACY_LIGHT` a surface with the old gloss map mirrors its surroundings as that frame's ambient highlight did, in the light pass and in the forward shaders: `gloss * 0.5 * (N.V)^32`, most of it seen head-on and nothing at a graze, in place of the Fresnel fit, which made wet terrain a mirror toward the horizon. The sun shafts of `r_volumetric_quality medium` and up are that frame's default ones, the alternative march of `lighting/accum_volumetric_sun.hlsl` (two Schlick lobes forward of the sun, absorption tinted by the fog, a fade to the fog at the far end; 16 samples at medium and high, 24 at ultra); `low` keeps the original shafts. The original ones at medium laid an even haze of 0.16 linear over the sky and everything far (`wrong_shading`, weather `sun_shafts`: far pylons 141 against 127 in that frame, 120 with its march).

A lamp's shadow is looked up from a point lifted off its surface along the normal by a texel of the lamp's map at that depth, times `1 + 3 (1 - N.L)`, and by 2.5 cm at least (`surface_lamp_shape` of `common/surface_light.hlsli`), as the sun's cascades lift theirs. The fixed 2.5 cm of the deferred frame is less than a texel a few metres from a torch (a 512 map): ground the torch grazed shadowed itself in stripes a pixel row apart, in that frame too (`test_scene_2`: mean difference of neighbouring rows 1.70 there, 1.40 here before, 0.82 after, against 1.0 between rows two apart).

Far grass: past the distance where the far mesh takes over, a blade the thinning kept draws its far mesh once for every blade the thinning took around it, up to eight (`SCENE_GRASS_COPIES`; the count is `1 / kept share`, its fraction settled by the blade's own lot, in `common/scene_grass_cull.cs.hlsl`). A copy stands 0.3 to 1 m from the blade on the blade's own ground plane, turned its own way (bits 20-23 of a visible entry, `pull_grass`). Only the forward list has copies; shadow views draw one mesh a blade, the same whole or far mesh the main view picks. On the far hill of `caustics` the grass covered 36.5 % of the patch without copies and 44.4 % with them, at the same frame time (1.57 ms). What does not sway (leaves, twigs) is drawn a centimetre above its place: in the ground's plane it won or lost the depth test by where the eye stood. A grass texture is black where it is cut out, so its colour is divided by the alpha it was read with (0.25 at least).

The frame's lights are taken nearest to the eye first (by distance less range, sorted before `RenderForwardLampShadows`): it has room for 63 lights and 64 shadow maps, a point light takes six, and in the order the lights were found a lamp two metres from the eye had no shadow while lamps 22 to 40 m off held every map (`shadow_problem`). The baked water's trace of the screen (`water_mirror`) reaches as far as the fog distance in 32 steps; reaching twice the water's distance plus 4 m it ended just past a far bank and mirrored the feet of the trees on it (`ssr_problem`).

Lamps the eye cannot see (2026-11-26, `LampSelection.cpp`, `common/scene_lamps_seen.cs.hlsl`): where the lamp maps are drawn, a compute pass tests the sphere of every shadowed lamp (position, range; up to 64) against the depth pyramid of the frame before, in that frame's view (`HizOfFrameBefore`), and writes a word a lamp; the buffer is copied into a ring of eight readback buffers and the collect reads the oldest, seven frames old, with the frame's number in it as a stamp (`LampSelection::Read`). A lamp that answer finds wholly behind what the frame drew is unseen: its parts that have a cached depth keep it and cast nothing that moves into it, and no part of it draws grass (`SpotShadowPart::IsUnseen`); a part drawn whole casts as before, and a lamp with no part left that casts asks the game for no objects (`LampWanted`). The part of a sphere past the screen's edge is left out of the test, and a lamp whose sphere reaches the near plane (the eye inside its range) is always seen, so a lamp in the next room whose range reaches the eye is not dropped. A lamp no answer names (a new one, a camera cut, no pyramid: `r_culling_quality 0` or a frame that is not drawn from the scene's clusters) is seen. A lamp that comes into view casts what moves up to seven frames late; its static shadow is there at once. `r4_spot_shadow_cache_stats` prints `unseen=` (parts). A lamp part draws grass only when the level's grass slots hold any within the lamp's range (`CDetailManager::HasGrassNear`, read from the level's slots, not the cache): before, every part within 40 m culled every blade and drew, grass in reach or not. On `light_problem` (ultra, 1920x1080, D3D12, `r_render_thread off`): `ForwardLampShadows` on the GPU 2.12 to 0.46 ms (`SceneGrass` 30 draws and 1.75 ms to 1 and 0.06), on the primary thread 0.87 to 0.48 ms, the GPU frame 6.50 to 4.87 ms; six of 36 parts unseen in that view, which the pass times do not show.

`r_debug_view` on the paths that light a pixel once: the pass that lights the frame's surfaces shows `base`, `normal`, `roughness`, `hemi`, `lit`, `highlight` and `mirror`; for every other view (`overdraw_raster`, `overdraw_shaded`, `clusters`, `triangles`, `instances`, `materials`, `lights`, `lmap`, `streaming`, `heightfield`) the frame is drawn forward (`GpuScene::IsVisibilityFrame`), with the same clusters in the same order, so the two overdraw counts are of that forward draw and not of the id pass. A debug view is shown with an exposure of one and no bloom (`phase_combine`): exposed by its own average, a count read brighter the fewer layers the frame had. The two overdraw views add the same step a layer (`DEBUG_OVERDRAW_STEP`, written undecoded by the colour pass too), so `overdraw_raster` is never under `overdraw_shaded`; with no depth pass before it (ambient occlusion off) `overdraw_raster` writes depth itself, or the sky drawn after it covered the count. At low shading quality `overdraw_shaded` counts what is cut out by its texture as whole triangles: there is no depth pass whose shader makes the cut.

`r_rendering_path` (it replaces `r_visibility_buffer`) says how the level's opaque surfaces reach the frame. 0, the default, is the preset's choice (`RenderingPath`: 2 for every preset). 1, deferred: every level surface keeps its surface record in the depth pass, as the terrain and the models do, and `common/scene_light.cs.hlsl` lights it once a pixel. 2: the level is drawn as ids and resolved (`common/scene_resolve.cs.hlsl`), the terrain, the models and the grass keep their surfaces as in 1. 3: forward, every surface shaded by its own pixel shader. 1 and 2 fall back to 3 where the settings rule the lit-once pass out (`GpuScene::IsVisibilityFrame`): ambient occlusion off, shading quality below high (no mapped normals in the thin target), or MSAA. Shadow quality does not decide it: from medium shading quality up the level is lit by the weather's sun and its lamps, with nothing a lightmap holds of them (`ForwardDynamicLight`, `USE_SURFACE_DYNAMIC`), and `ps_r_lighting_quality` only says how many cascades shadow that sun: none at shadow quality off, the widest alone at low and medium (`SURFACE_SINGLE_CASCADE`), all from high. The heightfield shadow covers what the cascades leave at every one of them. Low shading quality alone keeps the baked light and the baked sun direction.

What low shading quality (the minimal preset) leaves out besides: nothing is tested against a depth pyramid at culling quality low (`GpuScene::BuildHiZ` builds none, the view's one cull draws all its frustum holds, and what follows it draws the grass alone); no surface is drawn as its depth first, as that split is made only for the occlusion pass or MSAA; the terrain takes its one detail texture (`impl_dt`) in place of the four masked layers. Low shading quality fogs by the vertex's share (`r1_fog`) to the fog colour and then, as the other qualities do, into the sky behind the pixel by the fog squared (`RenderSkyBehind` runs at every quality): fogged to the one colour alone a far tree stood as a pale shape against the clouds. A model's glass (`CBlender_Model` with strict sorting, or blended with an alpha reference under 16) is one blended pass with no cut and no depth write, sorted back to front, as the deferred frame's `uber_forward` drew it: written into the depth, the lens of a collimator sight hid the reticle's mark a hair behind it. The reticle (`screens/models_reflex_reticle.hlsl`) reads its mark through the model's material when the scene draws it. The bullets' tracers and the HUD's world primitives (`OnRenderPPUI_main`) are drawn with the frame's depth bound; with none they showed through walls. At every quality the pass that pushes the picture by the distortion mask (`DistortCombine`) runs only in a frame that drew something into the mask, or under the main menu.

Local lights are not part of that sum. A lamp added to the sun and the sky before the decode came out about twenty times too bright in daylight, so the forward shaders gather the local lights apart, in linear light with decoded colours as `accum_base.hlsl` takes them (`surface_data::local`), and `surface_add_local` adds them to the decoded colour. Their highlights are still added in gamma space, and a metal no longer mirrors a lamp through its `reflect` share.

## Shading quality

`r_shading_quality low|medium|high|ultra` (default `high`) sets how much the scene resolve spends on a pixel of level geometry. `high` runs everything with 16-tap anisotropic filtering, including steep parallax of the materials whose textures ask for it and of terrain layers, when those options are enabled. `medium` leaves out the roughness filter that widens highlights where the normal changes fast between pixels, uses one tangent frame per triangle, and filters bump and surface maps with 4 taps and lightmaps and the terrain mask with plain trilinear filtering. `low` also leaves out the detail maps of non-terrain materials and the second bump map of non-PBR materials, and filters colour maps with 4 taps and everything else trilinearly. The setting also sets the texture anisotropy (1 / 2 / 4 / 8x for `low` to `ultra`). Each level is its own shader variant, compiled the first time it is used. The pass also lights the level's pixels with the sun, the ambient and the fog, through the same shader code as the deferred sun and combine passes, into a target of its own. Nothing on screen reads those targets yet, so the setting does not change the picture.

What each tier of `r_shading_quality` adds on the simplified frame, as of 2026-11-16:

| Preset | Define | Picture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| low | `USE_SURFACE_CLASSIC` | The classic baked look: weather colours unscaled, local lights added to the baked light in gamma space, no bloom and no colour grading (`combine_2.hlsl` decodes the gamma scene, then exposes and tone-maps it as the other tiers do; the pass is rebuilt when the quality changes), no bump, no sun cascade, no snow. |
| medium | - | Bump and PBR maps, the sun cascade, tone mapping, the light scales, local lights in linear light, and the sky cube mirrored in bumped surfaces (`USE_SURFACE_IBL`, sky cube branch). A bumped model takes its sky light as the dynamic renderer does: the tracker's six-face cube by the vertex normal (`hemi_cube_*`, filled for the baked frame too), times `1.256 * hemi colour`; the same share is how much sky it mirrors. The sky part of the light is tinted by the coarsest sky cube level at half strength, and follows the mapped normal at 30% of the `normal.y` ramp. The HUD takes the sun's shadow of one point near the eye and its contact shadow at full strength with no sun left in it. Screen-space occlusion multiplies the light gamma-encoded, and `gtao_render.hlsl` takes its normal from the depth on both frames. |
| high | `USE_SURFACE_HIGH` | Medium plus occlusion, HUD contact shadows, screen-space reflections, rain wetness, the light that scatters through a surface with a scatter value in its PBR map (`surface_scatter`: the skin term on models, the soft edge on foliage and terrain), and the detail texture's bump maps on non-terrain materials. |
| ultra | `USE_SURFACE_HIGH` | High plus order-independent transparency of the materials that ask for it, when MSAA is off. |

Detail bump (high shading quality, colour pass only): a bumped surface whose detail texture has bump maps (`r_detail_bump`, the texture description) binds them as `s_detailBump` / `s_detailBumpX` (`R1Bump` in `r1_blender_tex.h`, `USE_TDETAIL_BUMP`), and `default_fetch` adds the detail normal as `MaterialDetail` of the advanced frame does: before the PBR green is turned and before an old normal map's height is halved; PBR metalness and roughness are scaled by twice the detail's bump maps, its scatter and occlusion by them, an old gloss by twice the detail alpha (clamped to 0..1 where the advanced frame is not). All of it fades out with the detail colour (`calc_detail`), which the advanced frame does not fade. The scene's batched draws read the maps from the material record (`SCENE_MATERIAL_DETAIL_BUMP`); the define is left out of their folded passes, so the bins do not split. The depth pass and its thin normal do not take it. A material whose detail bump has no place in the buckets stays batched without it; the load prints `* [scene] detail bump: N materials have one, M of them are drawn without it`, and a texture with a detail bump but no bump map of its own prints `! [forward] texture [...] has a detail bump but no bump of its own`. Models take the detail texture as the level does (`CBlender_Model::canBeDetailed`, `model_def_hq_dt`): a door or a crate was smooth beside a grained wall. A model the scene draws has no `dt_params.xy` of its own, so its vertex stage scales the detail coordinates by the material record's `detail_scale`.

Particles of the forward frame fog by view depth (`r1_fog_depth`): a HUD particle is drawn camera-relative, where the world fog plane would fade it by where the camera looks.

The simplified frame lights and shadows by the sun direction stored in the level file, the one its lightmaps were baked with (`CLight_DB::SunBaked`, `SunDirection()`), not by the weather's moving sun: what the cascade casts stays where the lightmaps have it. The sun disc and its flares still follow the weather.

## Shader files

`shaders\` holds no files at its top level. Folders: `common` (shared includes, the scene's own passes), `surface`, `lighting`, `postfx`, `effects`, `ui`, `screens`, `editor`, `blends` (the XML blends).

A shader stage is asked for by name (`bloom_downsample`, `postprocess_CM`, `lmap_dt`). The loader tries, in this order:

1. A file of the old layout, `<name>.<stage>.hlsl`, under `shaders\d3d11\` and then `shaders\`. An addon's file wins this way.
2. A name listed in a description (`.json`) in one of the folders: a permutation of the `.hlsl` file beside the description.
3. The shader's own file, `<folder>\<name>.hlsl`.

For 2 and 3 the source handed to the compiler is generated (the permutation's defines and one include) and the compiler is asked for the entry point `<stage>_main`; a file of the old layout is compiled with the entry `main`. A name must exist once: two files of the same name in different folders shadow each other (the folders are searched in a fixed order). Names are matched without regard to case.

**One file per shader.** Outside `surface`, a shader is one file named after it that holds all its stages side by side, with entry points `vs_main`, `ps_main`, `gs_main`, `cs_main`. The record one stage hands to the next and the resources both read are declared once. A variant that is only defines over another shader is not a file: it is a line in `<shader>.json`:

```json
{
    "type": "Custom",
    "permutations": {
        "ps": { "particle_hard": ["DISABLE_SOFT_PARTICLES"] }
    }
}
```

**Surface shaders.** No shader in `surface\` selects a section by a `*_PASS` value any more. A file is one shader: one `vs_main` and one `ps_main`, with no stage guards; its permutations are the few defines its `.json` gives each name, read in place where the variants part, and the rest of the code is shared. Each file says at its top what its defines mean. The files the deferred frame used and the XML blends still name: `deferred` (vertex: level, `DEFERRED_TREE`, `DEFERRED_GRASS`, `DEFERRED_MODEL`; pixel: G-buffer, `DEFERRED_TERRAIN`, `DEFERRED_LIT` - the former `forward_base`; names `deffer_*`, `forward_base`; `deffer_model` + `ui_base` light the 3D icons), `shadow` (`SHADOW_TREE`, `SHADOW_MODEL`), `lod`, `interior` (`forwrad_interior`), `faded` (surfaces that fade by how they face the eye: `base_lplanes` and the `model_distort*` vertex shaders many effect pixel shaders pair with), `lod_atlas` (the editor's `lod_downsample`), `detail` (the brush of the detail layers editor). The forward level frame's: `world`, `imposter`, `billboard` (particles and `r1_glow`), `lightplane` (and the unlit `r1_simple`), `wallmark`, `water_baked` (`r1_water`, `r1_waterd`), all on the entries below; `blob` (`r1_shadow_blur`, `r1_shadow_world`, kept for materials that name those blenders); the distortion combine is `postfx\r1_distort.hlsl`. The rewrite of `deferred`, `shadow`, `lod`, `tess` (deleted with the deferred frame) and `interior` was checked by bytecode: every permutation, alone and with each define its file reacts to, compiles with fxc to the same stripped bytes as the packed file did.

A surface shader drawn forward is a file of entries only (`fetch_entry`: the surface under the pixel; `light_entry`: what one local light adds; `shade_entry`: its final colour), between two includes: `common\surface_forward.hlsli` (the vertex-to-pixel record, the defaults `default_fetch` / `default_shade`, the feature constants) and `common\surface_forward_entry.hlsli` (the one `vs_main` and the one `ps_main` of all of them). What used to be separate shaders are feature sets of it, listed in its description: `FEATURE_SET` says what the vertex is (level geometry, terrain, tree), where the baked light comes from (lightmap, vertex, terrain lightmap with hemi in the base alpha, tree) and which extras it has (detail texture, four masked detail layers, no sway).

In static lighting mode `r_shading_quality high` adds bump and specular to level surfaces whose base texture has a bump map (lightmapped and vertex-lit ones; not terrain, trees or models): the sun is shaded per pixel with the mapped normal and leaves a highlight scaled by the map's gloss, both let through by the baked sun visibility as before. The baked light of lamps and the hemisphere have no direction and stay as they are, so indoors without sun the surfaces look the same. The choice is made when a level's shaders are built: after changing `r_shading_quality` reload the level. Lower qualities use the plain shaders.

Local lights of a frame whose level is drawn forward (lamps, the torch, anomalies; not the ones baked into lightmaps) are gathered while the frame collects and handed to every surface shader as one buffer (`g_surface_lights`, at most 63 a frame). A surface walks those that reach its 16-pixel tile of the screen (`light_tile_word` of `common/light_lists.hlsli`, the bits `light_tiles` marks for the pass that lights the frame's surfaces; a face of the cube around the eye, which is no part of the screen, still loops over all of them): range falloff as in the dynamic renderer, a cone for spots, no shadows, diffuse only. A surface with no side (a particle) takes them from every direction.

Tree cards seen from afar (`surface/imposter`, name `lod_static`), self-lit surfaces (`r1_selflight`, `r1_model_selflight` in `surface/world`) and planes of light (`surface/lightplane`: `r1_lplanes`, `r1_model_lplanes`, `r1_exoscreen`) are forward Surface shaders too; a surface file may add `vtx_entry` (define `USE_VERTEX_ENTRY`) to change the vertex the shared stage made. The scene tables, cluster cache and texture buckets of the visibility-buffer path are not built at level load, and grass is drawn only by the forward detail draw.

Fog of models in static mode is taken from the depth of the view, not from a plane in the world: the HUD is drawn in a space of its own, and the world's plane fogged the weapon depending on where the camera looked.

`surface\billboard.hlsl` is the particle of static lighting mode (`r1_particle`): set and blended particles are lit by the sky and the sun (`r1_particle_lit`), additive and multiplying ones are left as they are.

`surface\world.hlsl` is the first: in static lighting mode it is the shader of `lmap`, `lmap_dt`, `vert`, `vert_dt`, `impl_dt`, `impl_dt_hq` and `tree_w` / `tree_s` (`_dt`). The include handler hands out each file once per compile, so a file cannot include itself.

Check after moving or rewriting shaders: every name must still preprocess (and, for a rearrangement, to the same text) under the plain, static, editor and feature-rich define sets; then start the game in dynamic and in static mode and look for `is missing. Replace with stub_default` and `has no permutations` in the log.

## Render features

The renderer has one record of switches, `RenderFeatures` (`g_RenderFeatures`, `src/xrEngine/RenderFeatures.h`). Code asks for the feature it depends on. The renderer fills the record when it starts.

| Feature | Value | What depends on it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `SunShadows` | above low shading and lighting quality, set every frame by `SyncShadingQuality` | the sun cascades |
| `LampShadows` | on | shadow flags, textures of lights, the spot shadow cache |
| `Volumetrics`, `DepthOfField` | on | sun shafts and volumetric lamps; the depth of field; the user's own option for each still applies on top |
| `MotionVectors` | on | the velocity target, the projection jitter, TAA and motion blur |
| `Upscaling` | on | the render scale and the upscalers |
| `SpriteGlows` | on | the sprite glow manager |

Each feature has a console switch, `r_<name>` (`sun_shadows`, `lamp_shadows`, `volumetrics`, `depth_of_field`, `motion_vectors`, `upscaling`, `sprite_glows`) with the values `preset`, `off`, `on`. The switches are read from the config before the renderer starts and take effect at the next start; each one that is not `preset` is written to the log with what it resolved to. `RenderFeaturesResolve` keeps `SunShadows` off until the renderer sets it and `Upscaling` behind `MotionVectors`. The frame always lights by the baked light model and is always forward; the game lights its lamps as a dynamic renderer does (`get_generation` is `GENERATION_R2`). They are to be replaced by the settings scheme.

The XML blend attribute of the baked family is `simplified="1"` (was `r1_static`); the blend option `USE_SIMPLIFIED_LIGHTING` is always taken, and the shipped blends have it folded into their own content. The shader define `USE_SIMPLIFIED_LIGHTING` is no longer set. `USE_LEGACY_LIGHT` is always set; `ui/ui_base.hlsl` undefines it for 3D icons above low shading quality, so the files it includes keep both light models. Console: `r1_lmodel_lerp`, `r1_dlights`, `r1_dlights_clip` and `r1_detail_textures` are removed (nothing read them); `r1_pps_u`, `r1_pps_v`, `r1_glows_per_frame`, `r1_fog_luminance`, `r1_full_detail_distance_scale` and `r1_use_terrain_mask` are now `r_pps_u`, `r_pps_v`, `r_glows_per_frame`, `r_fog_luminance`, `r_full_detail_distance_scale` and `r_terrain_mask`.

Shading quality in the simplified preset (`r_shading_quality`, applied at once, no restart): `low` is the plain baked shading of the old static renderer; `medium` adds bump with the legacy or PBR highlight (below) and the sun cascade; `high` adds the sky mirrored in bumped surfaces and gives the occlusion pass the mapped normals of the surfaces instead of normals built from the depth. `r_sun_shadows` does not act in this preset: the renderer sets `SunShadows` from the quality every frame (`CRender::SyncShadingQuality`), and any change of the quality compiles the materials again.

The sun cascade of the simplified preset: one slice (the far cascade's slice and size, placed around the eye, not along the view as in the advanced frame; its centre is built from whole steps along the light's three axes, a texel sideways and 4 m in depth, so the matrix is bit-identical while the eye stays inside one step) is rendered each frame with the level, trees and dynamic objects (`CRender::RenderFarSunCascade`, `SunCascadeFar.cpp`; the baked-light blenders have a `Shadow` element). Which cascades a frame draws, and their matrices, are settled when the forward frame starts (`BeginFarSunCascades`, after the occlusion map): the portals of each are walked on the task pool (`SunCascadeBuild`, a task a cascade, from a copy of its view no other code writes; the static leftovers of the batch are collected there too when it leaves the cascades any) while the main view collects, and `RenderFarSunCascade` waits for them (`SunCascadeWait`) before it takes the dynamic objects on the primary thread. Since 2026-11 the frame draws all three cascades this way (`r2_sun_near`, their geometric mean, `r2_sun_far`), one inside the other around the eye: the nearest every frame, the other two a frame each in turn, a cascade that waits being sampled with the matrix it was drawn with. `surface_sun_shadow` takes the finest cascade a point is inside and gives way to the next at its edge. Grass casts into the first `r2_sun_details_cascades` of them. The level's static casters come from the scene's GPU-culled depth batch, as in the advanced frame (`GpuScene::DrawDepth`): the scene is loaded in both presets, in this one without the material texture buckets (only the alpha-tested bases are copied, for the depth pass), and the cascade's graph holds the few statics the batch leaves out and the moving objects it does not take (see `r_scene_models` for the moving casters of the cascades).

The simplified frame draws the level's lightmapped surfaces of the default blender from the scene's clusters (`GpuScene::CullForward`, `GpuScene::DrawForward`): the cull sorts the visible clusters by pipeline (the shaders and state of a material's `ShaderVariant::Cluster` passes, compiled as the high-quality element with `SURFACE_CLUSTER`), and the frame makes one indirect draw per pipeline through NRI for the depth pass and one for the colour pass. The vertex stage pulls its vertex by cluster (`common/scene_forward.hlsli`), the pixel stage takes the material's maps, the lightmap included, from the scene's texture buckets (`common/scene_buckets.hlsli`). Visuals drawn this way are left out of the main graph (`IsForwardBatched`). The head slice of a streamed texture is looked up in a structured buffer (`resolve_heads`, pixel t57 in the forward shaders and t48 in the compute shading pass, written once a frame): as an array in a constant block the same lookup cost the terrain's depth draw 0.49 ms in place of 0.14. The scene's textures leave out the levels the engine's own loader leaves out (`render.experemental.target_res`, `r_streaming_quality`). A map is one read: the head slice where it is loaded, which holds the texture's whole mip chain, else the tail (`sample_map`); working the level out in the shader and blending two reads cost a wall-filling view about a quarter of its pixel time. Streamed heads are asked for by the cull, not by pixels: a shown cluster asks for the textures of its material when its texel density (measured once on the GPU, `scene_density`) and its distance need more than the tail (`AskHeads` in `scene_cull`; the feedback buffer is read back each frame, `GpuTextures::Update`); the grass asks for its own while there is grass. The frame's local lights and decals reach it as the scene's own copies of the records (`GpuScene::SetForwardRecords`): the old backend's dynamic buffers cannot be bound by another device. The default, alpha-tested, vertex-lit, tree and terrain blenders (`B_BmmD` and `B_LmBmmD`, one class), the environment-mapped lightmapped one (`B_LmEbB`, not its blended variant) and the emissive one (`B_LaEmB`) compile the element; blended passes and level visuals of model shaders still go through the graph. LOD cards (`FLOD`, `details_lod.xml`) are records of the scene (`SceneCard`: the group's sphere, the eight views' normals, the facets' place in the heap, the material): `GpuScene::CullCards` runs after the main view's clusters are drawn and keeps a card in the frustum, a visited sector and the fog whose group's `radius / distance^2 * lod_factor` is at least the discard threshold (`SceneView.thresholds.x`) and under `r2_ssa_lod_a`, not hidden by this frame's depth pyramid (no CPU occlusion), and writes `SCENE_CARD_WORDS` words a card past everything else in the visible list: the shift towards the eye, the two views that face it most, the blend between them and the fade (as `r_dsgraph_render_lods` made them). `DrawForward` draws every kept card twice from one indirect draw of six vertices an instance: cut out at ForwardLods (the card material's `l_special` pass, alpha reference 200, alpha to coverage under MSAA) and blended where it fades in at ForwardOpaqueLate (its `normal` pass, no depth write); the views and their hemi are the material's base and hemi maps in the buckets (`s_base0`, `s_hemi0`, read so for the card materials alone: other surfaces bind other textures to those registers). The cards' maps take their places in the buckets after every other material's, so the level and the grass never lose a bucket to an atlas, and a map that found no bucket is bound as it is (see the textures bound as they are below; load line `textures without a place in the buckets: N (names)`). The forward pipelines take blend, depth write and alpha to coverage from the pass state. A card whose material has a map neither in the buckets nor bound, whose passes are not those of the first card material, or whose facets are outside the heap's pages stays in the graph's `mapLOD` (the load line `LOD cards: N of M drawn by the scene; left to the graph: ...`), and the editors draw all of theirs there. An environment-mapped lightmapped surface keeps its own shaders (`lmapE`, not folded into `lmap`) and reads its cube from the scene's cube array. An emissive surface's map takes the place of the mask in its record (`SceneMaterial.terrain[0]`, `s_emission`); `USE_R1_EMISSION` is now compiled in (the blender cleared its defines before the pass was compiled, so emission never showed). Whether the main view draws a level visual from the clusters is decided by its material alone (`LevelBinWhy`: a cluster pass, every map in the buckets or bound as it is, a mirrored cube the cube array holds, not a model shader), whatever its shadow: a visual the depth batch does not cast has clusters for the main view alone (`SCENE_CLUSTER_MAIN` without `SCENE_CLUSTER_DEPTH`), and a caster the main view does not take has them for the shadows alone. A tree's place and baked-light factors come from the scene's tables, and the cull drops the meshes of an LOD group, trees and other meshes alike, when the group is small enough on screen for its card to stand in (`SceneTreeLight.lod`, `SceneView::lod`, the threshold of `r2_ssa_lod_b`, as the graph drops the group's children); a mesh of a group that is not a tree has a transform slot of its own, the identity, for the group alone. A mesh LOD group (`MT_MESH_LODS`, the levels picked by distance) has nothing in the scene: the graph draws it in every view. Texture detail on these surfaces is bounded by `r_texture_budget`: the heads of streamed textures share that pool. Each level mesh is a hierarchy of clusters built at load and cached (`clusterlod.h` of meshoptimizer): coarser clusters are made by simplifying groups of finer ones, indices only, and the cull draws a cluster while its own error is under `r_lod_error` pixels (default 1, 0 turns it off) and the error of the group made from it is over; every view, shadows included, takes the detail by the main view's eye. Alpha-tested meshes are never simplified. A view that fills the pipeline lists runs its cluster pass once per band of distance (`SCENE_BANDS`, 4 / 12 / 40 m, the band in `thresholds.w`): alpha-tested clusters are taken by the band they stand in and so come out near to far, which lets the depth of near leaves reject far ones; opaque clusters are all taken by the first pass. Every cull of the scene has two levels (`GpuScene::Dispatch`): `scene_ranges` tests one sphere per run of clusters of an instance (`SceneRange`, built from the clusters themselves) against the view's planes and writes the runs it keeps as chunks of 64 clusters, and `scene_cull` is dispatched indirectly over those chunks only, so a view does not read the clusters of what is outside it (on `test_rain` 4.0 k of 135 k clusters are in the main frustum). The main view is culled in two parts against a depth pyramid (`GpuScene::BuildHiZ`; `scene_hiz` writes six levels a dispatch, a group of 16 x 16 threads passing each next level down through its shared memory, so the pyramid takes two dispatches; it starts at a quarter of the depth's size, 256 at 1080p, as no cluster is small enough on screen to be tested on a finer level, and its first level is read by gathers). A cluster is tested as a sphere and as a box (`SceneCluster.extent`), and is hidden when either is: a sphere around flat or long geometry reaches the eye long before its box does, and a bound that reaches the near plane cannot be tested at all, so room-sized floors and roofs next to the eye are still drawn. The test (`common/scene_occlusion.hlsli`) reads the four corners of the sphere's rectangle on the level where it is two texels wide, or one level finer where it still lies on two texels; `scene_ranges` makes it for a whole run of clusters first, and a hidden run is passed on only to have its clusters marked as not seen): what the frame before showed is drawn first, then whatever that depth does not hide. `r_anti_aliasing msaa` turns the path off. Its casters are taken at full detail with no size or distance culling. It is captured along the direction of the level's own directional light (`CLight_DB::SunBakedDirection`, from level.lights), not the weather's; the shading of surfaces still takes the weather's direction. The forward surface entry reads it (`surface_sun_shadow`): inside the cascade its value replaces the baked sun shadow of the surface, whether that comes from a lightmap's alpha or was baked into the vertex or the instance (vertex-lit statics, statics of the tree blender); past its edge the baked one remains, with a fade between them. Models and grass, which have no baked sun shadow, multiply their sun term by it. Tree cards, particles and unlit surfaces do not read it. Blob shadows (the projected per-object shadows of the old static renderer) are removed. The other feature switches are applied at the next start, and the console says so.

Grass in the simplified preset is thinned with distance when the visible instances are gathered (`GrassThinSize`, `GrassThin.h`): the rule of the scene's GPU cull (`GrassSize`, `scene_tables.hlsli`), full density up to 24 m, then a share of the blades that falls with the square of the distance down to 1/16, the kept ones growing to cover the ground. Past about 20 m (15 to 25 m, a blade at a time: `GrassIsFar`, the rule of `scene_grass_cull.cs.hlsl`) an instance is drawn with the far mesh of its model, a quarter of the faces: each list of instances is kept near first, and a model takes two draws. The blades are not sent every frame: the details worker sends a slot's blades to the scene's blade buffer when a frame first shows the slot (`CDetailManager::StoreSlot`, `GrassSlotStore`), only those its distance can keep, with room to come nearer (a slot cache holds millions of blades), and again when it is trampled; a frame gets a word a blade, where it is stored and its bin, and the cull works out the thinning size and the far mesh itself with the worker's own lot. The frame draws it with the scene's clusters (`GpuScene::DrawGrass`, `GpuScene::DrawForward`): after the depth pyramid of the level's first part is built, the gathered instances are culled on the GPU, each blade against the frustum and that pyramid (which hides about 60 % of them on `test_rain`), into one list per model and mesh, and each is one indexed instanced indirect draw from the scene's grass index buffer, back faces culled as in the graph's draws, after the opaque surfaces, the terrain and the cut-out ones. On `test_rain` at 1080p (2026-11-16) the depth pass runs the pixel stage 17.8 M times against the graph's 18.9 M; its draws take 0.36 ms of GPU time and the pass 0.59 ms with the culls and the depth pyramid, against the graph's 0.44 ms. Each pipeline's draw has its own GPU scope, named by its pixel shader. The lot of a blade on the CPU is a hash of its position, not the shader's (nothing in this preset needs the two to agree); a slot keeps its instances in the order of their lot, so the gather stops at the first one the slot's distance thins out. Its cost is geometry, not pixels: on `render_perf` at 1080p the pass went from 1.15 ms to 0.12 ms.

The scene's grass draws cut their pixels by `r2_aref_quality` (default 115), the reference of the advanced preset's grass, not by the 200 of the detail blender: with 200 a blade keeps about half of its pixels. In the depth pass a cut-out drawn in clusters tests its alpha with a plain `discard`; under MSAA it writes the samples that pass the test as `SV_Coverage`, as the graph's passes do (`SURFACE_CLUSTER_COVERAGE`). Where the device shades at a coarse rate with per-pixel coverage (variable-rate shading tier 2) and MSAA is off, `USE_COARSE_ALPHA` draws the depth pass of cut-out clusters and of the grass at one pixel shader run per 2x2 block: the shader tests alpha at each of the four pixels and writes the answers as `SV_Coverage`, depth staying per pixel. The mask's lowest bit is the bottom right pixel and its fourth bit the top left one (measured, not documented). `r_variable_shading` (`off` by default) turns it off and on with one shader recompile; it is the switch of every use of a coarse shading rate. `GpuScene::IsCoarseAlpha` decides; the depth pass of the three reference saves costs 0.09-0.12 ms less with it.

`r_shadow_quality` (off, low, medium, high, ultra; default high) says how the sun is shadowed (it replaces the old `r_lighting_quality`); whose sun it is the shading quality says: from medium up the weather's moving sun with the downward tilt the other frame gives it, and nothing a lightmap holds (`USE_SURFACE_DYNAMIC`: no baked lamp light, no baked sun shadow; static level lamps are not lit, as in the other frame), at low the level's lightmaps and its baked sun. `off`: no sun cascade and no lamp shadows; the heightfield alone shadows the weather's sun. `low` and `medium`: one wide sun cascade (its offsets halved, as its texels are several times larger); the lamps cast shadows from `medium` up. `high`: three sun cascades. `CLight_DB::IsSunBaked` (low shading quality) decides the sun's direction; a change of the setting compiles the materials again. `ultra` (ray traced) is not drawn and runs as `high`. The sun's disc and glare stand where the renderer lights from (`IRender_interface::SunDirection`), not always where the weather has the sun.

Textures bound as they are (2026-11-23, `BindTexture` in `GpuSceneForward.cpp`): a map of a material whose file the buckets cannot take (not a BC1 / BC3 / BC7 2D file with mips, not a power of two, over 4096, or past a full bucket) is bound as it is in the forward draws' set, its whole copy at pixel `t64`..`t79` (`SCENE_TEXTURE_BOUND`, 16 a level, range 12 of `ForwardRanges`). Its slot in the material record names bucket 16 + n, slice 0, with no head, stream or repeat. Only the draws of such materials read it: their pixel stages are compiled again with `SCENE_BUCKETS_BOUND` (`BoundPass`, made once a pass from the stage's own file and defines by `CResourceManager::CreatePSWith`), under which `common/scene_forward.hlsli` declares the registers and `sample_bound`, and `sample_slice` (`common/scene_buckets.hlsli`) branches to it before the buckets. Every other forward pixel stage is compiled without the define and declares none of it: the branch in every forward shader cost the GPU frame 0.2-0.5 ms on D3D12 ultra (`ScenePrepass` up to 13 %). The material is complete. A level or model material with such a map gets a bin of its own (`SceneBin::IsBound`, `FindBin`), whose depth, colour and face passes are the bound passes, so it never shares a bin with materials of the same shaders; the bin keeps the step of its passes (opaque, terrain, cut out, blended) and is drawn among the bins of that step in the order the bins were made, as any other. The sorted lists (level visuals, models, the HUD, distortion, the scope's mask) draw such a material's surfaces with the bound pass of their pass (`SortedStatic::IsBound`, `ModelMaterial::IsBound`), made at the load or when the model joins. The grass and the LOD cards are one draw each: it is made with the define only when one of its materials has such a map. The depth batch and the shadows read the alpha array alone and are not affected. Pipelines are made only for the bins there are: none on a level without such materials; a bound bin adds its colour and depth pipelines (and the face pipeline of a level bin), and the shader manifest lists the extra pixel stages because they were made. A cube, a volume, an array, a render target, a sequence or a video is not bound, and neither is a texture past the 16th (`no bound register left`): its material stays in the graph. A surface that is no single 2D texture when drawn shows the placeholder. A missing file is read as the engine's placeholder (`ed\ed_not_existing_texture`, `ed\ed_dummy_bump` / `ed\ed_dummy_bump#` for a bump), as `texture_load` reads it, and goes into the buckets like any file. A model shader that joins later binds its textures the same way. Load lines: `* [scene] textures bound as they are: N of 16 registers (names); missing files read as the placeholder: M`; `* [scene] materials: C of M complete, B of them with a map bound as it is; not complete by reason: ...`; `* [scene] forward pipelines: ...; N read the textures bound as they are` (the bound bins); the end of `* [scene] LOD cards: ...; the cards' and the grass's draws read the textures bound as they are: yes|no, yes|no`; the reason in the stats lines is `a map neither in the buckets nor bound`.

Video memory of the level's textures in the forward level frame: a texture of a level material lives in the scene's buckets (a tail always, a head streamed into pools that `r_texture_budget` limits), and such a texture has no whole ordinary copy: `CRender::level_Load` defers the load of every texture made while the level's shaders are made, `GpuScene::Load` tells which of them have a place in the buckets (`GpuScene::HasNoWholeCopy`; log line `textures without a whole copy`), and `CResourceManager::UploadPending` loads the rest. So the budget's limit by the video memory left to the process is taken with those copies not there. A draw of the old path that binds such a texture loads it whole (`apply_load`), and the level's unload logs which ones did (`whole copies taken back`): on a marsh save 30 of 632. On that save at 4K textures the card holds 4.95 GB with `r_texture_budget 512` against 7.6 GB with the copies (2026-11-18). The frame that shades a pixel once keeps the whole copies, as its level draws read them. `r_texture_budget` does not limit the textures of objects, weapons and the HUD, nor render targets or geometry. `render_memory_stats` logs the loaded textures and the render targets by their sizes on the device, with the largest of them, and the bytes the textures' levels uploaded (`MB uploaded`).

Which streamed head gets a place in its pool (`GpuTextures::Update`): the scene's cull asks for a head with how much it is needed (the levels of the head the nearest pixel of a visible cluster takes, in the low byte of the feedback word, under the frame; `AskHead` of `scene_cull.cs.hlsl`). Every head has a heat: what it was needed is added for every frame it is asked for and a share is taken away every frame (`HeatKept`, half is left after 138 frames), so one number holds how much, how often and how lately. A head is loaded once its heat reaches `HeatAsked` (what flickers into view for a frame waits), the hottest of those that wait first, one at a time: its file is read and laid out as the slice takes it by a thread of its own (`OneJobThread`, `GpuTextures::ReadHead`), and the frame only copies the finished levels into its place when they are ready (`GpuTextures::TakeLoaded`; read on the primary thread, a head of the largest size stopped the frame for 11-17 ms). It takes a free place, else the place of the coldest head: any head not asked for in `KeepFrames`, or a head `HeatTaken` times colder than itself, asked for or not: a surface in front of the eye takes the place of a far one. The pools share one budget: a pool whose heads have waited `StarvedFrames` with every place of it asked for takes the bytes of places at the end of another pool whose heads nothing has asked for in `KeepFrames`, or are `HeatTaken` times colder than the head that waits (`GpuTextures::Rebalance`; log line `head pools: ... grows by ...`). Both arrays are made again at their new sizes by the same thread (creating one on the primary thread stopped the frame for 14-26 ms) and, once made, the slices kept are copied on the GPU; the sizes change at most once in `ResizeGap` frames; a slice never changes its place, so the frames in flight keep reading the old array until it is destroyed a few frames later. `render_memory_stats` logs every pool: its places, the heads held, those asked for now and those that wait. After the pools it lists the level textures an old-path draw has loaded whole again (`whole copies taken back: N of M textures`, the first twelve by name): each of them was bound by a draw outside the batched path. A texture bound for the first time while it is not loaded is read by a worker (`CTexture::apply_load` starts `CTexture::StartLoading`; needs `r__mt_texture_load on` and a slot that samples a 2D texture) and the placeholder stands for it until the file is read. On every device the worker only reads the file's levels (`TextureDecode::Read`, a plain block-compressed 2D file of power-of-two sides; any other is loaded where it is bound) and queues them; the frame's streaming step makes the queued textures that fit 16 MB a frame, and always one, with NRI and fills them through the scene's streamer (`TextureDecode::Upload`, `UploadBudget`, zone `TextureWholeUpload`, 4.8 ms at its peak when hundreds are asked for at once; one a frame left the level drawn with placeholders for seconds after MSAA was turned on, with the budget it is whole within 120 frames), and the next bind takes it, read by shaders, into the texture's `GpuResource` (`TextureDecode::Create`). NRI makes the texture typeless, so its views name the file's format; a default view of it is invalid and removed the D3D12 device. Outside a level's frames nothing takes the queue (`TextureDecode::IsUploading`) and a bind loads the texture itself. Read inside the draw, a large texture stopped the frame for 10-67 ms. When the scene is built, a level texture a worker is still loading is let go with those already loaded: left alone it finished as a whole copy, 567 of 602 on D3D11 where the level's textures load in parallel.

The forward level frame makes the pipeline of every bin for a pass in the first frame that draws the pass (`GpuScene::DrawForward`; log line `forward pipelines made ahead of their first cluster`), not when a bin's first cluster comes into view.

`r_culling_quality` (low, high; default high): `low` builds no depth pyramid (`GpuScene::BuildHiZ`), so the main view's one cull draws all its frustum holds, nothing is tested against the depth and the grass is not hidden by it; it also takes the lowest grass budget. Both values can be chosen at any shading quality. At `high` the main view's models are tested against the pyramid as well as the frustum (`IsModelHidden` of `scene_cull.cs.hlsl`): in the view's first cull, against the pyramid the frame before left and with that frame's view and projection (`HizView`, `HizProject`), so the answer is that frame's and a model that comes out from behind a wall is drawn one frame late, its sphere showing before it does. A model whose sphere touched that frame's screen edge or its near plane, the HUD, and every model of the frame after a camera cut are not tested; the editors do not test. In a replay of one `shadow_problem` frame the level frame took 5.3 ms with the test and 6.4 ms without it (replay times, not the engine's). Measured on `test_scene_2`, preset high, D3D12, switched live: GPU frame 3.86 ms at high, 3.30 ms at low, the same picture. The grass budget of the forward level frame was fixed at the medium value before (the layers of grass a 32-pixel tile of the screen keeps: 4, 8 or all of them, here 8; `GrassTileSize`, `SceneGrassView::budget`). With a budget the main view's grass cull runs twice (`scene_grass_cull.cs.hlsl`): the first pass adds, for every blade that passed the frustum and the depth pyramid, the share of its tile it covers into the words after the draw arguments (`SCENE_GRASS_TILE_BASE`); the second keeps a blade by its lot where the sum is more layers than the budget. A blade larger than half a tile is the front of the picture and always stays; the shadow views are not budgeted. On `grass_overdraw` at 1080p (D3D11, 2026-11-19) it draws 5630 blades for 8163 at 8 layers and 4548 at 4, with the picture unchanged at 8; the depth pass does not move by more than its run-to-run noise (0.03 ms), since the blades dropped are hidden ones the depth test already rejected, and the cull costs 0.015 ms more. `r_grass_quality` (low, medium, high; default medium) owns the grass of the forward level frame (`GrassThinStart`, `GrassFarStart` of `GrassThin.h`, handed to the grass cull): every blade is kept to 24, 48 or 96 m from the eye, past which the kept share falls with the square of the distance, and a blade keeps its whole mesh to 20, 40 or 80 m. A slot's blades are stored by the distance the setting keeps them at, so a change shows in full after the level is loaded again. On `streaming_check` at 1080p (2026-11-18) the GPU frame takes 3.03 ms at low, 3.58 ms at medium and 4.29 ms at high.

`r_grass_distance` (50 to 500 m, default 300; the presets set 100 / 150 / 200 / 300 / 500 / 500) sizes the slot cache around the eye (`SetDetailRadius`, used by the command, the detail layers panel and the level load, so a config that never runs the command still gets 300 m) and makes it again when changed. The grass ends at the nearest of that distance, the weather's fog distance and the far plane, both already capped by `r_distance_quality`: a bound of each frame's work, not of the cache, so the fog moving with the weather builds nothing again, and the edge it moves lies where the fog hides everything. Slots that come into the cache are unpacked on the details worker after the frame's lists (`UnpackPending`), only within that distance, nearest first and those in view before those twice as near out of it, for 1 ms of a task plus 20 us per pending slot in view, up to 8 ms; before, the game unpacked 7 slots a frame, which a walk at 5 m/s with a 300 m cache (about 750 new slots a second) outran, so patches 2 m square grew in late. Empty slots are taken at once. The slots' blade store (`GrassSlotStore`, 2^19 blades) is filled nearest slot first, and when a slot finds no place the slots the task did not show give theirs back; before, a slot kept its place out of view and a full store left visible slots ungrown. A blade's wind variant is a hash of its place (it was the global random, so a slot unpacked again changed), and a game slot keeps its swaying variants at any distance: the old screen size switch (`r_ssaDISCARD`) moved a whole slot to the still variant at once, about 200 m out. The tile budget of the grass cull counts a blade into the four tiles around it by its place and reads them the same way, and a blade near its tile's cut shrinks before it goes: with one tile a blade, a blade crossing a tile border or a count moving by a few blades turned blades on and off in 32-pixel blocks. Memory at 1000 m: the cache is 1001^2 slot records of 416 bytes (about 420 MB) whatever the fog; the blades are unpacked only within the reach. No frame time is measured here.

`r_scene_models` (0 or 1; default 1): in the forward level frame, world models (characters, items, weapons lying in the world) are drawn by the scene's own pass with the level, one indirect draw per model shader, in runs of 64 triangles of each mesh. 0 leaves them to the draw graph, one draw a mesh, as before. Taken when its shader is the plain or the environment-mapped model shader, or a level shader (default, vertex-lit, alpha-tested, environment-mapped lightmapped, tree; not terrain or self-lit) worn by a moving object: that one is compiled once more with the model shaders of its features (`ShaderVariant::ClusterModel`: detail, bump, mirrored cube) and lit as a model, by the cube its light tracker measures where it stands, not by its own lightmap. A model shader first seen after the level loaded joins the scene in the frame that first draws it, eight shaders a frame: its textures that were not loaded with the level get a place among 256 spare 512-pixel slices per texture format, or a bound register where they have none (their larger mips stream in once the streaming pools have grown for them, which takes ten seconds or more) and its material one of 1024 spare records. The environment cubes the level's and the models' materials mirror at load are one cube array of the scene (`BuildEnv`, `scene_env_build.cs.hlsl`: RGBA16F, faces of the largest source up to 256 texels, every mip taken from the source's matching mip), read in place of the pass's cube (`t30`) by the material's `SceneMaterial.env`; a model made later that mirrors a cube the array does not hold stays in the draw graph, and so does a cube that is not six square faces of a plain format. The opaque parts of the HUD (hands, weapon) are taken by the same rules: they are drawn with the HUD's own field of view and in front of the world, as before, and are never culled. Sorted (back-to-front) surfaces of moving objects and of the HUD get model records after the opaque ones, which no cull takes: the sorted lists draw them in their order (see the sorted list below). An XML blend's model shader joins as the model shaders do once its element says `cluster="1"` (see the XML blends below): the self-lit models and the screens of the devices into the opaque bins, the lenses, reticles, collimators, light planes and anomaly glass into the sorted lists, their `l_special` into distortion and the scope's mask. Emissive surfaces, the HUD's 3D UI and the XML blends without `cluster="1"` (the clock and compass arrows, which read `s_lmap` at a register of the buckets, and the exoskeleton screen) stay in the draw graph, under MSAA too. The log line `* [scene] forward cull:` (after `render_memory_stats`) ends with the opaque models taken (HUD parts included), the runs drawn and the mesh parts of the world left to the graph; the next line, `* [scene] mesh parts left in the graph by reason:`, says why each of those stayed (the moving surfaces of the sorted list counted with them; `no element for a moving object` is listed again by the vertex shader of its pass), and `* [scene] HUD parts taken` counts the HUD's parts taken and left, by the same reasons, its sorted ones included. On `render_perf` at 1080p (D3D12, 2026-11-21, 43 models taken, 34 left): frame 6.09 ms with it against 6.06 ms without, GPU 6.02 against 6.01: no gain and no loss at this count.

The frame's table of moving objects (2026-11-23, `GpuSceneObjects.cpp`, `GpuSceneModels.cpp`) replaces the main view's and the sun cascades' own collections of moving objects. `GpuScene::CollectObjects` (inside `render_main`, zone `FrameObjects`) applies the main view's tests as they were (the spatial query of its frustum, a sector its portals reached and a frustum they left there, the occlusion map, fog, the full-detail distance, the size on the screen at a field of view of 67 degrees), adds the lights and glows it meets, poses the skeletons once, and calls `renderable_Render` of each object and particle system once (the old loop called it once per portal frustum that held it), in the order of the query, with the main phase. Every call of the frame goes to the scene's collector (`SceneCollector`, `SceneCollector.cpp`; `GpuScene::BeginObjects` at the start of `render_main` resets the table and points it at the main view, `GpuScene::Objects` hands it to `renderable_Render` and `Render_Last`), not to a graph: `add_Dynamic` walks the visual as the graph did (a particle group's effects, a hierarchy's and a skeleton's children, a skeleton's LOD mesh by the main eye's `SsaLodA`, the dynamic size steps of `r__optimize_dynamic_geom`, the SSA discard, a visual once a collection) and hands each leaf (`MovingLeaf`: visual, object, world matrix, SSA, squared distance, object of the table, HUD) to `AddMainLeaf`, which selects the element as `rimp_select_sh_dynamic` did and writes it at once: an opaque part of the world or of the HUD becomes a record of the opaque bins, a sorted surface (`mapSorted`, `mapHUDSorted`) a record of the sorted lists plus its entry in that list of `GraphMain` with the graph's key (distance, then insertion), distortion and the scope's mask their entry (`mapDistort`, `mapHUDDistort`, `mapHUDScopeMask`) and a record when the surface has no sorted one; particle effects only their entry (the walks take them out, see the sorted list). What the scene cannot draw goes to the one leftover list with its reason (`MovingLefts`: the element's first pass of the second priority, no element, joining, emissive, not a mesh, heap page, hidden part, triangles, caps, or `r_scene_models 0` / a level the scene does not bin). Neither the graph's packet lists nor `r_dsgraph_insert_dynamic` are filled or run for the main view in the game. `GpuScene::BuildObjects` (after `render_main`, the HUD collected) joins the model shaders the calls met (`JoinModels`, eight a frame, the world's opaque ones first, then the HUD's, then the sorted lists'), takes the leftovers whose shader joined now, and hands the rest to `GraphMain` as the packets the graph made of them (`InsertPasses` into `DynamicPasses`, the HUD's into `mapHUD`; a sorted surface is already in its list), which draw them where they drew them before (`r_dsgraph_render_graph`, `r_dsgraph_render_hud`, `r_dsgraph_render_node` from the walks); it counts the list by reason (`mesh parts left in the graph by reason`, `HUD parts taken ..., left in the graph by reason`, now with `second priority` and `the scene takes no models`), puts the sorted lists' records after the opaque ones, and finishes what waits for every call to be done: a rigid part where its skeleton is posed (`SkinningPass::Snapshot`) and every record's light (its object's tracker, `update_smooth`). A taken world mesh leaves the graph only its passes of the second priority. `GpuScene::CollectCasters` (once a frame, `CRender::CollectShadowCasters`: in `RenderForwardLampShadows` when the frame has shadowed lamps, else at the start of `RenderFarSunCascade`; once the cascades' portals are traversed and the lamp parts hit, see the lamp shadows below) finds what each drawn cascade's collection would have called (its spatial query, made with the lamps' when the frame has shadowed lamps, a sector its portals reached, a frustum they left there) and what each lamp part would have called: an object the main view called is not called again, its parts' records are flagged as casters; any other object is called once, in the shadow phase, into the collector, whose view is the main view's eye (a skeleton's detail is chosen by the main eye; nothing is dropped for its size), after the main view's objects' parts are flagged: each leaf's shadow passes become records after the main view's that only the shadow views draw as the collector adds them (`AddShadowLeaf`), or go to the graphs of the cascades and lamp parts that cast it. The table holds at most 4096 records (`SCENE_MODELS`) and 4096 objects. A record carries its flags (`SCENE_OBJECT_MAIN`, `CASTER`, `TRANSLUCENT`, `SHADOW_ONLY`, `HUD`, `SORTED`, `DISTORT`), its object's sector and its object; the skinning pass runs once for the table and the table is written once a frame (`UploadModels`, at `CollectCasters` or before the main view's cull) and copied into the models' buffer by the first cull that reads it. Every drawn cascade culls its casters out of the whole table (`AddCaster` of `scene_cull.cs.hlsl`: the caster flag, the record's sector against the cascade's visited sectors, never a translucent one, not the object the view leaves out, then the planes); `PrepareView` only writes the view block. A part whose shadow pass the batch cannot draw as it is (see below), a part over the caps, and every part with `r_scene_models 0`, goes to the graphs of the cascades that would have collected its object (the pass of its shadow element, or every pass for an object of the main view, `InsertPasses`), which draw it themselves. Differences from the old collections: an object both views saw is posed and called once, with the main phase; a part the main view dropped for its size casts no shadow; a skeleton far from the eye casts its LOD mesh; a caster in a reached sector but outside every portal frustum of the cascade is drawn by the GPU test. The rain map casts statics alone.

Objects only the shadow views see (2026-11-23, `GpuSceneObjects.cpp`): nothing here changes an object the main view called. (1) Visible shadow: such an object is called, posed and given records only when its shadow can reach the main view (`ViewBase`, a plane-by-plane test of the hull of two spheres, which may keep a shadow out of view but never drops one in it). For the sun (`IsSunShadowSeen`) the hull is the capsule its sphere sweeps along the sun's direction for `L = min(FarEnd - D.C + R, (C.y + R - Floor) / -D.y)`, where `FarEnd = D.eye + 1.41421 * size of the widest cascade + 1 m` is where the cascades' depth ends and `Floor` is the lowest point of the level's sectors; for a lamp part (`IsLampShadowSeen`, in `HitLamp`) it is the cone from the lamp around the sphere out to `max(range, d)`: spheres of radius `R + d * s` at the object and `max(range, d) * s` at the far end, `s = R / sqrt(d^2 - R^2)`, the lamp inside the sphere keeping it. An object every view of it drops is not called at all; a lamp part that dropped one marks its tile partial (`GpuTileView::IsPartial`) and `TestTile` gives it hash 0, so the spot shadow cache bakes nothing for it while that lasts, and a part whose entry holds a bake drops nothing (a caster left out of a bake would change its hash and build the cache again; with these two rules a still object whose shadow leaves the screen never causes a rebuild). Decor objects are always cast. (2) Distant skeletons: a skeleton only the shadow views cast keeps its pose this frame when it was posed less than an interval ago, the interval growing from 0 at 30 m from the eye (`ShadowPoseNear`) to `UCalc_Interval` (100 ms) at 40 m (`ShadowPoseFar`): within 30 m it is posed every frame. Nothing poses a held skeleton in that frame (`PoseSkeletons` drops it, `SkinningPass::Snapshot` only stores it); its bones are as the last pose left them, so its records skin that pose again at its current world matrix (the skinning pass's output is made anew every frame, nothing of the frame before is kept). Its animation tracks still advance every frame (`OnCalculateBones` in `HoldPose`, on the primary thread), so the animations' time and their callbacks stay the game's; its update callback (foot IK, attachments placed by it, scripted actions) runs only when it is posed, as in the engine's own slow update (`CalculateBones(false)`, the torch's owner out of view). (3) Entering the view: a held skeleton the main view sees is posed in its collection as any other; `StoreVisualMatrix` starts its motion history anew (previous = current, bones and world) when the pose before was held, or when the skeleton was not stored in the frame before, so motion vectors do not smear. The line `* [scene] frame objects in the frame before` ends with the shadow-only objects called, those left out with their shadow off the screen, and the skeletons that kept their pose.

The lamp parts cast the moving objects out of the same table, in tiles (2026-11-23): the parts of one atlas page, up to 16 (`SCENE_VIEW_TILES`), are queued by `QueueTile` with their view, their place in the page, the sectors their portals reached and the object they leave out (a decor lamp: the one object it casts, a tile a decor object), and `DrawTiles` culls the whole table once for all of them (`AddTiles` of `scene_cull.cs.hlsl`, `SCENE_VIEW_TILED`: a thread a record, tested against each tile by the caster flag, the translucent flag with `r4_translucent_shadows`, the object, the tile's sectors and the tile's frustum but its near plane) and draws them with one pair of indirect draws (`USE_TILES` of `scene_depth`: the tile is in the visible entry from `SCENE_VISIBLE_TILE_SHIFT`, the run below it). No view takes records of its own any more: the models' buffer holds the table alone. A view's cull tests each record's sphere (the skeleton's for a part, read after the skeletons are posed) against the view's planes and adds its runs to the view's list of opaque clusters or, for a pass cut out by a texture of the alpha array, to its cut-out list; the view's two indirect draws of `scene_depth` take them with the level (the model branch of `scene_depth.vs.hlsl`, table `t17` of the draw layout; the tiles draw their cut-out ones with `USE_TILES` and `USE_AREF`). Taken, for the table: rigid, skinned and progressive meshes (a part at the detail the main view picked last; a rigid progressive mesh at its full detail in the table's shadow-only records and in a view whose graph draws full detail, at the detail the main view picked last elsewhere) whose shadow pass is one pass of the model shader's, or a level shader's on a rigid mesh, with the batch's cull mode, bias, slope bias, depth clip and depth test; with `r4_translucent_shadows` the objects only lamps see are called with the second priority too, and the records of that priority are flagged translucent: only the lamps cast them (depth alone, in any order). A cut-out pass is taken only when its texture is one of the level's alpha-tested textures (the alpha array is made at load from those, each slice weighed between the four nearest texels of its source (`scene_alpha_build`): filled with the nearest texel, a texture smaller than the 1024 slice stood in it as squares, and every depth pass that cuts by the array (shadows, and the main view's under MSAA) drew grass and leaves as blocks); it must not blend and must write depth. The rest goes to the graphs of the views that would have collected it (a lamp part: those whose portals reached its object), with the static level visuals the depth batch leaves out (see the static casters below). `r_scene_models 0` leaves every moving caster of every view to its graph, which is the A/B for a picture check. The line `* [scene] sun cascade casters taken` (after `render_memory_stats`, with the forward cull's) counts per cascade the table's casters of the objects its collection would have called, why the rest went to its graph (cascades summed), and the static and moving packets each cascade's graph drew itself; the lines `* [scene] lamp shadow views in the frame before`, `* [scene] rain map in the frame before` and `* [scene] spot shadow cache in the frame before` count what their graphs drew in the frame before, summed over the views of that kind, plus the packets of the lamps' translucent pass; the lamps' line also counts the table's records the lamp parts cast, once for each part whose portals reached the object, and why the rest went to the parts' graphs (the spot shadow cache's line casts none of its own: its parts are counted with the lamps'); `* [scene] frame objects in the frame before` and `* [scene] frame objects' parts` describe the table (see the profiler document). The advanced frame's cascades are gone with the advanced frame.

Static casters of the depth batch (2026-11-21, `GpuScene::Load`, `ClassifyCaster`), the same in both presets: a level visual casts through the batch when its shadow element is one pass, not sorted back to front at the first priority, whose pixel shader is `shadow_base` reading at most `s_base`, and whose vertex shader is `shadow_base` (a static), `shadow_lod` (a tree, placed by its own matrix) or `shadow_model` (a static: its vertices are in the world, and the model shader's world is the identity there). Its state must be the batch's, or draw as the batch's pipelines do (`IsStaticState`: cull mode, winding, bias, bias clamp, slope bias, depth clip, fill mode and depth test equal, no blend, depth written). A visual whose shadow pass is not the batch's own (the model shader, or a state of its own) casts through the batch; the main view takes it from the clusters too when its material has a draw of the scene (see above). Progressive meshes, with or without the simplified mesh, opaque or cut out, cast the clusters the main view's detail picks (every view takes its detail by the main eye, `SceneView.detail`; a cut-out mesh keeps its finest level), no longer the detail a shadow view's own distance picked. A cut-out texture the buckets have no place for (not a plain 2D BC1 / BC3 / BC7 file, not a power of two or over 4096, a full bucket; a missing file is read as the placeholder), of a level caster or of a model shader's single shadow pass, gets a slice of the alpha array made from its whole copy (`scene_alpha_build.cs.hlsl` with `USE_WHOLE`, at load), while the array stays within 2048 slices of about 1.33 MB each: its casters cast through the batch for the shadows alone, since the main view draws from the buckets. A pass of a later priority (priority 2 or 3, or an element sorted back to front at it: translucent casters, which only a lamp's translucent pass drew) casts through the batch too: its instance carries `SCENE_INSTANCE_TRANSLUCENT`, and the cull draws it only in a view with `SCENE_VIEW_TRANSLUCENT`, which the main view and the faces always are and a shadow view is where its graph collects the second priority (a lamp, or a spot cache entry, with `r4_translucent_shadows`; a cascade or the rain map never). The pass writes depth alone (the colour target of the lamp atlas, `rt_smap_surf`, was never read and is gone), so its order against the rest does not matter. The spot cache keeps them in a cached depth while `r4_translucent_shadows` is on and drops every entry when it changes. What still stays out: no shadow element, more than one shadow pass, an element sorted back to front at the first priority (the graph files it with the sorted surfaces, which only a lamp's translucent pass draws), other shaders (a tree with a level shader, coloured `USE_TRANSPARENT` shadows, extra textures), other states, a texture that got no slice, a mesh outside the level's mesh data, a mesh LOD group (the group itself is the leftover: a view walks it and picks its level by distance). The leftovers a view walks leave out those without a shadow element, which put nothing into a shadow view. A view that collects the first pass alone (`pmask` without forward and wallmarks: the sun cascades, the rain map, a lamp without `r4_translucent_shadows`) walks a list of the leftovers without those that put nothing there (no shadow element, or every pass and the sorting of a later priority), which only a lamp's translucent pass draws; the list is made again when the materials are compiled again for new settings.

Tree shadows in the simplified preset: the baked-light tree blender compiles the shadow pass without `USE_TREEWAVE`, so leaf shadows stood still while the leaves swayed. The batch's sway flag of a tree (`SCENE_CLUSTER_WAVE`) is now taken from the main material there: a tree whose main pass sways (`SCENE_MATERIAL_PLACED` without `SCENE_MATERIAL_STILL`, `tree_w*`) sways in the batch's shadows by the same code and the same wave, wind and weight as the main view, and its clusters' bounds grow by the sway reach in every view, the main view's too. The two outer cascades, drawn every other frame, show the sway a frame late. A tree drawn by a graph (a lamp's or a cascade's leftovers) still uses its shadow pass and stands still. The advanced preset is unchanged (its shadow pass sways with `USE_TREEWAVE`).

Shadow views recorded on the workers (D3D12 with `r_parallel_views 1`; the default 0 records them on the primary thread, which measures 0.04-0.10 ms faster; `ViewRecorder`, `SunCascadeFar.cpp`, `ForwardLampShadows.cpp`): the sun cascades and the pages of the lamp atlas of the forward level frame record their batches on the task pool's workers, each view into a command buffer of the NRI device's ring (`NriDevice::BeginCommands`); D3D11 records the same commands on the primary thread into the old backend's list, in the same order. Four views have lists of their own (counters, visible lists, chunks, cull groups) and descriptor sets of their own, made at load next to the shared ones and written again only where those are (a geometry page opened, a larger skinned output, a new depth pyramid): a cascade is the view of its index, a lamp page the view of its page; the tables, the frame's model table and the shared outputs are only read. A view holds a list of batches (a cull and its two indirect depth draws) recorded one after another through its lists. The frame does it in three steps (`GpuScene::PrepareView` / `KeepDepth` / `PrepareViews` / `RecordView` / `FinishViews`). First, on the primary thread, the batches are kept instead of recorded: for every cascade drawn this frame, after the graph's dynamic part (its static part was built on the task pool, see below) and the clear of its slice, its cull block (`PrepareView`); for every lamp page, after its clear, its restores and what its parts' graphs and grass draw, the cull block of each part drawn whole and of each batch of moving casters (`KeepDepth` makes `DrawDepth` and `DrawTiles` keep them for the page's view). Then once for all of them (`PrepareViews`, zone `SceneViewsPrepare`, which also ends the keeping): one skinning pass, the constant blocks streamed, each target wrapped once for the batches into it in a row and handed over writable, the geometry pages, the skinned output and the shared buffers handed over in the states the recording expects, the models copied to where the cull reads them. Then each view's batches are recorded (zones `SunCascadeRecord` / `LampPageRecord` on the workers, inside `SunCascadeRecordViews` / `LampRecordViews` on the primary; GPU annotation `SunCascade` / `LampPage` in a capture, the buffers have no GPU scope of the old backend), and `NriDevice::Chain` queues them behind the frame's command buffer (zone `ViewsChain`): that buffer is closed (the old backend ends its markers in it first), the views' buffers follow it in view order and another frame buffer takes what the primary thread records next; the frame's one submit (`NriDevice::EndFrame`) hands every buffer to the queue in that order, so the cascades and the lamp pages make no submit of their own and the GPU runs them between the work recorded before and after them, with no state decay between the lists. Last, on the primary again, each cascade draws what the batch does not into the same slice (its grass, then its graph's leftover packets, `SunCascadeDraw`); a lamp page drew those before its batches. All of it is depth only over a cleared or restored tile, so the order changes no texel. On D3D11 the same steps run with the recording inside `SceneDepth` (`SceneTiles` for a batch of moving casters) and `SceneCull` passes of the old list, a cull and its draws in two passes: on D3D11 a pass that writes a list and then reads it loses the output. What a lamp page keeps on the primary thread, in this order before its batches: the clear and the restores (one NRI pass a page, a scissored triangle each part, which must come before every other draw into a tile), each part's portal traversal and statics, its graph, grass (an NRI pass of the scene, shared grass sets and uploads) and translucent pass. Only the graph and translucent passes are old-backend draws, and only for what the batch cannot draw (none on the test saves); they stay correct where they are, as the chain keeps every list in order. The cache builds and bakes draw at once, before the pages, into the entries' own depths that the restores read. The rain map and the reflection cube still record on the primary thread. Measured with the cascades alone (2026-11-21, then behind a switch): recording three cascades cost about 0.05 ms serially and the extra submit more (frame 6.375 against 6.205 ms); with the lamp pages (2026-11-23) each of the two submits cost 0.085-0.107 ms on the primary thread, which the chain replaces (not measured yet).

`r_aref_occlusion` (0 to 4; default 0; experimental) lets what is cut out by its texture hide the grass. In modes 1 to 3 the pyramid never holds the grass itself: only the level's cut-out clusters hide it. 4: the grass hides the grass. It is culled before the frame's pyramid is built, against the one the frame before left, and that one is built again, loose, at the end of the depth pass with the whole depth, grass included; the pyramid the clusters are tested on is loose as in 3. The test is a frame late, so a blade a moving camera uncovers can be missing for one frame. On `grass_overdraw` at 1080p (D3D11, 2026-11-19, two runs each way in one launch): 4169-4274 blades drawn for 8425-8448, the depth pass 0.63-0.64 ms against 0.63-0.66 (no gain beyond its noise: the blades it hides were hidden ones the depth test rejected cheaply), 0.03 ms for the second pyramid. With it the forward level frame draws the depth of its second part (the cut-out clusters) before the grass is culled and builds the depth pyramid again, so the grass is culled against that depth too. 1: a texel of the pyramid hides where all of it was drawn, which a crown with holes almost never does; 2: the first three reductions take the second farthest of four (`HIZ_LOOSE_LEVELS`, `reduce` of `scene_hiz.cs.hlsl`), so a texel hides where most of it was drawn, and grass a hole would show may be dropped. On `aref_test` at 1080p (2026-11-18): 1 changes nothing (grass depth 0.259 to 0.254 ms) for 0.035 ms of pyramid; 2 takes the grass from 0.253 to 0.219 ms in the depth pass and from 0.216 to 0.172 ms in the colour pass, 0.04 ms a frame in all after the pyramid. 3: the frame keeps its order and its one pyramid, built loose: the cull already draws first what the frame before showed and tests everything against the pyramid of that depth, so with a loose pyramid cut-out clusters hide one another and the grass, and so does the edge of anything opaque. On `aref_test`: the depth pass 0.890 to 0.780 ms and the colour pass 0.816 to 0.777 ms, with no second pyramid.

The occlusion test of the scene's cull (`rect_occluded` of `scene_occlusion.hlsli`) reads the pyramid at the level where a cluster's rectangle is at most four texels wide, texel by texel (25 reads at most), not four texels of the level where it is two wide: those four took in up to three times what the rectangle covers, and one far pixel in them kept a cluster nothing shows. A mesh that is cut out by its texture is gathered into clusters by place (`cluster_spatial`), not by shared edges: its cards share none. `render_memory_stats` logs, a few frames later, how many clusters the forward cull draws, by kind and by the part of the frame (`forward cull: clusters drawn`). Standing against a wall on `aref_test` (2026-11-18) the cull drew 1576 cut-out clusters before the two changes and 1334 smaller ones after; with a test that hides everything it is asked about, 11. What is left is the size of a cluster's box beside the gaps it is seen through: a cluster a few metres behind a wall covers a wide rectangle, and a window anywhere in it keeps it.

Cloud shadows (on from `r_shadow_quality` low) dim the sun in both frames by the same mask: a texture laid across the sun's direction that slides with the wind (`SunCloudMask.h`, `sunmask` of `sun_shadow.hlsli`; the forward level frame samples it in `default_fetch` with a world-space matrix in `m_sunmask`). The mask is an encoded share, about 0.6 under a typical sky, which leaves a third of the sun in linear light. A frame without it has the sun three times stronger against the sky's light, and after auto exposure everything the sun does not reach looks black.

The scene's passes run on D3D11 through NRI as they do on D3D12, with three rules its D3D11 backend imposes (each found as a frame with no level in it, 2026-11-19): a buffer one dispatch writes and a later dispatch of the same command list reads is written through an output slot the later pass does not use (`scene_ranges.cs.hlsl` writes `u5` / `u6`: NRI unbinds the slot the buffer was written through, whatever has been put there since); no view is bound to two output slots of one dispatch (the Hi-Z sets, D3D11 unbinds the first of them); and a pass that draws binds no output buffer (the forward layout has none: NRI then binds outputs again from slot 0 and the render targets are gone). A streamer whose dynamic buffer feeds texture uploads is made with no buffer usage (`GpuScene`'s streamer): D3D11 uploads a texture by reading the buffer back on the CPU, and with any usage NRI makes the buffer write-only there, which made a streamed head take 28 to 130 ms of a frame. What the cull asks of the textures is read back from the oldest of four copies, one a frame: mapping the copy just written makes D3D11 wait for the GPU. The D3D11 device's main depth is typeless and readable (`R24G8_TYPELESS`), since the Hi-Z build reads it. The learned shader list keeps the preset a stage was made in (`.preset`), and the start screen makes only the stages of the running preset: a surface shader of one preset does not compile with the other's defines. Every detail object casts a shadow in the sun cascades that take details (`r_shadow_quality high`, `r2_sun_details_cascades`), a fir or a bush that stands still as the grass that sways; until 2026-11-19 a shadow view left out those that stand still. Measured on a field of them: the sun cascades went from 0.442 to 0.445 ms. Grass in the forward level frame takes the sun by the model's normal turned to the eye at the pixel, as the advanced frame does, plus what comes through the blade; only grass that sways lets the sun through, a fir or a bush that stands still does not; that facing plus what comes through is the share of the sun the blade takes, in the gamma sum and in the linear one alike; the sky reaches a blade no more than its side turned to the eye looks up (`min(hemi, n.y * 0.25 + 0.5)`, the foliage rule of `AmbientLight`); at high lighting quality the baked "sun reaches this blade" flag is not used, the cascades answer that.

A sun cascade of the forward level frame gives way to the next one towards its sides only. The eye stands near the far end of a cascade's depth range, so a fade along the depth handed about half of everything around the eye to the coarser cascade: shadows by the feet were part sharp, part blurred, and changed with `r2_sun_details_cascades`.

Heightfield sun shadows (`r4_heightfield_shadows 1`, default 0, experimental; the forward port of the advanced frame's sun pass of 2026-11-13). At `r_shadow_quality high` (`USE_SURFACE_DYNAMIC`) nothing baked stands past the cascades, so a level that has `textures\heightfields\<level>.ltx` and `.dds` takes the sun's shadow of its ground there from a heightfield. The `.ltx` is one `[heightfield]` section: `version = 1`, `width` and `height` (2 to 4096 texels), `bits` (8 or 16), `origin_x` and `origin_z` (the world's x and z of texel 0), `step_x` (above 0) and `step_z` (below 0; metres a texel), `height_min` and `height_range` (metres), `height_crc32` (the IEEE CRC-32 of the whole `.dds`); keys it does not know are skipped. The `.dds` is a DX10 2D R16_UNORM or R8_UNORM texture of one mip and nothing else (a 148-byte header): code 0 is a hole that casts nothing, codes 1 to the last span `height_min` to `height_min + height_range`. The pair is read the first frame the switch is on (`HeightfieldShadowMap::Load`); a level without it logs `no textures\heightfields\<level>.ltx` once, a broken pair `invalid or incomplete`, and both keep the cascades alone. Each frame after the cascades (`HeightfieldShadowMap::Render`, GPU scope `HeightfieldShadows`) three dispatches through `NriPass::Dispatch` (`common\heightfield_shadow.cs.hlsl`, `HEIGHTFIELD_PASS` 0 to 2) scan the grid along the sun: rows along its major axis, sheared by the minor one; the running maximum of the height plus the sun's drop a step in blocks of 64, then of the blocks of a row, then every texel takes its ceiling back out of the rows. The result (`$user$heightfield_shadow`, R32F, the ceiling negated) is `s_heightfield_shadow`, pixel t49: the passes that bind their base with `r1_tex` list it, and `GpuScene::DrawForward` binds it by hand for the XML blends. `surface_sun_shadow` (`common/surface_forward.hlsli`) hands it the share of a point the cascades leave to the baked sun: what the last cascade's fade at its sides leaves, times the heightfield's coverage (which fades over its 4 border texels). So every forward surface that takes the cascades takes it, opaque, cut out, blended, WBOIT, models, grass, wallmarks, water and the reflection cube's faces (the HUD's point stands inside the nearest cascade); LOD cards, particles and unlit surfaces do not. The receiver is the surface's point raised by `r4_heightfield_bias` (0.75 m, 0 to 5) and one code step. `heighfield_filtering` (spelled so, as the advanced frame named it): `default` compares four texels and weighs the answers, `3x3`, `5x5`, `7x7` are the PCF filters of `pcf_filter.hlsli`. The answer is linear light and joins the cascades' gamma value raised to 1/2.2. Constants: `HeightfieldWorldToGrid`, `HeightfieldDecode`, `HeightfieldGrid`, `HeightfieldParams` (x: on) and `HeightfieldScan`, the end of `CBLight`. `r_debug_view heightfield` shows it alone. Zaton's 2730 x 2730 grid takes 15 MB of heights and 92 MB of scan targets. Not carried over from the advanced frame's pass: the blue-noise dither of the overlap with the last cascade (the forward cascades fade without one), its own fade band (the overlap is the last cascade's fade at its sides), its depth guard, the screen-space sun contact shadows it took the minimum with, and the facing-and-hemi estimate it blended to at the heightfield's edge (the forward frame is lit there, as past the cascades without a heightfield).

A blade of grass is tested against the pyramid by its model's own sphere (the model's bounding sphere moved onto its up axis and turned with the blade), not by the sphere around its root, which is about twice the model; the frustum test keeps the root sphere. On the `aref_test` wall view this leaves 1354 blades of 54 thousand, against 1613.

`render_memory_stats` logs what the forward cull drew a few frames later: clusters per kind (seen the frame before + new) and the grass blades drawn of those given to the cull. The line `main view left by reason` gives the static packets the main view's graph held that frame and the level visuals the main view draws from the graph, by reason, with the vertex shaders of the most common materials without a cluster pass; the same count is in the load log (`level visuals the main view draws from the graph`). Use these counts, not the brightness of `r_debug_view overdraw_raster`, to judge occlusion: that view saturates after a few layers, and a thousand blades of layered cards already fill it.

The shadow filter of the forward level frame (`SURFACE_SHADOW_QUALITY`, `surface_cascade_filter` and `surface_lamp_shape` in `surface_forward.hlsli`) follows `r_shadow_quality` and `r_pcss`. `low`: a square of three gathers a side for the sun, a 3x3 filter for the lamps. `medium` and `high`: five gathers a side, 5x5 for the lamps. With `r_pcss on` the sun's filter looks for what stands between it and the surface first and spreads its five gathers a side by that distance (PCSS; one to four texels apart, `SURFACE_SUN_SPREAD`), the lamps keep 5x5. On `occlusion_problem` at 1080p the forward frame takes 2.37 ms of GPU time with the 3x3 filter and 2.90 ms with PCSS (2026-11-18). A change of the settings compiles the materials again; the level's forward draws take the new passes of their materials after every such recompile (`GpuScene::RefreshForward`), their pipelines being made again.

Colour of the forward level frame against the frame that shades a pixel once: at low shading quality the forward frame stores its scene colour gamma-encoded in `rt_Generic_0` and `combine_2` decodes it, so it is compared as forward^2.2 against the other; above low it stores linear light as the other does (`USE_LINEAR_SCENE`) and the two compare directly. The forward frame takes the dynamic renderer's light data (the torch's `color_r2` / `range_r2` keys), scales a lamp's colour by `light::get_LOD` and lays fog over linear colour (fog amount and fog colour decoded by 2.2) before it encodes; at low shading quality the classic encoded fog and `r_fog_luminance` stay.

The HUD under a lamp: the HUD is drawn in a space of its own, and a world light's falloff on a HUD pixel is measured from the eye while its direction is told by the HUD's own place. That is what the frame that shades a pixel once does (it rebuilds the falloff position from the HUD's squashed depth with the world's projection, which lies at the eye); measured from the HUD's own place the torch's near light was half as strong on the hands. The GGX highlight has no division by pi, as that frame's distribution has none.

Lamp shadows of the forward level frame (`r_lamp_shadows`, on in the simplified preset): `RenderForwardLampShadows` runs after the frame has collected its lights and before they are uploaded. Every shadowed spot, and the six parts of every shadowed point light, is sized by `optimize_smap_size` and packed largest first into the four pages of the lamp atlas. Then, before anything is drawn: each part's spot shadow cache entry is looked up and built (`PrepareSpotShadow`); one sphere query a lamp (its position and range) and the drawn cascades' queries run on the workers (`LampQueries`); each part that casts what moves has its portals traversed and the objects of its query in a sector they reached, inside a frustum they left there, but the one it ignores, are its hits (`GpuScene::HitLamp`; a decor lamp's hits are its decor objects); `CollectShadowCasters` makes the frame's table with the cascades' (an object no view called yet is called once, in the shadow phase; those only lamps see are posed first within 100 m); a cached part checks what moves against its depth and bakes it (`BakeSpotShadow`). Then a page at a time: the page is cleared, the cached parts' depths restored (`RestoreSpotShadows`: the page bound once, a scissored draw each), each part draws what it draws itself (`RenderLampShadow`: a part drawn whole traverses its portals again for the batch's cull, and collects the statics the batch leaves only when it leaves the part's view any, and draws the level as one batch per part, the level's cull being of one view; a cached part draws its statics from the entry when the cache is not used; both their graphs, grass and translucent pass, and the table's parts the batch cannot draw for that part's hits), and `DrawTiles` casts what moves of all the page's parts out of the table; the page's batches (the level of each part drawn whole, the moving casters) are kept and recorded after the last page, on the workers with D3D12 and `r_parallel_views 1` (see the shadow views recorded on the workers). The editors draw each part from their own submitted scene (`RenderEditorLampShadow`: no table, no batch, no cache). The pages stay as drawn for the whole frame, so a light that does not fit casts no shadow. The light records (`g_surface_lights`, t56) carry the shadows: `color.w` of a light is the index of its first shadow entry, zero for none; an entry is two records from index 64 on, the world-to-atlas matrix and then validity and page; a point light has six entries in cube face order. `surface_lamp_shadow` samples `s_smap` (t51) with the comparison sampler. A spot also projects its texture (`light::Projector`, kept by `set_texture`; `lights\lights_spot01` when it has none): every spot that is not a HUD light has a shadow entry, with or without a map, whose second record carries the scale from the atlas to the texture, the texture's slot plus one and the middle of its place in the atlas. Up to eight textures a frame are bound as `s_forward_projector0..7`; a projected spot's cone only cuts, the texture shapes it, as in the frame that shades a pixel once. Tree impostors opt out (`SURFACE_NO_LAMP_SHADOWS`): their second hemisphere texture uses t51. HUD lights cast no shadow map. Before the parts are drawn their spatial queries run at once on the workers (`LampQueries`), with those of the sun cascades drawn this frame, which `GpuScene::HitLamp` and `GpuScene::CollectCasters` read in place of asking the database again; portal traversal, `renderable_Render` of the dynamic objects, the cache's classification and every draw but the kept batches stay on the primary thread, one part after another through `GraphPass`. The moving casters the batch takes from a part (`QueueTile`) are skinned with those of the other parts right before the batch is drawn (`DrawTiles`, zone `SceneTilesSkinning`): one skinning pass a page, unless a full batch draws it earlier (a part with translucent casters no longer does: their pass writes depth alone). Skinned packets the batch leaves in a part's graph (a cut-out texture, another shader) are still skinned as that graph draws them. An entry whose build finds no let-go depth of its size asks for one and draws its static casters directly that frame; the depths asked for are made after the present (`CreateSpotShadowDepths`, profiler `SpotShadowCache::CreateDepths`) and taken by the entry's next build, so no frame makes a target in its middle.

The spot shadow cache is the only way a lamp part is cached; it is always on (2026-11-23: `r4_spot_shadow_cache` and its modes are removed, an old line of it in a config logs one `! Unknown command` at load and is not saved again; the old mode 2 rebuilt every entry every frame). A spot or a point light's part with a sector and no decor objects keeps an entry (up to 128) keyed by its view and projection: its static casters are classified once, drawn into a depth of the entry's own (4 bytes a texel: 0.56 MiB for a 384 tile, 1 MiB for 512, 4 MiB for 1024) and copied into its atlas tile each frame by a scissored fullscreen draw (`RestoreSpotShadowCache`); the moving casters, the statics it cannot keep, the grass and the translucent pass are drawn over it. What moves in a cached part's view is tested on the CPU against the table as its tile's cull tests it (`GpuScene::TestTile`): a hash of those records, and when the set stays the same for 30 frames and holds no skinned part, all of it is baked into the depth (one tile drawn into the entry's depth) and the part casts nothing from the table until the set changes; then it is drawn over the old depth once and the entry is built again. A part whose set is empty queues no tile. A cached lamp farther than 40 m from the eye draws no moving casters and calls none. Grass is drawn into the shadow of a cached lamp within 20 m of the eye every frame (an uncached lamp: 40 m plus its radius), with the wind, so it is never baked; it needs the high shadow tier (`R2FLAG_LIGHTS_DETAILS`). A part seen for the first time, one whose view or projection changed since the frame before (a torch, a swinging lamp, a lamp whose range changes), one with decor objects and one past the 128 entries are drawn as uncached lamps (one batch of the level, the page's tiles for what moves, no classification): an entry bakes only after its lamp has kept still for two frames, so a lamp that moves every frame never bakes. An entry loses its depth when its lamp moves, when a caster's visual or texture changes, when the texture filter or the tile size changes, and for the budget (least recently used first); every entry is dropped when `r4_translucent_shadows` is toggled, on a level load, a device reset and `r4_spot_shadow_cache_clear`, and an entry unused for 1000 frames is dropped too. The budget is twice the bytes of the atlas (four pages of `r2_smap_size` at the depth's texel size: 128 MiB at 2048, 288 at 3072, 512 at 4096), which bound the tiles one frame can draw, so no shadow map size makes the parts drawn earlier in a frame evict those drawn later; `r4_spot_shadow_cache_mb` is removed (an old line logs one `! Unknown command`). Up to six let-go depths are kept outside the budget. `r4_spot_shadow_cache_stats` logs the counters of the last frame; the line `* [scene] spot shadow cache in the frame before` ends with the lamp parts of the frame before: hit, baked, drawn directly by reason (first sight, moved since the frame before, no entry left, over the budget, waiting for a depth, changed or failed), not taken (decor objects, no sector), the entries and their memory. After a few seconds on a level with still lamps nearly every part is a hit and baked, moved and over the budget read 0.

The scene's grass draws are two-sided whatever the grass blender's pass says (`IsTwoSided` of `CreateForwardPipeline`), as the shadow views and the old draws are: with the blender's back-face culling the forward level frame dropped the planes of a plant that face away, while its shadow kept the whole plant.

Cluster levels are built by meshoptimizer's `clusterlod` with `simplify_permissive` off. The permissive mode collapses across seams and borders and expects vertex attributes to hold it back; with positions alone it reduced a flight of steps to a few loose triangles whose error let them be drawn from three metres (`r_lod_error 0` showed the whole mesh). `clusters.cache` carries a version and is rebuilt when it changes.

Grass in the sun cascades is culled by the caster planes of the scene's casters as well (`AddCasterPlanes`): the sides of the main view the light leaves by, and the plane behind the eye. A coarse shading rate was tried for the alpha-tested shadow draws and removed: shader runs fell 3.6 times, the pass time did not move.

The main view's grass is culled by the scene's grass cull and drawn by `GpuScene::DrawForward` with its clusters (`CDetailManager::hw_Render`). Both the main view and the shadow views (sun cascades, spot lights) cull runs of the slots' stored blades, thinned by the main view's eye; a shadow view leaves the blades that stand still, and the frame sends its runs once. The simplified preset's sun cascade also culls its casters by the main view: a cluster outside a side of the main frustum (or behind the eye) is dropped when the light carries its shadow further out of view, which `GpuScene::Cull` does by adding those planes to the cascade's own (`SCENE_VIEW_PLANES` is 12). A shadow view draws the far mesh of every blade that has one, and its entries are sized for the largest mesh it draws. The simplified preset's sun cascade draws the grass the same way (`RenderFarSunCascade`, with `r_shadow_quality high`); as that view comes before the main one in the frame, the first view of a frame prepares the grass (`CDetailManager::Prepare`: the lists the worker finished, the wind). The per-frame lists are filled only in the editor, where the instanced path (`hw_Render_dump`) still draws them; in the game a view the scene cannot draw has no grass.

`r_lambert_factor` (0.5 to 1, default 1, applied at once) is how the computed lights of the simplified preset fall off with the angle: the sun on vertex-lit and bumped surfaces and the local lights take `pow(saturate(N.L * f + 1 - f), 1 / f)` (`r1_lambert`), Lambert at 1 and half Lambert at 0.5. Baked light, grass and tree cards are not touched. The value rides in `L_sun_dir_w.w`.

`r_anti_aliasing msaa` with `r_msaa 2x|4x|8x` (default `r_anti_aliasing off`, applied with one shader recompile) makes the forward level frame multisampled. The multisampled colour and distortion targets are resolved into the plain ones through NRI (`ResolveNri` of `NriPass.cpp`, called by `CRender::EndForwardMsaa`): the old D3D11 backend copies, a copy of a multisampled surface is not defined there, and after `r_restart` it gave a black frame. `CRender::SyncForwardMsaa` runs before the frame's graphs are collected: it makes or drops the multisampled targets when the setting or the target set changed (a video restart makes a new target set without them), and compiles the materials again when the setting changed, as their alpha-tested passes depend on it. `CRender::BeginForwardMsaa` puts multisampled twins in the place of `rt_Generic_0`, `rt_Generic_1` and the window depth, so every pass of the frame up to and including the distortion objects draws into them unchanged; `EndForwardMsaa` puts the single-sampled ones back and resolves the two colours (`ForwardMsaaResolve`). The window depth gets the nearest sample of the multisampled depth by a fullscreen pass that writes depth (`depth_resolve_ms`, blend and `postfx` shader of that name), so what is drawn after the frame is hidden by the scene as without MSAA; the stencil is not carried over. (`ResolveSubresourceRegion` on the D24S8 depth was refused by the D3D12 device, cause not found.) Alpha-tested passes of the frame that write depth and have a reference above zero (`CBlender_Compile::r_Pass`, the two normal elements; opaque ones, and those that asked to blend by alpha, as tree foliage does: their blending is turned off) are split in two by `r_End`. The first pass (`USE_ALPHA_PREPASS`) writes depth only: its pixel stage outputs `SV_Coverage`, the samples of the pixel that pass the alpha test (`r1_alpha_coverage`: where the alpha is within its own `fwidth` of the reference the test is taken at every sample position, `GetRenderTargetSamplePosition`). The second (`USE_ALPHA_PREPASSED`) draws the colour with the depth test at equal, no depth write and no alpha test, so a pixel is shaded once and only where the surface is in front. The graph draws every first pass of the opaque group before any second pass (the pass index is the top of its sort key). The level stays in the scene's clusters under MSAA: its depth and colour passes, its second part, the grass and the models and HUD models taken from the graph are drawn from the bins into the multisampled targets (colour, thin, velocity, depth), with pipelines made for the depth target's sample count; a change of the count makes the main view's pipelines again (zone `ForwardSamples`), the faces of the cube around the eye keep theirs, single-sampled. The depth pass of a cut-out cluster takes the coverage too (`SURFACE_CLUSTER_COVERAGE`, defined for the main view's alpha-tested cluster passes under MSAA; without MSAA it is a plain `discard`), and without the occlusion pass the frame draws the bins' depth before their colour, as the graph does. No pixel shader runs per sample. The depth pyramid of the two-part cull is built from the multisampled depth itself: `scene_hiz` compiled with `USE_MSAA` takes the farthest sample under each texel, so an edge hides only what all of its samples hide (the nearest-sample resolve into the window depth is for the screen-space passes and for what is drawn after the frame). The depth pass's velocity is resolved through NRI as the colours are (`RenderForwardOcclusion`). The level's textures stay in the buckets alone: the `* [scene] whole copy taken back` lines are those of the frame without MSAA. Sorted and HUD sorted surfaces, water, wallmarks, far cards, emissive and distortion parts and the static meshes the scene has no cluster of still draw through the graph into the multisampled targets. NOT VERIFIED: written 2026-11-22, not built or run; the costs below are of the frame that drew the level through the graph under MSAA. Both passes take the same vertex shader, so their depth is the same. an XML pass gets both by `atoc="1"` (only with MSAA on), which the opaque tree card pass of `details_lod.xml` has (`imposter.hlsl` takes the card's alpha as coverage as it is, unsharpened); Not run on D3D11. Cost on `grass_performance` at 1080p, 4x: frame 1.57 -> 2.67 ms (grass pass 0.32 -> 0.75 ms, opaque 0.51 -> 0.87 ms, resolves 0.14 ms). Without the depth pass, with alpha to coverage in the colour pass, it was 3.09 ms.

Ambient occlusion in the simplified preset: with `r_occlusion_quality` `medium` or `high` (`ForwardOcclusion()`; the old SSAO pass has no forward version, so this frame runs GTAO for both, and `off` turns the occlusion off), every pass of the level's normal elements that writes depth and binds its base texture through `r1_tex` (the mark of a surface blender of this frame; UI and effect blenders make their passes with default arguments and set the state after, so the arguments alone do not tell) is split by `r_End` into its depth (`USE_DEPTH_PREPASS`; no pixel stage without an alpha test, `SV_Coverage` with one) and its colour at equal depth (`USE_DEPTH_PREPASSED`), not only the alpha-tested ones under MSAA. The frame draws the depth of the HUD, the opaque group and the grass first (`ScenePrepass`: `r_dsgraph_render_hud(0)`, `r_dsgraph_render_graph(0, false, 0)`, grass with `ForwardPassOnly`), then `CRender::RenderForwardOcclusion` copies that depth (under MSAA after resolving it into the window depth) and runs the same depth pyramid and GTAO passes as the advanced frame, then the colour passes are drawn. A colour pass dims by the occlusion (`s_occ`, bound with every `s_base`) what reaches the pixel from all around, not the sun and not the local lights (`default_fetch`). The normals the occlusion works with: built from the depth (`depth_normal` in `gtao_render.hlsl`, as in the advanced preset), which gives one flat normal a triangle and shows the facets of a smooth low-poly mesh such as the hands; at `r_shading_quality high` (`ForwardOcclusionNormals()`, `USE_SURFACE_OCCLUSION_NORMALS`) the depth pass writes the thin target (`rt_Thin`: the surface's view-space normal, mapped where it has a normal map, and its roughness; a multisampled twin under MSAA, resolved by taking the word of the nearest sample together with its depth, since words cannot be averaged), and the occlusion pass reads that. A surface with no normal map writes its vertex normal and a roughness of 1 without fetching its textures, which keeps the grass depth pass cheap. The depth pass's state gives the second target its own write mask (`SetRS(1, D3DRS_COLORWRITEENABLE, ...)`: a state set for one target is what turns independent blend state on; without it the first target's zero mask applies to all). With no normal to write and no alpha test a depth pass has no pixel stage. Contact shadows of the sun on the HUD (`r_shadow_quality` `medium` or higher, `ForwardHudShadows()`, only with the occlusion on and at `r_shading_quality high`: the trace needs the surface's own normal, and with normals built from the depth it finds the surface in its own way and stipples it): after the occlusion a fullscreen pass (`hud_contact_shadows`) traces the HUD's depth towards the sun for the HUD's pixels and writes 1 elsewhere, fading the shadow out towards the surface's terminator (`r_lambert_factor` lets the sun reach past it, and that light is left alone); the colour pass multiplies the sun's reach by it (`s_generic` slot, `$user$hud_contact`). The trace answers yes or no a pixel and this frame has no temporal pass to settle it, so the pass has a trace of its own (`hud_trace`), a depth march after Tomasz Stachowiak's raymarch.hlsl in the HUD's projection, as `SunContactOcclusion` is for the world: the ray is a line on the screen with the position and 1 / z linear along it and a step of a pixel at most; a step is in shadow only where the ray is behind both the depth as the texels have it and the depth smoothed between them, and not further behind the nearer one than a thickness of 25 cm (the weapon's; a thinner one let each rib of a handguard cast its own stripe, and 8 cm let the sun past the weapon onto the hand beside it). The ray's start is shifted by a pattern of 4x4 pixels instead of noise, and a second pass (`hud_contact_filter`, `rt_HudContactRaw` -> `rt_HudContact`) averages the 4x4 pixels around each HUD pixel that belong to the same surface by depth and normal, which holds the whole pattern; the colour pass reads it for HUD pixels only (depth below 0.02), takes the darkest of the pixel and its four neighbours, and keeps 0.35 of the sun in the shadow, which brings the shadowed-to-lit brightness of the hand to that of the advanced preset on the same view (0.53 against 0.52 on the `hud_contact_shadow` save): there the HUD keeps light through the skin and more sky light. A step behind an occluder adds 7 / steps to the shadow, so a seventh of the ray must be blocked for a full one. At a silhouette, where the depth around the pixel gives no surface plane, the shaded normal stands in for it. Materials of one pass (XML blends, tree cards, water) are drawn with the depth passes as they are and take no occlusion; an opaque XML blend the scene draws from its clusters is split as the others and takes it. The setting is applied with one shader recompile: `SyncForwardMsaa` compiles the materials again when it changes. Cost on `grass_performance` at 1080p, no MSAA: frame 1.71 -> 1.88 ms (depth 0.45, occlusion 0.13; opaque colour 0.52 -> 0.27, grass 0.32 -> 0.16).

The sky mirrored in bumped surfaces, at `r_shading_quality high` (`USE_SURFACE_IBL`, set with the bump permutation; `sky_s0` / `sky_s1` bound with the bump maps): the sky cube is read along the mirrored view at a mip chosen by the roughness, tinted by `L_sky_color`, weighed by a fit to the integrated GGX response (f0 from the PBR maps, 0.04 and one minus the gloss map for legacy materials), and by how far the sky reaches the point, taken as the ambient light of the point over the sky's. Where the sky does not reach, the old term stays (`surface.reflect`). Any change of the shading quality compiles the materials again.

Bump and highlights in the simplified preset: from `r_shading_quality medium` up, a surface whose texture has bump maps takes the bumped permutation of its shader: lightmapped and vertex-lit level surfaces, models with or without an environment map (characters, guns, the HUD), and terrain, whose four detail layers bring their own maps weighed by the mask. A layer whose mask weight is zero at a pixel is not read (`surface_layer_map` and the loops over the layers in `default_fetch`): both the depth pass, which needs the layers' normals for the thin target, and the colour pass skip its maps, with the same picture. The highlight follows the model the maps were made for: a texture marked as PBR is shaded with GGX from its metalness and roughness, loses the diffuse colour of its metal part, gives the light falling on it back in the metal's own colour as a stand-in for its surroundings (`surface_data.reflect`), and its occlusion map darkens ambient and baked light only, never the direct light of the sun or a lamp; a texture with the old gloss map gets the old power highlight weighed by its gloss and keeps its diffuse colour. Both take the highlight from the sun and from every local light (`surface_highlight`). Statics drawn by the tree blender (trees and other placed objects, such as wrecked vehicles) have bumped permutations too (`tree_w_bump`, `tree_s_bump` and their `_dt` forms). On a bumped surface the sky's light follows the mapped normal as well: what is not the sun's direct term is scaled by how much more or less the mapped normal looks up than the vertex normal, so relief shows where no sun or lamp reaches.

The HUD is drawn in a space of its own, with the eye at the origin. The first record of the light buffer carries the eye's place in the world, and `surface_world` adds it before the local lights and the sun cascade are asked about a HUD point. A HUD model has no light tracker: it takes the viewer's (`CRender::ViewerLightTrack`).

Three HUD features of the forward level frame copy the advanced frame's:

- 3D scope lenses. Before `HudSorted`, `CRender::CopyForwardLensScene` copies the frame into `rt_Accumulator` (`$user$accum`), as the deferred frame did: the lit level with the opaque HUD, the sorted world surfaces and particles and the rain (`EnvironmentLast` now runs before `HudSorted`, as in the advanced frame). When every sorted HUD surface is a material with `scope_postprocess` (the Gauss lens), the scope's PPE (`phase_pp(&Device.ScopePostprocess)`) writes the copy instead, and `CBVisor.ScopePostprocessActive` is written for the lens shaders. Under MSAA the frame is resolved first (`ResolveNri`) into the plain `rt_Generic_0` that the PPE reads. The six lens blends (`models_zoom`, `_gauss`, `_lerp`, `zoomnight`, `models_lense_scope`, `model_scope_lense`) have forward options with the advanced lens shaders and the scope mask (`l_special`) that keeps the lens sharp under depth of field; `model_scope_lense` keeps the `model_env_lq` glass (the advanced one is lit by the old forward lights). Not in the copy, unlike the advanced one: world distortion and volumetric light, which this frame adds after the HUD over the whole picture, lens included. The lens shaders, the scope PPE, the interface target's composite (`ui_postprocess`) and the UI particle shaders convert through `ToneToDisplay` / `DisplayToTone` and `SceneFromGamma` (`common_functions.hlsli`): the frame lights with `USE_LEGACY_LIGHT`, whose `tonemap` leaves the display encoding out, and above low shading quality its scene is linear. The scope PPE's exposure takes nothing away for the bloom there, as this frame's `combine_2` does not.
- HUD raindrops (`hud_raindrops.hlsli`, `hud_rain`). A bumped model shader (`model_def_hq_bump`, `model_env_hq_bump`; `USE_SURFACE_HUD_RAIN` from `R1Bump`, `s_hud_rain` bound with the bump maps) evaluates the drops on HUD pixels: the base and the maps are read through their refraction, the mapped normal takes their tilt, and the drops lower a PBR roughness by 0.45 of their coverage or raise an old gloss by half of it. The world's models skip it at the HUD depth test. The drops lie on the unskinned model (`LoadSource`) in a graph draw and on the skinned one in a scene draw, whose record has only the skinned vertices: there they slide on bones that move against the model. The tilt is turned into the world by the pixel's derivatives, the scene's draw has no model matrix. `hud_rain.z` is the hemi brightness the advanced frame scales the drop relief by. Models without a normal map have no drops here: their sun is per vertex.
- 3D icons (`CUI3dStatic`, the Spawn Manager's and the weapon customise window's `BeginUIImage`). From `r_shading_quality medium` up the `UI` element of `B_MODEL` and `B_MODEL_EbB` is the advanced one (`uber_ui`: `deffer_model` + `ui_base` with the studio cubes and `CBUIIcon`); `USE_UI_DYNAMIC_LIGHT` drops `USE_LEGACY_LIGHT` for that shader, so the icon is lit and encoded as in the advanced frame (linear light in `rt_ui_color`, `ui_postprocess` tone maps and encodes it). Low shading quality keeps the unlit classic icon. The cascade lookup leaves the surface along its normal by one to four texels and the depth test gives a texel and a half away. The lookup is filtered by `surface_cascade_filter`: 3x3 taps, each the nearest of four texels, weighed by the lookup's place between texels. Taking the nearest of four closes the one-texel cracks that level meshes without shared vertices leave in the cascade; without it a crack opens and closes as the cascade moves and its light flickers on the surface behind.

Depth of field, sun shafts and volumetric lamps of the forward level frame (`r_depth_of_field` and `r_volumetrics`, on in the simplified preset; `r_dof` and `r_volumetric_quality` apply on top) are the passes of the other frame, run over the forward frame's colour, depth and shadow maps. `ForwardVolumetrics` (`ForwardVolumetrics.cpp`) runs after the multisampled targets are resolved, so it reads the plain depth: it copies the depth, marches the sun's cascades (`accum_direct_volumetric`; cascades the frame did not draw are skipped in the shader, below high lighting quality the widest one is all there is) and every lamp whose shadow map was drawn this frame (`accum_volumetric_lv`; a point lamp is its six parts; the volume it draws, a sphere, cone or sphere part, is written as triangles to the dynamic stream by `draw_volume`) into `rt_Generic_2`, and adds the filtered result to the scene (`phase_combine_volumetric`). The shaders write through `SceneFromGamma`, so the light lands the same in the linear scene and in the gamma scene of low shading quality. The HUD is already in the frame: its depth is centimetres from the eye, where nothing scatters. The depth of field (`phase_new_dof`, `dof()` of `combine_2`) needs only the mask of a scope's lens, cleared every frame and drawn after the resolve. `Volumetrics` no longer loads fluid volumes by itself: that needs `Fluid` too. Measured at 1920x1080 on D3D11, simplified against advanced: sun shafts at high quality 0.42 ms against 0.44 ms, the light added equal to three digits; eighteen lamp parts 0.80 ms against 0.74 ms; depth of field 0.57 ms against 0.60 ms. The stock weather has sun shafts only around 07:00 of `default_clear` (`sun_shafts_intensity`), and its levels flag almost no lamp volumetric (`set_light_volumetric` from a script turns one on).

TAA of the forward level frame (`r_anti_aliasing taa`; `r_motion_vectors`, on in the simplified preset): the pass is the other frame's `phase_taa`, in the shared `phase_post`. Three things feed it. The jitter: every vertex shader of the frame is `vs_main` of `surface_forward_entry.hlsli`, which adds `m_taa_jitter` to the clip position at its end, after the position its velocity is taken from; the depth pass and the colour pass run the same function, so their depths stay equal. The velocity: the frame has no G-buffer to write it to (`DISABLE_MOTION_VECTORS` stays defined), so `ForwardVelocity` (`ForwardVelocity.cpp`, after the multisampled targets are resolved) finds the camera's motion from the finished depth with `pharse_velocity`; with reflections on the frame has run that pass already. In this frame `combine_velocity` covers the sky too, whose shader writes no velocity here. The reactive mask is cleared and nothing marks it yet. Motion blur (`r_motion_blur`) reads the same velocity. `r_motion_vectors_quality` chooses the velocity. `low`: every pixel takes the camera's motion, found from the finished depth (`pharse_velocity`). `high` (the default, and what DLSS is always given): what stands still still takes the camera's motion from the depth, but a model (`SurfaceVertex_Model`: characters, items, the HUD) and the grass have their own. The depth pass writes it beside the thin target (`USE_SURFACE_VELOCITY`): a model by its transform and bones of the frame before, a blade of grass by its sway of the frame before, and both name their pixel in the thin target (`THIN_ID_MOVING`), which the frame clears first; the pass over the depth leaves those pixels. `high` needs the depth pass with the thin target, so high shading quality and `r_occlusion_quality` medium or higher; without them it is `low`. Static geometry never writes a velocity of its own: a version that did ghosted everywhere but on the HUD. Trees take no sway of the frame before. A change of the setting, or of whether anything needs velocity, compiles the materials again. Not done: the history is not reset after a device reset or a preset switch. Measured at 1920x1080 on D3D11: `phase_taa` 0.27 ms against 0.50 ms in the advanced preset, the velocity pass 0.02 ms; consecutive frames of a still view differ by 0.1/255 on average. With the camera swinging (a script turns the actor every frame) the velocity target is a smooth field of the turn with no pixel left at zero, and the picture is as sharp as the advanced preset's (edge energy within 3% on three frames); the grass and the HUD show their own motion over that field. DLSS during the swing is sharp in both presets. A camera that moves sideways and moving characters have not been checked.

Upscalers (`r_upscaler`, `r_scale`, `r_upscaling`). The frame is drawn at the render size and the shared `phase_post` upscales it to the output before bloom, tone mapping and the UI. On D3D12 DLSS, FSR and XeSS go through NRI's upscaler interface (`UpscaleNri.cpp`): one `nri::Upscaler` of the chosen kind and quality, made when the video restarts (`dxRenderDeviceRender::GetRenderScale`), which also gives the render size. NRI's sizes are output / 1.5, 1.7, 2 and 3 for quality, balanced, performance and ultra performance; the device cuts the scale to whole pixels and rounds up to even ones, so the scale is lowered until the device's size is not above the upscaler's. On D3D11 there is FSR 3 alone, through the engine's own wrapper, whichever upscaler is asked for: this NRI version has no DLSS, XeSS or FSR on D3D11. `PhaseUpscaleNri` first runs `upscale_guides` (0.02 ms): the motion in pixels, from this frame to the one before, into `$user$upscale_motion` (R16G16F) and the depth into `$user$upscale_depth` (R32F), because NRI's FSR takes neither the frame's R16G16_SNORM velocity nor its D24 depth and NRI's XeSS ignores a motion scale. Then one NRI dispatch on the old backend's command list (`BeginNriCommands`), with the output moved to storage access and back; the colour is HDR, exposure is automatic, the reactive mask goes to DLSS and FSR. The history is reset on a camera cut. Changing the kind or quality destroys the upscaler after the device has finished the frames that named it (`GRHI->DevicePtr->Flush()`): without the wait the D3D12 device was removed. If an upscaler cannot be made or refuses the frame, the frame is scaled plainly until the video restarts. In the simplified preset the jitter is on only with TAA or an upscaler that the `Upscaling` feature allows (before, an `r_upscaler` left at an upscaler jittered the forward frame with nothing to resolve it). Measured at 1920x1080 output, quality (1280x720), details_render, whole frame: advanced on D3D12 native TAA 5.95 ms, DLSS 5.22 (4.97 with the engine's old wrapper), FSR 4.90, XeSS 5.46 (8.56 with the old wrapper); simplified on D3D12 native TAA 4.11, DLSS 3.43, FSR 3.30, XeSS 3.68; simplified on D3D11 FSR 3 3.21. The dispatch itself: DLSS 0.95 ms, FSR 0.59, XeSS 1.03. Not checked: with a moving camera, with MSAA, a preset switch with an upscaler alive, the editors (their build links no upscaler SDK any more), output sizes whose third is odd (2560x1440 at ultra performance draws 852x478 for an upscaler made for 853x480).

Water of the forward level frame (`r1_water`, `shaders/surface/water_baked.hlsl`) is shaded as the advanced frame's water (`effects/water.hlsl`): in gamma space, decoded to the scene's linear light once at the end. Its base, ripples and foam lie over the world (`xz * 0.3`), the ripples in that shader's tangent frame (tangent along -x); the sky is turned by `L_sky_color.w` and tinted by `L_sky_color`. With `p` the fifth power of how much the eye grazes the ripples, the mirror is `(0.2 + 0.2 p)` of what is mirrored and covers `0.45 + 0.45 p` of the scene (both toward the base colour by its alpha); the fog is laid by distance in gamma and thins the water by its square. What is mirrored: the sky; over it the cube around the eye; over that the screen. `r_water_reflections` (`USE_SSLR_ON_WATER`): `water_mirror` marches a ray off the ripples in view space against the depth (`$user$position`), twenty steps that grow, halves the crossing four times and takes what the frame before showed there (`s_frame_before`, `$user$generic`, through `m_VP_old`); its share falls with the ray's turn toward the eye, near the end of its reach and at the screen's edges. Where the frame keeps the cube (`r_reflection_quality high` with the reflections of the world, `HasReflectionCube`; the shader's `USE_OFFSCREEN_REFLECTIONS` with `USE_SURFACE_REFLECTIONS`) `water_cube` marches the ray through it as `FastViewReflections` does (`s_water_env`, `$user$env`; `s_water_env_dist`, `$user$env_temp`): a hit counts whole, a ray that meets nothing falls to the sky by the fog over the farthest distance it passed, since the cube's colour faces are never cleared. Without the cube nothing of it is read. `r_soft_water` (`USE_SOFT_WATER`): from the depth behind the pixel the shore fades in, deep water takes `0.1 * water_intensity` of its light, a band of foam follows the shore, the sun glitters on the ripples under the sun cascade and the bottom's caustics take the direct light on the water (sun and lamps), standing in for the advanced frame's accumulated light. Unlike the advanced frame: no history of the cube's march (`$user$water_probe_prev`), so its noise is left to TAA; the trace of the screen is the water's own, not `ScreenSpaceLocalReflections`. The depth is the copy made in `RenderForwardOcclusion` after the depth pass, before any water is drawn, under MSAA from the nearest sample. NOT VERIFIED: written 2026-11-22 for the `water_problem` save and not built or run; the old shader mirrored the sky at 0.55 to 0.8 and covered 0.75 to 1 of the scene, which made the pond a pale sky mirror and flipped it between the traced bank and the sky by view direction.

The sorted list of the forward level frame (2026-11-22, `GpuScene::DrawSorted`, `GpuSceneSorted.cpp`). A blended level visual whose material its own element sorts back to front (`bStrictB2F`, the weighed ones included) gets a `ClusterSorted` element at compile: its one `NormalHQ` pass compiled with `SURFACE_CLUSTER`, its blend, depth test, depth write and colour masks kept (and weighed under WBOIT, as `NormalHQ` is). At load such a visual keeps clusters of its mesh as built (no detail levels, never welded) that carry neither `SCENE_CLUSTER_MAIN` nor `SCENE_CLUSTER_DEPTH` unless it casts, so no cull and no opaque bin takes them; the textures of their materials are always asked for, as no cull asks for them. The graph still collects these visuals into `mapSorted` with the same key (distance, then insertion). `ForwardSorted` walks `mapSorted` back to front: neighbours the scene holds whose `ClusterSorted` pass is the same are one instanced draw (`DrawForward` part 32: an instance a cluster, the clusters written past the cards into the visible list from an upload buffer), and a packet the scene does not hold (XML blends without `cluster="1"`, materials with a map neither in the buckets nor bound) ends the run and is drawn by the graph in its place, so the order never breaks. A sorted surface of a moving object is a model record of the frame (made by the scene's collector as it is added, after the opaque records; its skinned vertices read after the skinning pass): its runs are entries of the same list, drawn with the material's `ClusterSorted` pass, or `ClusterModel` for a level shader on it. A walk takes the particle effects out of its list first (`TakeParticles`): each is recorded with every pass of its element as batches of the dynamic stream with what the walk binds (under the HUD's projection for the HUD's lists), and submitted where its key falls among the packets left, the scene's runs flushed before it; in a list drawn in insertion order, after the packets that were before it. The scene's collector puts them into the lists (`AddMainLeaf`); the graph draws none of the main view's. The HUD's sorted list (`mapHUDSorted`, after `CopyForwardLensScene`, which still decides from the whole list) is walked the same way: its records carry the HUD's projection, the graph's packets are drawn under the HUD's; under WBOIT a model blender's HUD element, which keeps its blend sorted, is its `ClusterModel`. `RenderForwardOit` takes the weighed packets out of `mapSorted` the same way: the graph's in any order, then the scene's, a draw per pass. The editors and a level without clusters draw everything through the graph. Distortion (`mapDistort`, then `mapHUDDistort`, into `rt_Generic_1` / `rt_DistortMs` before `EndForwardMsaa`) and the scope's mask (`mapHUDScopeMask`, into `rt_DofScopeMask` over the plain depth after it) are walked the same way by `GpuScene::DrawDistortion`, in the lists' insertion order (the distortion target blends `srcalpha, invsrcalpha`, which depends on the order): a packet is the XML blend's `l_special` element, drawn by the scene with its `ClusterModel` element (the `l_special` pass compiled as a cluster pass), a static's from its clusters, a moving object's or the HUD's from the record its sorted surface has (one with none gets one for it); the scope's mask only for the HUD's records. Particles there are taken out and submitted in their place as in the sorted list; what the scene does not hold is the graph's in its place. A segment whose pass binds a texture by register (an XML blend's model surface binds its whole list, `s_base` included; see below) that an earlier segment of the same draw binds otherwise starts a draw of its own. Log lines: at load `* [scene] sorted level visuals: N of M drawn by the sorted list from C clusters; left to the graph: ...`; with the forward cull's stats `* [scene] sorted list in the frame before: the scene drew N surfaces in D draws and P particle effects; left in the graph by reason: ...`, the same for `weighed surfaces (WBOIT)`, `HUD's sorted list`, `distortion`, `HUD's distortion` and `scope's mask`, and `* [scene] graph passes in the frame before: second priority static S, moving M`.

XML blends drawn from the scene's clusters (2026-11-22, `CXMLBlend::Compile`). A blend's `normal` element with `cluster="1"` and one pass gets, outside the editors, its pass compiled once more with `SURFACE_CLUSTER`, its state as the XML writes it: a `ClusterSorted` element when the element is strict (the sorted or the weighed list above), else a `Cluster` element; an `l_special` element with it (distortion, the scope's mask) gets its pass compiled so as the `ClusterModel` element, its state kept (2026-11-23). An opaque one that leaves depth, blend and colour mask alone (no `blend*`, `zfunc`, `color`, `aref`, `zwrite="0"`) is split into its depth and its colour at equal depth under the occlusion, as the blenders' level surfaces are; a pass whose pixel stage writes its colour alone says `prepass="0"` (the depth pass writes the occlusion's normals too). A model's blend is one whose vertex shader is a model's (the name has `model`: `r1_model_*`, `deffer_model`, `models_*`, `model_*`); its cluster pass reads the model's record (`SURFACE_CLUSTER_MODEL` for the Surface shaders; `common\scene_model.hlsli` for the others: `deffer_model` of `surface\deferred.hlsl`, `model_distort*` of `surface\faded.hlsl`, `model_scope_lense`, `models_reflex_lens`, `models_reflex_reticle`, `scope_lense_mask`, which take what the old draw had in `m_W`, `m_WV`, `m_WVP` and `L_dynamic_props` from the record, the HUD's view as the world's turn). The shipped blends with it: `effects_water`, `effects_waterryaska`, `effects_waterstuden` (their `l_special` too), `effects_wallmarkblend`, `effects_wallmarkmult`, `effects_wallmarkset`, `effects_lightplanes`, `selflight`, `selflight2`; the models' `models_selflight`, `_selflightl`, `_selflightw`, `_selflight_det`, `models_compscreen`, `models_digiclock_*_screen` (opaque; the screens read their base from the buckets), `models_lense_scope`, `models_zoom`, `_zoom_lerp`, `_zoomnight`, `_zoom_gauss`, `model_scope_lense` (with their scope masks), `models_reflex_lens`, `models_lfo_glass_lens_weapons`, `models_reflex_reticle`, `models_lfo_light_dot_weapons`, `models_collimator`, `models_collimsight`, `models_laserray`, `models_lightplanes`, `models_pda`, `models_condition`, `models_energy_gauss`, `models_exohealth`, `models_xdistort*`, `models_pautina`, `models_xanomaly`, `models_xmonolith`, `models_xwindows` (with their distortion). A bin whose colour pass blends (`SceneBin::Order` 4) is drawn in `ForwardOpaqueLate` by `DrawForward` part 64 (both parts of the list in one call), after the cards' fade and before `r_dsgraph_render_graph(1)`, which drew those statics there by pass, never by distance; its pass textures are bound in that call alone. Their meshes keep the clusters they were built with (no detail levels, never welded: their vertex stages move or light their own vertices). A pass's list is bound by register where its pixel stage reads it outside the buckets, the tables and the records (`IsForwardRead`): a draw of the scene that binds another texture at a register an earlier draw of the frame bound takes a descriptor set of its own (pools of 16 sets, made as a frame needs them), as D3D12 reads a set when the frame runs. Of an XML blend's own surface (vertex shader `r1_*`, and every XML blend's model surface, `ShaderElement::flags.OwnMaps`) only `s_base` is a material map from the buckets, and the rest of its list is bound as it is (a sequence at its frame below the buckets): water's ripples `s_water_nmap` t1, foam `s_water_foam` t2, caustics `s_water_caustic` t3 (a sequence; the stock ripples need not be block-compressed), its sky is `sky_s0` / `sky_s1` (`$user$sky0` / `$user$sky1`, as the bumped surfaces bind it), and what it reads of the frame has registers of its own: `s_position` t37, `s_frame_before` t40, `s_water_env` t46, `s_water_env_dist` t47. The sun cascade and the lamp shadow maps a wallmark or water reads are those the opaque draws bound earlier in the frame. At load `* [scene] forward pipelines: ...; N used ones blend, drawn after the sky`; the forward cull's stats count `blended` clusters; `level visuals the main view draws from the graph` counts a sorted visual by the sorted list's verdict (one it draws is not counted), and it and the sorted line name the first three textures that kept materials out of the buckets (map, texture, why).

Every preset renders into the same targets and ends its frame in the same post chain (`CRenderTarget::phase_post`: anti-aliasing, scaling, bloom and exposure, tonemap, sharpening, visor effects, the game's post-process). So the player's picture options (`r_saturation`, `r_vignette`, `r_sharpening`, the gas mask and rain-drop effects, night vision) apply in static lighting too.

The window's swap chain is NRI's on Windows, for both APIs (`NriDevice::CreateSwapChain`): flip model, three textures, `R8G8B8A8_UNORM`, tearing allowed when `r_vsync` is off, waitable where the device can, and `r_latency` frames queued (`normal` is 2, `low` is 1). It always has the window's size in pixels (`GRHI->SwapChain`), not `r_resolution`. The old backends wrap its native back buffers and draw into them as before. A change of the window's size, of `r_vsync` or of `r_latency` makes the swap chain alone again at the next frame boundary, before `GRHI->BeginFrame` (`message_loop` compares `Device.WantedSwapChain()` with what was made and calls `IRenderDeviceRender::ResizeSwapChain`): the renderer lets go of its NRI pass wrappers, `CRHI::ResizeSwapChain` unbinds every render target, and the backend drains (`NriDevice::DrainAll`: what is recorded is submitted and the queue waited for until idle), drops its back buffer views, has NRI make the swap chain again and wraps the new back buffers. Nothing else is touched: no `seqDeviceReset` or `seqResolutionChanged`, no target, shader or interface is made again, the D3D12 frame slots stay (the GPU is idle; they are keyed by the frame value, not by the swap chain's texture). While the window is dragged the swap chain follows at most once a frame; minimised it keeps its size. `CRHI::BeginFrame` waits on it before the frame samples its input (`NriDevice::BeginFrame`, profiler `WaitForPresent`): DXGI's wait counts the swap chain's queued presents whatever the present id, so a present still on the submit thread is counted once it is made, and the wait needs only presents made a frame before. What reads the back buffer through `ReadRenderTargetPixels` still gets blue first. The log says what was made: `* Swap chain 1920x1080: 3 textures, 2 frames queued, tearing allowed, waitable`. Measured on `perf_test` (RTX 3070 Ti): the wait takes 0.001 to 0.002 ms on both APIs with `r_vsync` off and on and with one or three frames queued, and `Present` still blocks (D3D11 1.6 ms without vsync, 4.5 to 5.0 ms with it): on this driver the frame's wait stays in `Present`, and the queue depth changed no frame time. Under `-prof_gpu` the D3D12 backend no longer hands Optick the swap chain for its flip marker. Other platforms keep the D3D11 backend's own swap chain. 

D3D12 submission is `NriDevice`'s (B0, 2026-11-23). The frame has one command buffer at a time from the NRI device's ring (`NriDevice::FrameCommands`); the old backend records into its native list, which it borrows (`InternalDevice12::Commands`), and owns no list or allocator of its own. Before `NriDevice` closes the frame's buffer (chain, flush, drain, end of frame) it calls the backend's `CloseList` (given with `NriDevice::Borrow`), which records the queued upload copies and ends the open GPU markers; the next command takes the buffer that follows and begins the markers again. A plain game frame makes one queue submit: `InternalDevice12::Present` moves the back buffer to present and calls `NriDevice::EndFrame`, which closes the frame's buffer and hands every buffer of the frame in order (the buffers closed by a chain, the worker buffers behind them, the last frame buffer), the frame's signal and the present to the submit thread, which makes the one `QueueSubmit` and the `QueuePresent` (see below); the next frame begins there. Before B0 a frame made three: the backend's `Submit` at present, the empty submit of `NriDevice::Present` and the frame pacer's own signal. The backend's per-frame data (upload chunks, descriptor tables, timestamp heaps; three slots) is keyed by the frame value modulo three, not by the swap chain's texture: `PrepareSlot` takes a slot again at the first list or upload of a frame, after waiting for the last frame that used it (frame N - 3, which the pacer's wait for N - q has normally finished), so `Present` waits for nothing. `Flush` records the queued copies and calls `NriDevice::DrainAll`, then frees everything the backend retired. NRI passes (`BeginNriCommands` / `EndNriCommands`) record into the frame's buffer itself, so every NRI pass stays in order with the old backend's draws, barriers and GPU markers between them; nothing is wrapped or submitted per pass. `BeginExternalPass` moves the surfaces a pass declares (read, colour target, depth target) in the old backend's state tracker, which is the one table of their states: a pass that moves an old surface itself (MSAA resolve, upscaler output) moves it back before it ends, and the buffers NRI owns keep their own states in the scene (`Move`). `EndExternalPass` only makes the old backend set its descriptor heaps, root signature and pipeline again at its next draw. On D3D11 a pass still runs when it ends: one NRI command buffer, kept for every pass, stores the commands and replays them on the immediate context at the pass's `QueueSubmit`, followed by the input layout and shader unbinds; it cannot stay open over several passes, as the old backend's calls between them reach the context at once, the scene destroys the texture wrappers and views a pass used as soon as it ends, and old buffers are updated between passes (grass bins). Command buffers recorded elsewhere (the shadow views recorded on the workers) go to `NriDevice::Chain`. `IRHIDevice::SubmitExternalCommands` with no buffers (ImGui's own windows) calls `NriDevice::Flush`: once the submit thread is idle, what is recorded goes to the queue at once without the frame's signal, so ImGui's own list and its window's present run after it, on the primary thread, before the frame is handed over; its buffers are taken again once the frame is done.

The submit thread (B1, 2026-11-23; `NriSubmitThread`, thread `X-Ray Submit Thread`, D3D12 only; `r_submit_thread`, default 0, menu "Submit thread" in the video options since 2026-11-23). With `r_submit_thread 0` the packet below is made the same way and executed in place on the primary thread at the end of `EndFrame`, as on D3D11, so the present is back on the primary thread. The value is read once a frame in `EndFrame` after `Idle`, when the thread holds no packet, so it switches at run time, in a level, with nothing else to do: the thread is made on every D3D12 device and only sleeps while the value is 0, and the back buffer is the one the swap chain gave (in place) or the guess below (on the thread) for that frame alone. `NriDevice::EndFrame` on the primary thread closes the frame's buffer, waits until the thread has finished the frame before (`Idle`, zone `SubmitWait`, normally done long before) and, under the device's lock, stamps the ring slots of the frame's buffers with the frame value, moves the buffers into the one packet (`NriPacket`: the buffers in order, the frame fence and its value, the swap chain, the present id; the packet's array and the chain's are swapped, nothing is allocated), takes the next frame value and wakes the thread. The thread makes the `QueueSubmit` with the frame's signal, the `QueuePresent` and `AcquireNextTexture` (zones `SubmitThread`, `QueueSubmit`, `QueuePresent`). The primary thread does not wait for the present: the next frame draws into the back buffer after this one (`NriDevice::AcquireTexture`; a flip-model swap chain gives its textures in turn, one a present), and the queue takes that frame's lists after the present before it, when that buffer is the current one, as D3D12 requires. The thread compares the texture the swap chain gave with that guess; a different one is logged (`! NRI: the swap chain gave back buffer ...`) and taken for the frames after. So there is no copy and no output target of its own: the gamma pass, ImGui and the startup frame still draw into the back buffer, at no extra GPU cost. Everything else that reaches the queue waits until the thread is idle first: `Flush`, `DrainAll` (every flush site, synchronous readbacks such as the screenshot and the autotest's shot, resize, `vid_restart`, an `r_vsync` or `r_latency` change, shutdown) and `UploadData`; the renderer's own `Help.UploadData` calls only fill objects no frame uses yet and go straight to the queue, which D3D12 lets any thread use. The swap chain is made, resized and destroyed only on the primary thread with the thread idle. There is no exclusive fullscreen (NRI's swap chain blocks DXGI's window changes and Alt+Enter), so a flip-model present on the thread sends the window no message and never waits for the primary thread. A submit or present that fails sets a flag: `InternalDevice12::BeginFrame` reports it as a device removal before the next frame records, and `EndFrame` returns false. Frames in flight do not change: the frame on the thread is one of the q the pacer allows, a ring slot is stamped when it is handed over (never reset before that frame is done), and the backend's three slots wait for frame N - 3, which is submitted before frame N starts (`EndFrame` of N - 1 waited for N - 2). D3D11 always runs the same packet in place on the primary thread, as its queue replays on the immediate context the frame is recorded with; `r_submit_thread` does nothing there. Under Tracy the D3D12 backend waits for the thread before `TracyD3D12NewFrame`, whose signal goes to the queue; Optick's GPU zones are recorded on the primary thread and have no queue hook.

`NriDevice` owns the frame: its value, its command buffers, one fence (`NriDevice frame fence`) and, on D3D12, every submit. The frame value (`FrameFence`) is what the frame's submit signals, one value a frame; `CompletedFence` is what the GPU has reached and `Wait` waits for a value on the host. `Retire` keeps an NRI object (texture, buffer, view, descriptor pool, pipeline, layout, query pool, command buffer or allocator) with the value of the frame being recorded, not that of the next submit, so the stamp stays right when submission lags behind recording; the old D3D12 backend stamps its retired objects, descriptors, pooled streams and timestamp readbacks the same way. `NriDevice::BeginFrame` destroys what the GPU has passed, in one reused array. The command buffers (`BeginCommands`, `FrameCommands`) come from a ring of allocator and buffer pairs that grows only when every pair is still on the GPU; a pair is taken again once the frame that carried it is done. Stop points: `DrainAll` closes the frame's buffer, waits for the submit thread, submits every buffer without the frame's signal, waits until the queue is idle (`QueueWaitIdle`) and destroys everything retired whatever its stamp; the old backend's `Flush` (the renderer's flush sites, synchronous readbacks, a full descriptor table), a resize, `vid_restart` and shutdown (destroying the swap chain drains) go through it, on the primary thread only. `UploadData` is NRI's upload helper under the device's lock, after the submit thread (its copy is submitted and waited for); the renderer's own `Help.UploadData` calls still submit straight on the queue. On D3D11, where the old backend draws on the immediate context, `EndFrame` is a submit that only signals the frame's value, then the present; the D3D11 NRI passes still submit their replays themselves. Read-back tickets (`ReadbackBuffer`, `ReadbackTexture`, `ReadbackData`, `ReleaseReadback`) copy into pooled host read-back buffers and give the data only once the fence has passed the copy, so they never wait; on D3D11 the map waits for the GPU if the copy is still running (NRI maps with `D3D11_MAP_READ` and copies a texture's rows from its staging copy at the map), which happens only with the query fence of a device without `ID3D11Device5`, as that one reports its last signal at once. The editor occlusion grid retires its resources this way instead of flushing.

Frame pacing (`FramePacer`, in the renderer) waits on the NRI device's frame fence; it has no fence and no submit of its own. `message_loop` calls `Device.PaceFrame` right after `GRHI->BeginFrame` and before the SDL events, the last thing before the input is read: the frame then starts from input taken as late as the GPU allows, and the joins of the worker threads and the frame limiter are already behind it. It waits until frame N - q has finished on the GPU, N being the frame about to be recorded and q the frame queue (`r_latency`: `normal` 2, `low` 1, at most 3), on both APIs: the D3D12 backend no longer waits inside `Present`, so 3 gives three frames on D3D12 too (it was taken as 2). The frame on the submit thread counts among them: with q = 1 the wait for frame N - 1 also covers its submit, as the present on the primary thread did before; the same frames are in flight when a frame starts as before the thread. D3D11: the pacer is the only wait for the GPU apart from DXGI's frame latency. NRI's D3D11 fence without `ID3D11Device5` is a query that can only be waited for at its last signal: there the wait holds for the frame before, so every q acts as 1 (the pacer kept four fences of its own for it before B0). A change of `r_latency` or `r_vsync` makes the swap chain alone again at the next frame (`r_latency` its queue length too), with no device reset; the pacer reads it every frame. Profiler: `FramePacer` is the wait.

`r_fps_limit` (and the 60 of the startup screen and the main menu's limit) sleeps until 1.5 ms before the frame's deadline (`SDL_DelayNS`), then spins to it: the same limit for less CPU. Profiler: `FPS limiter`.

Latency: `render_memory_stats` logs `* Frame latency: 9.81 ms mean, 12.40 ms worst over 128 frames, from the input to the end on the GPU, frame queue 2`, over the last 128 frames: from the SDL input of a frame to the moment the pacer saw the frame fence pass that frame. The pacer looks at the fence when a frame starts and after each present, so the number is exact when it waits (GPU-bound) and up to a frame high when the GPU finished earlier. NRI's query fence of old D3D11 runtimes cannot be looked at without waiting: there the line reads low. In an autotest run put `render_memory_stats` in `-autotest_cmd` after the frames to measure.

A device reset (`r_restart`): `reset_begin` destroys the NRI wrapper cache (`NriPasses`) and the targets; whatever reads a surface through it makes it again (`NriPass::WrapRead`), since a level frame may draw nothing through `NriPass::Draw`, which was the only place that did, and the forward frame then had no shadow maps, light tiles or sky (a dark frame on D3D11, a hung device on D3D12). The grass manager and its models outlive the reset: the scene's tables name their geometry, clusters and shaders, and a bin holds its material's shader by reference. `CRender::Render` is the one frame function. Its start is shared (jitter, screen constants, the item UI target). It continues in `RenderForwardLevel`: occlusion and collection, the opaque pass (HUD, level, grass, LODs, sky), wallmarks, the second graph pass, sorted geometry, flares, distortion, then `phase_post`; the sun cascade of dynamic objects and sprite glows run only behind `SunShadows` and `SpriteGlows`. The depth pyramid is made again for a new size, and every frame's set of the grass cull then takes the new one (`CreateHiZ`): a set that kept the destroyed view crashed NRI's D3D11 backend at the second or third `r_restart`.

The deferred frame is removed (2026-11-22). Gone with it: the deferred tail of `CRender::Render` and `render_forward`, `phase_combine`, `phase_wallmarks`, `PhaseScreenSpace` (`thin_pack`, the old SSAO pass, the world's sun contact shadows), light accumulation (`accum_point`, `accum_spot`, `accum_reflected`, `accum_volumetric`, `accum_direct_cascade`, `phase_accumulator`, `enable_scissor`), the lamps' occlusion queries (`R_occlusion`, `light_vis.cpp`, `phase_occq`), `render_lights`, `render_sun_cascades` and the sun shadow cache, the deferred rain pass (`render_rain`, `draw_rain`, `CBlender_rain`), the G-buffer puddles (`phase_puddles`, `PhaseRainPuddles`), deferred decals (`ApplyDecals`), the frame's own light buffers (`BuildForwardLights`, `GpuScene::AddLight`), the visibility buffer (`GpuScene::DrawVisibility`, `Shade`, `DumpTargets`, `.geomx`), the 3D fluid volumes, the deferred blenders (`CBlender_deffer_*`, `CBlender_accum_*`, `CBlender_light_occq`, `CBlender_SSAO`, `uber_forward*`), the G-buffer targets `rt_Color`, `rt_Normal`, `rt_Surface` and `SunContactTarget`, and the in-game preset switch (`SwitchPreset`). Removed after it as dead: the feature switches `r_reflections`, `r_wetness`, `r_tessellation`, `r_fluid`, `r_emissive`, `r_deferred_decals` (`r_sun_shadows` stays: the renderer sets it), the emissive and deferred-wallmark lists of the graph, the hemi lights of `build.lights`, the fast-path (`OGF_FASTPATH`) shadow meshes and the second set of level geometry buffers, tessellation (`r_TessPass`, the LOD factor), and the console variables `r_dump_targets`, `r4_sun_shadow_cache`, `r2_zfill`, `r2_zfill_depth`, `r2_exp_splitscene`, `r2_mt`, `r2_allow_r1_lights`, `r2_sun`, `r2_sun_lumscale_sky`, `r2_dhemi_light_scale`, `r2_dhemi_light_flow`, `r3_volumetric_smoke`, `r3_fog_reload`, `r4_enable_tessellation`, `r4_tess_max`, `r4_tess_dist_near` and `r4_tess_dist_far`.

## View rectangle

Three sizes: the window W, always the swap chain's size (the window's client area in pixels); the allocated output A, `r_resolution` at the last `r_restart` (`Device.OutputWidth/Height`), the size every screen target is made with and the largest frame; the view V <= A (`Device.TargetWidth/Height`, render size `HalfTarget` against `AllocHalfTarget`), the part of the targets the frame draws into, from their top-left corner. `Device.FitView` sets V at the start of every frame: the Game window when it is open (tool builds), else the window itself, pixel for pixel when it fits into A, or shrunk with the window's proportions until it does; `r_view_scale` (0.25..1, tool builds only) shrinks it further for testing. A windowed window sized by its frame becomes the video mode: the swap chain follows at the next frame and the view is fitted meanwhile; once the size has rested for about 20 frames with the mouse released, `r_resolution` takes the window's size (made even) and a reset makes the targets and the interface for it, as `r_restart` does. So a windowed frame is drawn pixel for pixel at any window size after a short stop. A borderless window never changes `r_resolution`: there a smaller mode is the render size, stretched over the display. A reset raises `seqResolutionChanged` only when the allocated output size or the render scale changed. The render scale is chosen for the video mode, so the render targets hold the largest render size; the view renders at the same share of it (`HalfTarget`). The upscalers' output is the view: DLSS, FSR and XeSS through NRI (D3D12) are made again for a new view size once it has rested as the interface does (below), and until then the frame is scaled plainly; the first upscaled frame after that, after a reset and after any frame drawn without the upscaler starts its history anew. The view's render size is its share of the targets' rounded up to even pixels, which can be a pixel or two larger than what the upscaler made for that view takes: the upscaler then reads one or two lines fewer. FSR 3 on D3D11 is made for the video mode and takes the view's sizes as they are; it starts its history anew whenever the view changes size. Plain TAA reads its history inside the view of the frame before, so it follows any change at once.

The gamma pass puts V into the window (`CRenderTarget::PhaseGammaApply`, `Device.PresentRect`): pixel for pixel when the window holds it (a window one pixel wider or taller than the even view leaves that line black), else stretched over the window with V's proportions, centred, filtered (`smp_rtlinear`); where the view does not cover the window the swap chain is cleared black first, every frame. W == V is a plain copy with no clear and no filtering (`gamma_apply.hlsl` loads the pixel when `c_scale.zw` is 1). In tool builds the gamma pass writes the debug scene target and ImGui shows it at the same rectangle over a black background (`DrawMainViewport`), or in the Game window. The game's cursor and `IR_GetMousePosIndependent` map window pixels through the same rectangle (`UICursor::UpdateCursorPosition`; in relative mode a mouse move is scaled by the picture's size on screen, the Game window's included). A cursor the game centres (menus, dialogs: `CUICursor::Center`) puts the mouse in the middle of that rectangle, or of the Game window's picture, and leaves the mouse alone while the tools take the input. The Game window can be docked anywhere or taken out into an OS window of its own (an ImGui viewport, `CImGuiManager::GetGameViewport` / `GetGameWindow`, recorded by `DrawGameWindow` every frame); the warp, the mouse grab, relative mode, the game's keyboard focus and the tools' overlays over the picture then target that window, and its viewport is drawn and presented every frame (sync interval 0) while the other tool viewports keep their 60 Hz cap. Fonts and interface layouts follow V once it has kept its size for about 20 frames with the mouse released (`on_idle`, `g_UiView`); a reset fits the view before it builds them, so after an `r_restart` to a mode the window does not match they are made once, for V.

`r_resolution` is taken only by `r_restart`: changed alone it changes nothing (the frame keeps drawing into the targets made for the old mode, whose size every per-frame lookup reads from `Device.OutputWidth/Height`). `r_restart` makes the targets at the new mode; a windowed window of another size takes the new mode as its size and is centred (not smaller than 640x480; a mode the display cannot hold ends as the size the window got).

`r_fullscreen` (registered in every configuration, applied by `r_restart`; default `off`, `borderless` is the only other token) is borderless: the window covers its display at the desktop size and the display mode is never changed; there is no exclusive fullscreen. The swap chain then has the display's size and the view is A fitted into it (an `r_resolution` of the desktop's proportions is drawn whole and stretched over the display). Windowed, the window is resizable by its frame; the swap chain follows the next frame and the view moves with it. Off Windows the D3D11 backend still creates its swap chain with `Windowed = !r_fullscreen`, which DXVK / DXMT may turn into a display mode change. A window event (size, move, display, display scale, minimise, restore, focus, fullscreen) refreshes `Device.Window` (pixel size, display, display scale, minimised, focused, occluded, fullscreen) from SDL.

Autotest: the hidden window is windowed and sized to `r_resolution` by every `r_restart`, so W == A there; `r_view_scale` and `imgui_game_window 1` give V != A and V != W.

Editors: `FitView` does not run. Each viewport is the whole output: when its size or render quality changes `TUI::Redraw` remakes the targets with A = V = the viewport (`Device.OutputWidth/Height` = `TargetWidth/Height`) and `AllocHalfTarget` = `HalfTarget` = the scene size, so every share above is 1. A window resize (`ResizeBuffers` in editor mode) sets them to the window until the next viewport redraw.

Captures take the view, the game's picture without the tools: in tool builds the output target's corner of V's size (where the gamma pass leaves it before ImGui draws), in the others the swap chain's `PresentRect`. The `screenshot` command, save and level-map shots (`ScreenshotManager`), the multiplayer screenshot (`ScreenshotAsyncEnd`), the autotest `shot`, `-autotest_shot` and `-autotest_hash`, the Cinema frame export (`Autotest::CaptureFrame`) and the Cinema colour picks (`CImGuiManager::ReadGameViewColor`) all read it. Only the autotest `shot_window` reads the whole swap chain, tools and bars included. A readback buffer is sized for the surface it reads, the larger of A and W.

- `screen_res.xy` / `scaled_screen_res.xy` are the view in pixels, `.zw` a texel of the allocated target. `view_uv.xy` is the share of a screen target the view takes, `.zw` the last UV inside it; `view_uv_prev` is the same for the frame before.
- A fullscreen triangle gives the UV of a screen target (`texcoord *= view_uv.xy`). A pass that needs a share of the view (unprojection, motion, a map of its own such as the reflection map of `combine_vslr`, a fixed-size source) divides by `view_uv.xy`.
- A share of the view becomes a lookup with `ViewToTarget()`; what the frame before left (TAA history, SSLR history and its picture of the last frame, the water mirror, the picture behind rain drops, the depth-of-field blur history) is read with `ViewToTargetBefore()`, so the view may change size every frame.
- The two depth pyramids hold only the view: the scene occlusion pyramid is built from the view's part of the depth and always spans the whole view; the SSLR pyramid keeps the view a pixel a texel in its corner and its levels are reduced over the view's part only.
- Targets made for the screen outside the `CRenderTarget` constructor are created at the allocated size under `CRT::MarkViewSized` (the depth-of-field blur, the upscalers' motion and depth, the SSLR targets the forward frame asks for later); `CRT::ViewWidth()/ViewHeight()` give the part to draw. None of them is made again while the view changes size: the light tiles are sized from the allocated target and cover it whole.
